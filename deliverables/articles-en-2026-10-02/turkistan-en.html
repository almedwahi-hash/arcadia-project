Turkistan is the spiritual capital of Kazakhstan, and its Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi was the first Kazakh site to be inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The city lies in the south of the country, two hours from Shymkent and an hour and a half by air from Almaty, and since 2018 it has been completely transformed: a new airport, the Karavan Saray tourist complex with its canals, boats and flying theatre, and modern hotels. For Gulf families who want a religious and historical dimension to their Kazakhstan trip, Turkistan, Shymkent and the archaeological city of Otrar are an addition worth two to three days. This guide explains how to get there, the sights, the itinerary, the cost and visit etiquette.

Who was Khoja Ahmed Yasawi?

A Turkic poet, scholar and Sufi who lived in the twelfth century (he died in about 1166) in the city of Yasi, today's Turkistan. The Yasawi order, through which Islam spread among the Turkic peoples of Central Asia, is attributed to him, and his collection of poems, the Diwan-i Hikmet ("Book of Wisdom"), is among the oldest literary works in a Turkic language. At the end of the fourteenth century Timur (Tamerlane) built over his grave the vast mausoleum that stands today. It is one of the largest domed buildings in the Islamic world (its main dome is 18 metres in diameter, and the building rises to about 39 metres), and its exterior decoration was left unfinished, which makes it a rare example of Timurid building techniques.

The main sights in Turkistan

  • Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi: the central hall with its giant bronze cauldron (the Tay Kazan), traditionally said to be cast from an alloy of seven metals, the tomb chamber, the mosque, the library and the dervishes' rooms. Allow one to two hours for the visit.
  • The old bathhouse and the underground mosque (Hilvet): part of the complex; the place is associated with the retreat of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi in his final years.
  • Karavan Saray complex (Keruen Saray): a modern tourist town next to the mausoleum, with water canals and boats, a "flying theatre" offering a virtual flight over Kazakhstan, restaurants, shops, a museum and a luxury hotel. Excellent for children.
  • The city's history museums: artefacts from the Silk Road and the cities of the south.
  • The archaeological city of Otrar and the Arystan Bab Mausoleum: about an hour's drive away, the ruins of the city destroyed by the Mongols in the thirteenth century and the mausoleum of Arystan Bab, the teacher of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi. Local tradition is to visit it before Turkistan.

How do you get to Turkistan?

  • By air: Turkistan International Airport receives flights from Almaty and Astana (an hour and a half from Almaty).
  • Via Shymkent: there are more flights to Shymkent (Kazakhstan's third city), followed by about two hours by car or train.
  • By train from Almaty: an overnight train (allow 13 hours or more) for those who want to experience the steppe.

Suggested itinerary: two days in southern Kazakhstan

  1. Day 1: morning flight from Almaty, the Arystan Bab Mausoleum and Otrar before noon, arrival in Turkistan, the Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi in the afternoon, and the evening at the Karavan Saray complex with the flying theatre and dinner by the canal.
  2. Day 2: the underground mosque and the museums in the morning, transfer to Shymkent, Independence Park and the Shymkent bazaar, then an evening flight to Almaty or Astana.

Visit etiquette

  • The mausoleum is a place of worship and prayer: dress modestly, women cover their heads inside the halls, and shoes are removed in the prayer areas.
  • Photography rules differ between the courtyards and the interior; follow the signs and your guide.
  • Tickets are inexpensive; the museums and the flying theatre are charged separately.
  • Friday prayers are held in the city's mosques, a short distance from the complex.

Weather and the best time to visit

The south is hotter than Almaty: summer reaches 35–40°C, so the best months are April–May and September–October (20–28°C). Winter is milder than in the north of the country and also suitable for a visit. For planning by season, see the best time for family travel.

Food and accommodation

Local food in southern Kazakhstan is halal, and the southern plov, the shashlik and the Uyghur laghman are excellent. For accommodation there is a luxury hotel inside the Karavan Saray complex next to the mausoleum, 3- and 4-star hotels in the city, and a wider choice of hotels, including international brands, in Shymkent. Prices are $60–180 per night.

Approximate cost

For a family of four, two days with domestic flights from Almaty, a private car, an Arabic-speaking guide and a 4-star hotel: about 3,000–4,500 SAR, or $800–1,200. For the wider budget, see the cost of tourism in Kazakhstan.

Yes. The Karavan Saray complex, with its boats and flying theatre, was designed for families, and distances inside the complex are short.

One day for the mausoleum and the complex, and two days with Otrar and Shymkent.
