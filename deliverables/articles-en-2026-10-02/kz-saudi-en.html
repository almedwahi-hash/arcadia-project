Kazakhstan has in recent years become one of the fastest-growing summer destinations for Saudi families, and for practical reasons: no visa, flights from Saudi Arabia of about five hours, cool mountain weather in July, halal food everywhere, and prices well below those of Europe. This guide is written specifically for Saudi travellers: entry, flights and schedules, costs in Saudi riyals, the best times set against the Saudi holidays, and what children and older travellers need.

Visa-free entry for Saudi citizens

Saudi passport holders enter Kazakhstan visa-free for 30 days on each visit (up to a maximum of 90 days within 180 days), and a passport valid for at least 6 months is all you need. There are no fees and no advance registration; your passport is stamped at the airport and the hotel registers your stay automatically. Children need their own passports. For longer stays (study or work), see Kazakhstan visa.

Best time for Saudi travellers, by holiday

  • Summer holiday (late June to August): the ideal season; Almaty is 27–32 degrees by day and the mountains 15–20, and all the lakes and roads are open. This is the peak of Saudi demand, so book 6–8 weeks ahead.
  • Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha: excellent when they fall between May and October; the large mosques in Almaty and Astana hold Eid prayers and the occasion is officially celebrated (Eid al-Adha is a public holiday in Kazakhstan).
  • Mid-year break (January): snow and skiing at Shymbulak and Medeu; Almaty is -5 to 2 degrees, a good choice for those who want a real winter at a lower cost.
  • Spring break (March): the least suitable period (melting snow), unless you come for Nauryz on 21–23 March.

Halal food and prayer

Kazakhstan is a Muslim-majority country; the meat in Kazakh, Uzbek and Turkish restaurants is generally halal, and Arabic restaurants (Syrian, Lebanese and Yemeni) can be found in central Almaty around Dostyk Avenue. There are mosques in every district, the largest being the Central Mosque in Almaty and the Hazrat Sultan Mosque in Astana. In summer the days are long, with a very early Fajr (around 3 am in June).

Tips for Saudi travellers

  • Roaming on Saudi SIM cards is expensive; buy a Beeline or Tele2 SIM at the airport (20 GB for about 30 SAR, or $8).
  • A private car with a driver is far cheaper than in Saudi Arabia (300–400 SAR, or $80–105, for a full day) and saves you renting a car and driving in the mountains.
  • The weather in the mountains turns cold suddenly; carry jackets even in August.
  • The Saudi embassy in Astana is available for emergencies; save its number before you travel.
  • Older travellers are more comfortable in Almaty than in Astana (moderate altitude, short distances, good private hospitals).
