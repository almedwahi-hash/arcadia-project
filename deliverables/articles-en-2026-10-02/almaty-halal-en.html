The first question every Gulf family asks before travelling to Almaty is simple: "Is the food halal?" The short answer is yes, in most restaurants. Kazakhstan is a Muslim-majority country, and the meat served in Kazakh, Uzbek, Turkish and Uyghur restaurants is normally halal. The details still matter: some European and Asian restaurants do not use halal meat, and many restaurants serve alcohol alongside halal food. This guide explains how to recognise a halal restaurant in Almaty, the best types of restaurants for Arab and Muslim families, the dishes worth trying, prices in tenge, and where to pray while you are out shopping.

How do you know a restaurant in Almaty is halal?

Look for the word "Халал" (halal in Cyrillic) or "Halal" on the storefront, on the menu, or on the restaurant's page in 2GIS, the map app everyone uses in Kazakhstan. A practical rule:

  • Kazakh, Uzbek, Uyghur, Turkish and Arabic restaurants: usually halal, and many say so openly.
  • Georgian, Russian and European restaurants and international cafés: ask where the meat comes from. Some dishes contain pork, which menus normally list as "свинина".
  • International fast-food chains: most branches in Kazakhstan serve locally certified halal meat, but check for the halal sign in the branch itself.

The question every waiter understands is "Это халал?" (Is this halal?). English is usually spoken in the tourist restaurants in the city centre.

Best types of restaurants for Muslim families in Almaty

1. Kazakh and Uzbek restaurants (the authentic experience)

This is the first thing we recommend to every family. The best known is the Navat chain, with branches across the city, serving Uzbek and Kazakh cuisine in an oriental setting at reasonable prices, with picture menus that make ordering easy. Alasha is famous for its traditional atmosphere and evening folklore shows, and needs a reservation in the summer season. Dishes not to miss: beshbarmak (the national dish of meat and pasta sheets), plov (rice with meat and carrots), manty (steamed dumplings filled with meat), shashlik (grilled meat skewers) and baursak (fried dough served with tea).

2. Uyghur restaurants (the famous lagman)

The Uyghurs are Muslim, and their cuisine is a favourite with Gulf visitors because it is close to spicy Asian flavours. The signature dish is lagman: hand-pulled noodles with meat and vegetables, served as a soup or stir-fried. You will find lagman restaurants in almost every district. The Lanzhou chain of halal Chinese noodles is one of the best known and cheapest, and works very well for a quick meal with children.

3. Turkish and Arabic restaurants

As the number of Gulf tourists has grown, Turkish and Arabic restaurants have opened in recent years around Dostyk Avenue, the Golden Square district and the Mega Center area, serving grills, shawarma, kabsa, mandi, foul and hummus. Their names change from season to season, so ask your Arcadia guide on arrival for the Arabic restaurant nearest your hotel. Our team keeps track of which ones are actually open and updates the list every season.

4. Halal burgers and fast food

The Kazakh burger chain Bahandi is one of the most successful local chains and states that its meat is halal. It has branches in malls and on the main streets, and suits children and a quick lunch between tours.

5. Mall food courts

Dostyk Plaza, Mega Center Alma-Ata, Esentai Mall and Forum have large food courts with Kazakh, Uyghur and Turkish options, burgers and pizza, and prayer rooms inside the mall. This is the easiest solution for a large family in which everyone wants something different.

6. Cafés and sweets

Almaty is very much a café city. Try Kazakh-style tea with milk, specialty coffee in the cafés of pedestrian Panfilov Street, and chak-chak (fried dough with honey), which makes an excellent gift to take home.

Restaurants with mountain views

For special occasions, the restaurants on Kok Tobe hill (reached by cable car from the city centre) and those at Medeu and Shymbulak, at an altitude of 2,200 metres, serve Kazakh and international dishes with a view of the mountains. How much does food cost in Almaty?

Prices are approximate for 2026 and move with the exchange rate (one Saudi riyal is about 130–140 tenge):

  • Fast food or a bowl of lagman: 2,500–4,000 tenge per person (about 20–30 SAR, or $5–8).
  • Mid-range Kazakh or Uzbek restaurant: 5,000–8,000 tenge per person (about 35–60 SAR, or $9–16), including a main dish and tea.
  • Upscale restaurant or one with a mountain view: 12,000–20,000 tenge per person (about 90–150 SAR, or $24–40).
  • A family of four for a full day: 25,000–45,000 tenge (about 180–330 SAR, or $48–88) on average.

Tipping is not obligatory, and a 10% service charge is sometimes added in larger restaurants. Visa and Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere; carry a little cash for small restaurants and the bazaar. For full trip costs, see the cost of tourism in Kazakhstan.

The Green Bazaar: the local food experience

The Green Bazaar (Зелёный базар) in central Almaty is the best place to taste local produce: dried fruit and nuts, Kazakh cheeses, mountain honey, the apples the city is named after, and, for the adventurous, kumis (fermented mare's milk) and shubat (camel's milk). Inside the market there are simple restaurants serving shashlik and lagman at low prices.

Where to pray while shopping and sightseeing

  • Almaty Central Mosque in the city centre is one of the largest mosques in Kazakhstan, and Friday prayers there are well attended.
  • Prayer rooms in malls: Mega Center, Dostyk Plaza, Esentai Mall and Forum have prayer rooms for men and women.
  • Neighbourhood mosques are found across the city; search "мечеть" in 2GIS to find the nearest one.

Practical tips for families

  • Book popular restaurants for the evening in July and August, when the Gulf season fills them up.
  • Kazakh dishes are rich and large; one dish is often enough for two people when you are eating with children.
  • Ask for tea "without milk" if you are not used to Kazakh milk tea.
  • Bottled water is available everywhere. Almaty's tap water comes from the mountains, but bottled water is the better choice for children.
  • For vegetarians: vegetable plov, salads and potato samsa are available in Uzbek restaurants.

Frequently asked questions

Is all food in Almaty halal?
Most of it is, especially in Kazakh, Uzbek, Turkish and Uyghur restaurants, but European and Georgian restaurants may serve pork, so always ask or look for the "Халал" sign.

Are there Arabic restaurants in Almaty?
Yes, a growing number of Arabic and Turkish restaurants around Dostyk Avenue and the Golden Square. Their names change every season, and the Arcadia team will point you to the ones that are open.

Do restaurants serve alcohol?
Many do, even alongside halal food. For families who prefer an alcohol-free setting, traditional Uzbek restaurants, food courts and Arabic restaurants are a better fit.

Which dish should I try?
Beshbarmak as the national dish, Uyghur lagman, manty, shashlik, and baursak with tea.

Is eating out expensive?
No. Eating in Almaty costs roughly 40–50% less than in Dubai or Riyadh at mid-range restaurants.
