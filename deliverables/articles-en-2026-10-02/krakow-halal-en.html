Krakow is the Polish city most visited by Gulf families, and finding halal food there is easier than in many other European cities: halal Turkish, Arabic, Indian and Pakistani restaurants in the city centre and the Kazimierz district, food courts in the large malls, and safe vegetarian and fish dishes from the Polish kitchen everywhere. On the other hand, traditional Polish cuisine relies heavily on pork, so do not order any meat dish in a Polish restaurant without asking. This guide sets out the options for a family, the safe dishes, prices in zloty, the mosque and places to pray, and tips for nearby Zakopane.

How do you recognise a halal restaurant in Krakow?

  • Look for a Halal sign on the storefront or on the restaurant's Google Maps page. In Poland the word is written the same way in Polish.
  • Turkish, Arabic, Indian, Pakistani and Bangladeshi restaurants: usually halal, and they say so.
  • Polish and Italian restaurants: ask about the meat. Pork is "wieprzowina", beef is "wołowina" and chicken is "kurczak".
  • The useful phrase is "Czy to jest halal?", and English is spoken in every restaurant in central Krakow.

Best types of halal restaurants for families in Krakow

1. Turkish restaurants (the most common option)

There are dozens of Turkish restaurants around the Main Square (Rynek), Grodzka Street and the Kazimierz district: charcoal-grilled kebab, pide, lahmacun, soups and salads. Most are halal, child-friendly and reasonably priced.

2. Arabic restaurants

Syrian, Lebanese and Palestinian restaurants have opened in recent years in the centre and in Kazimierz, serving grills, shawarma, falafel, hummus, manakish and kunafa. Their names change; the Arcadia team will point you to the ones that are open near your hotel.

3. Indian and Pakistani restaurants

An excellent choice for families who like rich dishes: halal biryani, curry and tandoori chicken, in the city centre and near the railway station.

4. Mall food courts

Galeria Krakowska next to the railway station, Bonarka and Serenada have halal kebab counters, pizza, sushi and desserts. They are the easiest option on rainy days and with children.

5. Safe Polish dishes

You can taste Polish cuisine without worry through pierogi filled with cheese and potato, mushrooms or fruit; vegetarian zapiekanka at Plac Nowy in Kazimierz; żurek soup (ask for it without sausage); grilled fish; fried potatoes; and, in Zakopane, oscypek (grilled mountain cheese served with cranberry).

Sweets and cafés

Krakow is a café city. Polish cakes (sernik cheesecake, szarlotka apple pie), pączki (jam-filled doughnuts) and hot chocolate in the cafés of the Main Square are all safe choices. In summer there is ice cream (lody) on every street.

How much does food cost in Krakow?

One zloty is worth roughly one Saudi riyal, so prices are easy to work out:

  • Kebab or a halal fast-food meal: 25–40 zloty (about $7–11).
  • Mid-range Turkish or Arabic restaurant: 50–90 zloty per person (about $13–24).
  • Upscale restaurant on the Main Square: 120–200 zloty per person (about $32–53).
  • A family of four for a full day: 250–400 zloty (about $67–107).

Bank cards from the Gulf work everywhere, and a 10% tip is optional. For full trip costs, see the cost of a family trip to Poland.

The mosque and places to pray

Krakow has an Islamic centre with a mosque where Friday and daily prayers are held, a few minutes by car from the city centre, and there are informal prayer spaces in some Arabic restaurants. In hotels you can ask for the direction of the qibla, and prayer-time apps work normally. There is no mosque in Zakopane, so prayers there are at the hotel.

Zakopane and the mountains: what to eat

In Zakopane there are fewer halal options: Turkish kebab restaurants on Krupówki Street, and cheese, potato and fish dishes in the mountain inns (karczma), with grilled trout as a safe choice. The best plan is to have your main lunch in Krakow and eat lightly in the mountains, or to take meals with you from Krakow's halal restaurants.

Practical tips

  • Book restaurants on the Main Square for the evening in summer and in December (Christmas market season).
  • Ask about alcohol in cooking; some Polish sauces contain wine.
  • Water in restaurants is charged for; ask for "woda niegazowana" (still water).
  • Many restaurants close the kitchen at 10 pm; Turkish restaurants stay open later.

Frequently asked questions

Is halal food available in Krakow?
Yes, in Turkish, Arabic, Indian and Pakistani restaurants in the city centre and Kazimierz, and in mall food courts.

Are Polish restaurants halal?
Generally not, but vegetarian pierogi, fish and desserts are safe.

Is there a mosque in Krakow?
Yes, an Islamic centre with a mosque where Friday prayers are held.

How much does food cost for a family?
About 250–400 zloty a day for a family of four in mid-range restaurants.
