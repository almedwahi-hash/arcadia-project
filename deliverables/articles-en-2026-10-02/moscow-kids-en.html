Moscow is a city made for families more than many people expect: giant parks, one of the largest indoor amusement parks in Europe, a world-class circus, a metro that looks like a palace, and winter and summer activities in every district. In this guide we have gathered ten experiences tried by the Gulf families who travelled with us to Russia, arranged by age, with a suggested 5-day program and practical tips.

1. Dream Island (Ostrov Mechty)

One of the largest indoor amusement parks in Europe, 15 minutes by metro from the centre of Moscow. Nine themed zones, rides for little ones from the age of three and others for teenagers, and restaurants in the adjoining complex. A full day is not enough, and a single ticket covers all the rides.

2. Gorky Park and the Museum of Cosmonautics

In summer Gorky Park is for bicycles, boats and fountains; in winter it turns into one of the largest ice rinks in the city. The Museum of Cosmonautics, at VDNKh on the other side of the city, displays real spacecraft and spacesuits that captivate children.

3. The Bolshoi Moscow State Circus

A two-hour show with animals, acrobats and magic on most days of the week, suitable for all ages from three years. Book the front seats in advance during the season.

4. The zoo and the dinosaur museum

Moscow Zoo, in the heart of the city, is one of the oldest in Europe, and the Orlov Paleontological Museum has full-size dinosaur skeletons that little ones love.

5. A river cruise by boat

Two hours on the Moskva River, passing the Kremlin, the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour and Gorky Park, with dinner on board. A relaxing experience for the family after a long day of walking, and in winter ice-breaking boats operate.

6. The Moscow Metro

No special ticket is needed: stations such as Komsomolskaya, Mayakovskaya and Ploshchad Revolyutsii, with their chandeliers and mosaics, make children call it "the underground museum".

7. Ostankino Tower and Moscow City

The observation deck of Ostankino Tower, with its glass floor at a height of 337 metres, or the Panorama 360 deck in Moscow City, which bills itself as home to the highest ice-cream factory in the world, with free tasting.

8. Red Square and GUM

The square, the Kremlin and the colourful St Basil's Cathedral, then the historic GUM department store with its famous ice cream, and from late November to February the winter market and the ice rink inside the square itself. The details are in the Red Square guide for Arab tourists.

9. The aquarium and interactive science

The Moskvarium aquarium at the VDNKh exhibition centre, with dolphin shows, and the Experimentanium interactive science museum, where children can touch everything. Both are ideal for rainy and cold days.

10. Shopping and toys at the Central Children's Store

The largest toy store in Russia, in a historic building on Lubyanka Square, a short walk from the Kremlin, with a rooftop observation deck and a Museum of Childhood.

A suggested 5-day program with children

  • Day 1: arrival, Red Square in the evening, ice cream at GUM.
  • Day 2: a full day at Dream Island.
  • Day 3: the Kremlin in the morning, the toy store, the river cruise in the evening.
  • Day 4: Gorky Park or the Museum of Cosmonautics, the circus in the evening.
  • Day 5: Moscow City, shopping at Afimall, departure.

It can be extended to a week by adding more of Moscow's parks and sights (see the 10 best places to visit in Moscow) or two days in St Petersburg through the 10-day Moscow and St Petersburg program.

Tips for families

  • Season: May to September for the parks and boats, and December and January for snow and the winter markets. See the best time for family travel.
  • Accommodation: hotels with family rooms near Tverskaya Street or the Arbat; see the comprehensive Moscow guide.
  • Halal food: Arab, Turkish and Uzbek restaurants in every district, and the Moscow Cathedral Mosque near Prospekt Mira metro station.
  • Getting around: the metro is fast and cheap, and a private car with a driver is included in our program for the long days.
  • Budget: five days for a family of four is roughly $2,500 to $3,800 excluding flights; the breakdown is in the Russia travel cost guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moscow safe for families?

Yes, the city centre and the tourist areas are safe and well policed, and the guide accompanies you throughout the program.

Do I need a visa?

Citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Oman enter Russia without a visa for up to 90 days, and citizens of Kuwait and Bahrain obtain an e-visa within days. See the Russia visa guide.

Do Gulf bank cards work in Moscow?

Not at present: foreign Visa and Mastercard cards do not work in Russia. Bring cash in dollars and exchange it for roubles at the banks (UnionPay and Mir cards are accepted), and we help you pay through local apps when needed.

What is the best age for children?

From 4 to 15 they enjoy the whole list; with infants, stick to the parks and the river cruise.
