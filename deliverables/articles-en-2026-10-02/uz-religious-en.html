Uzbekistan is not only a tourist destination; it is the land of the great imams of hadith, jurisprudence and creed: Imam al-Bukhari in Samarkand, Imam al-Tirmidhi in Termez, Imam al-Maturidi, Abu al-Layth al-Samarqandi, Baha-ud-Din Naqshband in Bukhara, Burhan al-Din al-Marghinani, author of "Al-Hidayah", in Fergana, and the Mushaf of Uthman ibn Affan, kept in Tashkent. A trip to Uzbekistan therefore combines sightseeing and religious visits at the same time, and this is what makes it the favourite destination of many Gulf families who want a journey with meaning. This guide sets out the most important religious and historic sites in Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Termez, with a 6-day itinerary, visit etiquette and costs.

Tashkent: the Uthman Quran and the Hazrati Imam complex

  • Hazrati Imam complex: the religious centre of the capital, comprising the Hazrati Imam Mosque, the Barak-Khan Madrasah, the Muyi Muborak Madrasah and the mausoleum of Imam al-Qaffal al-Shashi (one of the leading Shafi'i jurists of the fourth century AH).
  • The Uthman Quran at the Center for Islamic Civilization: in November 2025 the Uthman Quran was moved from the Muyi Muborak Library to the new Center for Islamic Civilization next to the Hazrati Imam complex. It is one of the oldest Quran manuscripts in the world, written in Kufic script on deerskin parchment and attributed to the era of the Caliph Uthman ibn Affan (may God be pleased with him). Check the entry fee and photography rules when you visit.
  • Kukeldash Madrasah: from the 16th century, near Chorsu Bazaar, and still working as a religious school.
  • Minor Mosque: a modern white-marble mosque on the bank of the canal, good for prayer and for photographs.

Tashkent needs one day. For where to stay in the capital, see the best hotels in Tashkent.

Samarkand: Imam al-Bukhari, Shah-i-Zinda and the Registan

  • Imam al-Bukhari complex: in the village of Khartank, 25 kilometres from Samarkand, with the mausoleum of Imam Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari, author of "Al-Jami al-Sahih" (Sahih al-Bukhari), a mosque and a museum. The complex was rebuilt and reopened in March 2026 with a mosque for more than ten thousand worshippers, a new museum and hotels for visitors. Allow at least two hours, and early morning is quieter.
  • Mausoleum of Imam al-Maturidi: Abu Mansur al-Maturidi, the imam of Sunni Muslims in creed; his mausoleum is in the Chokardiza quarter inside the city.
  • Shah-i-Zinda: a street of mausoleums decorated with blue tilework, dating from the 11th to the 15th century, and said to hold the grave of Qutham ibn Abbas, cousin of the Prophet (peace be upon him). The most beautiful place for photographs in the whole of Uzbekistan.
  • Registan Square: three great madrasahs (Ulugh Beg, Sher-Dor and Tilya-Kori) that were the centre of learning in Central Asia.
  • Bibi-Khanym Mosque: one of the largest mosques in the Islamic world in the 15th century, with the Siab Bazaar for dried fruit next to it.
  • Gur-e-Amir Mausoleum: the tomb of Timur (Tamerlane) and his descendants; its architecture influenced the later Mughal tombs.

The details are in Samarkand, the capital of the Silk Road.

Bukhara: Naqshband and the Kalyan Minaret

  • Baha-ud-Din Naqshband complex: 12 kilometres from Bukhara, the mausoleum of the founder of the Naqshbandi order, with a mosque and gardens; people visit it from all over Central Asia.
  • Kalyan Minaret and Kalyan Mosque (Po-i-Kalyan): a 12th-century minaret 47 metres high and a mosque that holds ten thousand worshippers; facing them is the Mir-i-Arab Madrasah, which still teaches the Islamic sciences.
  • Ismail Samani Mausoleum: one of the oldest standing Islamic buildings in Central Asia (10th century), with unique brickwork decoration.
  • Chashma-Ayub: "Job's Spring", linked in local tradition to the Prophet Ayyub (Job), peace be upon him.
  • Chor Minor Madrasah and the Lyabi-Hauz complex for the evening and dinner by the pool.

Termez: Imam al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi

The city of Termez on the Afghan border (an hour's flight from Tashkent, or an overnight train) has the mausoleum of Imam Abu Isa al-Tirmidhi, author of "Sunan al-Tirmidhi", in the nearby town of Sherabad, the mausoleum of al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi on the bank of the Amu Darya river, and the old mosque and fortress of Kyrk-Kyz. The visit needs two extra days, and suits those who want the complete religious itinerary.

Fergana: Imam al-Marghinani and the city of silk

In the city of Margilan in the Fergana Valley are the memorial complex of Burhan al-Din al-Marghinani, author of "Al-Hidayah" in Hanafi jurisprudence, and the traditional Yodgorlik silk factory; in Rishtan there is the art of blue ceramics. It adds one or two nights, by train from Tashkent.

A 6-day itinerary: religious visits and sightseeing together

  1. Day 1, Tashkent: Hazrati Imam and the Uthman Quran at the Center for Islamic Civilization, Kukeldash, Chorsu Bazaar, Minor Mosque.
  2. Day 2, Samarkand: the high-speed Afrosiyob train in the morning (two hours), the Imam al-Bukhari complex, then Imam al-Maturidi, and the Registan in the evening.
  3. Day 3, Samarkand: Shah-i-Zinda in the morning, Bibi-Khanym and the Siab Bazaar, Gur-e-Amir, and the Ulugh Beg Observatory.
  4. Day 4, Bukhara: the train to Bukhara, the Naqshband complex in the afternoon, Lyabi-Hauz in the evening.
  5. Day 5, Bukhara: Kalyan and Mir-i-Arab, the Samani Mausoleum, Chashma-Ayub, the Ark Fortress, the domed bazaars.
  6. Day 6: flight from Bukhara to Tashkent and the journey home, or a two-day extension to Termez or Khiva.

Visit etiquette and dress

  • The mausoleums are places for supplication and recitation; keep quiet, do not walk in circuits around the graves, and keep to what you know of the guidance of the Sunnah.
  • A headscarf is required for women inside mosques and mausoleums, modest clothing for everyone, and shoes are removed on entering.
  • Photography is allowed in the outer courtyards, and not allowed at the Uthman Quran or inside some mausoleums.
  • Friday prayers are held in Arabic and Uzbek at the Kalyan Mosque, Hazrati Imam and the Minor Mosque, and the five daily prayers in all the mosques mentioned.
  • Giving charity to those in need at the gates of the mausoleums is a local custom, and there is no obligation.

Food, prayer and travel

Uzbekistan is a country that is more than 90% Muslim, and the food is halal in practically every restaurant: Samarkand plov, shashlik, samsa, lagman and tandoor bread. There are mosques in every neighbourhood, and bottled water is widely available. Citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman enter without a visa for 30 days. The best time for a religious trip

April to May and September to October for mild weather (20–28°C); summer is hot in Bukhara (40°C). Ramadan in Uzbekistan has a special atmosphere: the mosques are full and there are communal iftars in the squares, but some restaurants close during the day. Frequently asked questions

Where exactly is the mausoleum of Imam al-Bukhari?
In the village of Khartank, about 25 kilometres north of Samarkand; the drive takes 35 minutes.

Can the Uthman Quran be seen?
Yes. Since November 2025 it has been on display at the Center for Islamic Civilization next to the Hazrati Imam complex in Tashkent.

Is the trip suitable for older travellers?

How many days are enough for the religious visits?
5–6 days for Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara, and 8 days with Termez or Fergana.
