Uzbekistan is one of the closest and cheapest historic destinations to Saudi Arabia: a flight of four to five hours from Riyadh or Jeddah, visa-free entry, a Muslim country where local food is halal, and the cities of Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva, which every Saudi knows from the history books, along with the shrines of Imam al-Bukhari and Imam al-Tirmidhi. This guide is written specifically for Saudi travellers: entry, flights, costs in riyals, the best time according to the Saudi holidays, and the religious visits.

Visa-free entry for Saudis

Holders of a Saudi passport enter Uzbekistan without a visa for 30 days; a passport valid for 6 months is enough. There are no fees and no advance registration, and hotels register your stay automatically and give you a registration slip, which you should keep until you leave. Children travel on their own passports.

The best time for Saudis, according to the holidays

  • The spring break (March), and Eid al-Fitr if it falls in spring: Nowruz on 21 March and temperatures of 15–22°C; excellent.
  • April to May and September to October: the best of all (20–30°C); suits mid-term breaks, teachers and retired travellers.
  • The summer holiday (July to August): very hot in Bukhara and Khiva (40°C and above), but the trip works with morning and evening tours and a hotel with a pool, and prices are about 25% lower. The last two weeks of August are better than July.
  • The mid-year break (January): cold (0–8°C), but it is the cheapest time and the sights are empty; for families without small children.

The religious visits Saudis ask for

  • The Imam al-Bukhari complex in the village of Khartank near Samarkand (about 35 minutes by car): a large complex with a mosque, a museum and a library, rebuilt and reopened in March 2026; allow two hours.
  • The shrine of Baha-ud-Din Naqshband outside Bukhara (15 minutes).
  • The Imam al-Tirmidhi complex in Termez in the south of the country (a domestic flight, or 6 hours by car from Samarkand) for those who want the complete journey.
  • The Uthman Quran (may God be pleased with him), one of the oldest Quran manuscripts in the world; since November 2025 it has been kept at the new Center for Islamic Civilization next to the Hazrati Imam complex in Tashkent.
  • The mausoleum of Imam al-Maturidi in Samarkand, and the historic madrasahs of the Registan.

A 7-day itinerary that suits a Saudi family

Tashkent for one night (Hazrati Imam and Chorsu Bazaar), then Samarkand for two nights (the Registan, Shah-i-Zinda, Imam al-Bukhari), then Bukhara for two nights (the old city, Naqshband), then a last night in Tashkent for shopping, or Khiva by air instead. Tips specific to Saudi travellers

  • Modest dress is comfortable and familiar here; there are no special restrictions, and the abaya does not attract attention.
  • A Ucell or Beeline SIM card from the airport (about 20 SAR, or $5, for 20 GB) is far cheaper than roaming.
  • The heritage hotels in Bukhara and Samarkand are small and sell out in April and October; book two months ahead.
  • The Saudi embassy in Tashkent is available for emergencies.
  • Gifts: silk from Margilan, ceramics from Rishtan, and saffron and nuts from the Siab Bazaar in Samarkand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Uzbekistan suitable for older travellers?
Yes, the old cities are flat and the distances are short, the train is comfortable, and we choose hotels close to the sights to reduce walking.

How many days are enough for the religious visits together with sightseeing?
7 days are enough for Samarkand and Bukhara and their shrines; 9 days to add Termez or Khiva.

Are prices really lower than in Turkey?
Generally yes for hotels, food and transport, and with far fewer crowds.

Can I pay in riyals?
No; exchange US dollars or use your card, and ATMs give som at a good rate.
