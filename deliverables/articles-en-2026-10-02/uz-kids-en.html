Uzbekistan with children is easier than many parents expect: short journeys on the high-speed train, halal food that children love (samsa, bread and plov), wide squares to run around in, evening light shows, farms and craft villages, and a people who adore children. On the other hand, there are things to prepare for: the summer heat, the cobbled pavements of the old cities, which do not suit pushchairs, and the lack of large amusement parks outside Tashkent. This guide sets out the 10 best family activities in Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara, a 6-day program, and tips on health, food and getting around.

The best time to visit Uzbekistan with children

April–May and September–October: 20–28°C, ideal for walking and for the squares. Summer (June–August) is very hot (38–42°C in Bukhara) and calls for a morning and evening program with a midday nap; winter is cold (0–8°C) but quiet and cheap. The 10 best activities for children in Uzbekistan

1. Magic City amusement park in Tashkent

A theme park in the style of European castles, with rides, fountains, restaurants and evening shows, and the favourite spot of Tashkent's families. It suits all ages and stays open late in summer.

2. Chimgan and Lake Charvak (a day trip from Tashkent)

Mountains about an hour and a half to two hours from the capital, with a cable car, views and horse riding, and the blue Lake Charvak with boats and resorts on the shore. The perfect escape from the summer heat.

3. The Tashkent metro and the Railway Museum

Decorated metro stations that look like palaces (the space-themed Kosmonavtlar station is the most famous), and the Railway Museum with real locomotives that children can climb aboard.

4. The Registan by night in Samarkand

In the evening the three madrasahs are lit up, and in certain seasons shows with light and music are held on their facades. The square is wide and safe for running around.

5. Siab Bazaar and its sweets

The largest market in Samarkand, next to the Bibi-Khanym Mosque: decorated Samarkand bread, halva and dried fruit, and the stallholders generously offer children free samples.

6. Konigil village and silk-paper making

Near Samarkand, a traditional workshop makes paper from mulberry bark using a water mill, and children take part in making their own sheet of paper, with a restaurant by the stream and a small farm.

7. The puppet show near Lyabi-Hauz in Bukhara

The Lyabi-Hauz pool is surrounded by madrasahs and restaurants, with a traditional puppet theatre for children close by and camel rides near the fortress; in season there is an evening folklore show with dinner in the Nadir Divan-Begi Madrasah.

8. The Ark of Bukhara and the domed bazaars

A historic fortress with ramps and terraces to explore, domed bazaars selling bird-shaped scissors, puppets and hats, and craftsmen's workshops that welcome little ones.

9. The Afrosiyob high-speed train

The train between Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara reaches up to 250 km/h, with comfortable seats and a buffet car, and children treat it as an activity in itself. It saves time and spares you hours in the car.

10. The zoo and the Japanese Garden in Tashkent

A large zoo, and a Japanese garden with koi fish and wooden bridges next to the Tashkent TV Tower, which you can go up for a view over the city.

A 6-day program for the family

  1. Day 1, Tashkent: arrival, the Japanese Garden, Magic City in the evening.
  2. Day 2, Tashkent: the Hazrati Imam complex and the Uthman Quran, Chorsu Bazaar, the metro, the Railway Museum. Or a trip to Chimgan and Charvak.
  3. Day 3, Samarkand: the high-speed train in the morning, the Registan, Shah-i-Zinda, the Registan lit up in the evening.
  4. Day 4, Samarkand: Siab Bazaar, Konigil village, Gur-e-Amir, a rest at the hotel in the afternoon.
  5. Day 5, Bukhara: the train, Lyabi-Hauz and the puppet show, the domed bazaars, the folklore show.
  6. Day 6, Bukhara: the Ark, the Kalyan Minaret and a camel ride, the evening flight or train to Tashkent and the journey home.

Food for children

Local food is halal. The dishes children like best: samsa with meat, straight from the tandoor, bread (non), mild plov, chicken shashlik, manti, and fresh juices and cheap seasonal fruit (watermelon, grapes and apricots). Milk and yoghurt are in every supermarket, and bottled water is essential. For the practical details, see Uzbekistan facts: entry, flights and costs.

Health and getting around: tips

  • Pharmacies are everywhere and basic medicines are available, but bring your children's own medicines from home.
  • A pushchair is useful in Tashkent and around the hotels, and difficult in the lanes of Bukhara and Khiva; a baby carrier is better there.
  • The sun is strong: hats, sunscreen and water at all times, and in summer schedule visits before 11 am and after 5 pm.
  • Family hotels with pools are available in all three cities; ask for connecting rooms in advance.
  • Distances within the cities are short, and a private car with an Arabic-speaking guide makes long days possible with children.

Frequently asked questions

Is Uzbekistan safe for children?
Yes, it is one of the safest countries in the region, and the people are very friendly with children.

From what age does the trip suit children?
From 4 years and up the activities are enjoyable; infants are possible if you cut down the walking in the old cities.

Is the food suitable for children?
Yes, the dishes are simple and mostly not spicy, and local cuisine is halal.

Is the train or the car better between the cities?
The high-speed train is more comfortable and faster for children, and the car is for short trips within the cities and their surroundings.

Can it be combined with Kazakhstan?
