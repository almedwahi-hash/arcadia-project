Visa-free entry to Russia for Saudi citizens

On 11 May 2026 the agreement on the mutual waiver of visas between Saudi Arabia and Russia came into force, so the e-visa is no longer required for short visits. What matters to the traveller:

  • Who is covered: holders of ordinary, diplomatic and special Saudi passports.
  • Length of stay: up to 90 days per calendar year, in one stay or several.
  • Purposes covered: tourism, business visits, private visits, cultural and sporting events, and transit.
  • Not covered: work, study and permanent residence, which require a dedicated visa.
  • Before you travel: a valid passport, a hotel booking and a return ticket; check the latest official instructions, because the procedures may change.

Paying in Russia: the most important thing a Saudi traveller should know

Saudi Mada, Visa and Mastercard cards have not worked inside Russia since 2022, and the same goes for Apple Pay and similar apps. The practical solutions: (1) pay for the whole program to Arcadia before you travel, so that the hotels, the car, the tickets and most meals are already paid for; (2) carry US dollars or euros in cash (the riyal is not widely handled at exchange offices) and change them at a bank in the city, not at the airport; (3) ask us for a prepaid Mir card for shopping if you wish. The usual cash budget is $100–150 a day per family for shopping and meals not included in the program.

The best time for Saudi travellers, by holiday

  • Summer holiday (July – August): the best; 22–28 degrees, the Peterhof fountains are running, river cruises and parks. Book two months ahead.
  • Late May – June: the White Nights in St Petersburg, the most beautiful season and the most expensive.
  • Eid al-Adha: in late spring in the coming years, with mild weather; the Moscow Cathedral Mosque (one of the largest mosques in Europe) holds the Eid prayer.
  • Mid-year break (January): snow and New Year markets, but the cold reaches -10 degrees; for those who want winter, and avoid 1–8 January (a crowded Russian public holiday).

Halal food and prayer

Russia as a whole has an estimated 15–20 million Muslims, and in Moscow there are halal Tatar, Uzbek, Caucasian and Turkish restaurants in every district; chains such as Chaihona No. 1 and Uryuk are widespread (ask about halal at each branch). The Moscow Cathedral Mosque is near Prospekt Mira station, and the blue St Petersburg Mosque is near the Peter and Paul Fortress. Our guide fits the prayer times into the program.

Tips for the Saudi traveller

  • Carry your passport at all times; the police, museums and railway stations ask for it.
  • Install Yandex Go, Yandex Maps and an offline translator; Google Maps works but is less accurate for public transport.
  • A Russian SIM card (MTS or Beeline) requires a passport and registration at purchase, which now takes longer for foreigners, so allow time for it; 20 GB costs about 25 SAR (around $7).
  • The Saudi embassy in Moscow is available for emergencies; register your trip on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs platform before you travel.
  • Children under 7 travel free on the Moscow metro, and many museums admit children free or at a discount.
