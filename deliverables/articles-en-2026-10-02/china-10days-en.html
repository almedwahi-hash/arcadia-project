Ten days is the ideal length for a first family trip to China: enough for Beijing and Shanghai plus a third city with a different character, without wearing the children out. We designed this program for Gulf families who have actually travelled with us, and we set it out here with alternatives by season, the approximate cost and what to prepare before you travel.

The program at a glance

  • Days 1–4: Beijing — the Great Wall, the Forbidden City, the pandas and the Summer Palace.
  • Days 5–6: Xi'an — the Terracotta Army, cycling on the city wall and the Muslim Quarter.
  • Days 7–10: Shanghai — the Bund, Disneyland, Shanghai Tower and the water town of Zhujiajiao.

Travel between the cities is by high-speed train: Beijing–Xi'an takes about 4.5 hours on the fastest trains and Xi'an–Shanghai 6 to 7 hours (or about two and a half hours by air).

Day 1: arrival in Beijing

Airport pick-up by private car, hotel check-in, then a light evening stroll along Wangfujing Street. Do not push the children on arrival day.

Day 2: the Forbidden City and the Temple of Heaven

Tiananmen Square, then the Forbidden City in the morning (advance booking with your passport), Jingshan Hill for the view, a halal lunch in the Niujie district, and the Temple of Heaven park in the afternoon.

Day 3: the Great Wall of China

The Mutianyu section, going up by cable car and coming down by toboggan; set off at seven in the morning to avoid the crowds. The evening is free, or you can see an acrobatics show.

Day 4: the pandas and the Summer Palace

Beijing Zoo to see the pandas, then the Summer Palace and a boat ride on Kunming Lake. The capital's activities are covered in full in Beijing with kids.

Day 5: high-speed train to Xi'an and the Terracotta Army

The morning train to Xi'an, then straight to the Terracotta Army museum (an hour from the city). Thousands of life-size clay soldiers are an experience children remember for a long time.

Day 6: Xi'an city wall and the Muslim Quarter

Cycling (or riding multi-seat carts) on top of the old city wall, then the Great Mosque of Xi'an and the alleys of the Muslim Quarter with its famous halal food. In the evening, the train or a flight to Shanghai.

Day 7: modern Shanghai

Shanghai Tower (the highest observation deck in the city), then the Bund promenade along the river and a night river cruise to see the lights of Pudong. See our Shanghai guide for Arab visitors.

Day 8: Shanghai Disneyland

A full day at the park with the largest Disney castle in the world. Tickets are bought in advance through the official app, and arriving before opening saves hours of queueing. An alternative for younger children: the aquarium and the wild animal park.

Day 9: Zhujiajiao water town and Yu Garden

In the morning, the old water town with its canals and bridges (by traditional boat), and in the afternoon Yu Garden and its bazaar in the Old City. In the evening, shopping on Nanjing Road.

Day 10: departure

Free time for last-minute shopping, then the transfer to the airport. If you fly from Pudong Airport, the Maglev train from Longyang Road station takes 8 minutes, which is an experience in itself for children.

Alternatives by season and interest

  • Instead of Xi'an: Chengdu for the panda breeding base and Sichuan cuisine, or Guilin for the famous mountains of the Li River (suitable from April to October).
  • In winter: add two days for the ice festival in Harbin (about three hours by air from Shanghai), if the children can cope with severe cold.
  • For families travelling on business: two days can be added in Guangzhou or Yiwu for the markets; see the best cities to visit in China.

Approximate cost for a family of four

Excluding international flights, and based on 4-star hotels and a private car with an Arabic-speaking guide:

  • Hotels (9 nights): $1,400–2,100.
  • Domestic trains: $350–450.
  • Attraction tickets: $400–550 (Disneyland alone is about $300).
  • Car and guide: $120–180 per day.
  • Food: $50–90 per day.

Approximate total: $5,000–7,000 for ten days. More detail in China travel costs 2026.

What to prepare before you travel

  • A China visa, or a check of the latest exemption rules for your nationality: citizens of Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain currently enter visa-free for up to 30 days under a policy that runs until 31 December 2026, and citizens of the UAE and Qatar are visa-exempt for 30 days under standing mutual agreements.
  • The Alipay and WeChat apps linked to an international card, and Google Translate with the Chinese language pack downloaded for offline use.
  • An eSIM or a VPN plan chosen from our guide to the internet in China; Google, WhatsApp, Instagram and most Western apps are blocked in mainland China without a VPN or a roaming eSIM.
  • Your original passports (and a copy as a backup), because every railway station and major attraction asks for the passport on entry.
  • A list of halal restaurants in each city from our halal food guide.

Frequently asked questions

Are 10 days too many for children?
No, as long as you keep to one main activity in the morning and a lighter one in the afternoon, with two relatively restful days (days 1 and 10).

Is the high-speed train better than flying between the cities?
Yes, for journeys of up to 5 hours: the stations are central, there is no long wait and the seats are spacious. For Xi'an–Shanghai, flying is the better option.

Are family rooms available in the hotels?
Connecting rooms are less common than in the Gulf; we book family suites or two adjacent rooms for you, confirmed in advance.

Which month is best?
April–May and September–October. Avoid Golden Week (1–7 October) and the Chinese summer holidays in July and August.
