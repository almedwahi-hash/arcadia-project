"Almaty or Astana?" is the question we hear from every family planning a first trip to Kazakhstan. The two cities are completely different. Almaty is a city of mountains, lakes and nature 30 minutes from your hotel; Astana is a modern capital of towers, malls and futuristic museums on an open plain. This guide compares them on everything that matters to an Arab traveller: flights, weather, activities, children, food, cost and the number of days, and then gives a clear recommendation for each type of trip.

Flights and getting there

Most direct flights from Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai, Doha and Kuwait arrive in Almaty, which alone makes it the natural starting point. Astana has fewer direct flights and is usually added as a domestic flight from Almaty (1 hour 45 minutes, several flights a day) or by the overnight fast train (about 12 hours). Gulf citizens enter visa-free for 30 days in both cases; see our Kazakhstan entry guide.

Weather: the biggest difference between the two cities

In summer both cities are comfortable, but Almaty's advantage is that escaping the heat to the mountains takes half an hour. In winter the gap is enormous: Almaty's winter is relatively mild and snowy in the mountains, while Astana reaches -30°C with steppe winds. We do not recommend it for families from December to February, unless you want to experience extreme cold. The best months for Astana are May to September. See the best time for family travel.

Activities and sights

What only Almaty offers

  • Mountain nature: Shymbulak and Medeu, Big Almaty Lake and the Assy plateau.
  • The big day trips: Charyn Canyon and the Kolsai and Kaindy lakes.
  • The older city: tree-shaded streets, Kok Tobe, the Green Bazaar and Panfilov Street.
  • Winter: skiing and skating at Shymbulak and Medeu.

What only Astana offers

  • Modern architecture: the Baiterek tower, the Khan Shatyr tent with its indoor beach, the Nur Alem sphere (the Expo site) and the pyramid-shaped Palace of Peace and Reconciliation.
  • Astana Grand Mosque: the largest mosque in Central Asia, opened in 2022.
  • Museums: the National Museum, and the science museum at Nur Alem for children.
  • Burabay (Borovoe): lakes and pine forests three hours away, a different kind of nature from Almaty's mountains.

For more on both, see the cities of Kazakhstan.

With children: which is better?

Almaty wins for children over 5: cable cars, horses, lakes, water parks and a zoo, as described in family trips in Kazakhstan. Astana is excellent for small children over two days: the warm indoor beach at Khan Shatyr, the aquarium at Ailand and the science museum are all indoors, climate-controlled and pushchair-friendly.

Food and hotels

Food is halal in both cities, with Kazakh, Uzbek and Turkish restaurants everywhere; see halal restaurants in Almaty. Astana's hotels are generally newer (most were built after 2010) at prices similar to Almaty's, while Almaty stands out for hotels with mountain views and the mountain resort at Shymbulak, as covered in the best hotels in Almaty.

Cost

The difference is small. A day in Almaty with a private car and guide costs roughly the same as a day in Astana. The real difference is the domestic flight (about 250–450 SAR, or $65–120, per person one way) if you visit both. Details are in the cost of tourism in Kazakhstan.

  • A business trip or conference in Astana: add 3 days in Almaty before you fly home.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Almaty and Astana in short?
Almaty is nature, mountains and an older city; Astana is a modern capital of towers and architectural landmarks.

Which one is the capital?
Astana has been the capital since 1997. Almaty is the former capital, the largest city and the commercial and tourism centre.

Can you visit both cities in a week?
Yes: 5 days in Almaty and 2 in Astana, with a domestic flight.

Which is cheaper?
They are similar; the only difference is the domestic flight.

Which is better in winter?
Almaty, without competition, because of the skiing and the relatively mild weather.
