The best time to visit Moscow for families is usually from late May to June, or during September, when walking is easier and the days are well suited to sightseeing. Summer gives you long hours outside the hotel, while winter offers a completely different scene but needs good preparation and a plan that focuses more on indoor attractions.

Important note: the famous White Nights are associated mainly with St Petersburg. Moscow enjoys long summer days, but the two experiences should not be presented as the same thing.

Moscow by season

Spring: March to May

March and early April are a transitional period, and the ground may be wet or cold. Walking usually gets easier in May and the parks turn greener, but rain and changing temperatures remain possible. Carry a light layer and an umbrella, and have an indoor alternative ready.

Summer: June to August

The longest days of the year and the most flexible for sightseeing, parks and river cruises. June is very suitable for families, while some days in July and August can be hot or crowded. Book timed-entry attractions in advance and leave midday for a rest when needed.

Autumn: September to November

September usually combines milder weather with beautiful colours, which makes it one of the strongest choices. In October temperatures drop and the weather changes more quickly, while November is close to winter and calls for a coat and a shorter plan outdoors.

Winter: December to February

Moscow in winter is a beautiful cultural and city experience for those who prepare for the cold: museums, shows, cafes and landmarks covered in snow when conditions allow. Long outdoor tours are not suitable for every child, and you should build in warm-up breaks and shorter transfers.

The best time for Red Square and the Kremlin

Early morning on a working day is usually quieter than the middle of the day, but closures and events can change access. Book the official timed-entry routes, and do not schedule a second demanding visit on the same day with young children. The practical details are in our Moscow guide.

Moscow with children

  • One main activity a day, with a park or a flexible break.
  • A hotel close to a useful metro station, not just the name of a famous district.
  • A light coat and an umbrella in the transitional seasons, and proper winter clothing when it is cold.
  • An indoor back-up plan for any rainy or very cold day.
  • Extra time for security, entry and changing stations.

When should you combine Moscow and St Petersburg?

Seven to nine nights usually allow a comfortable split between the two cities. If your aim is the White Nights, plan for St Petersburg and see our White Nights guide. A short Moscow trip, on the other hand, is better not squeezed by adding a second city.

What changes in the program with the season?

In summer the program adds a river cruise, Gorky Park, Zaryadye Park and Kolomenskoye, and hotels are booked two months ahead. In winter these are replaced by ice skating in Gorky Park and at VDNKh, the indoor Dream Island theme park and more museums, with thermal clothing and non-slip shoes, and hotels are about 30% cheaper outside New Year week. Frequently asked questions

Is Moscow cold in summer?
Summer is usually mild to warm, but evenings and rain can make it feel cooler. Take a light layer and check the daily forecast.

Is January suitable for a family?
It suits families who can cope with the cold and want a winter program, but it is not the easiest choice for young children or for anyone who wants long hours outdoors.

Is September better than June?
Both are good; June gives longer days and greenery, while September is usually milder and quieter, with early autumn colours. Choose according to your program.
