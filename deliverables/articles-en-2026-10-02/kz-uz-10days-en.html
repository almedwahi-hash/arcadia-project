Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are neighbours that complement each other: the first is mountains, lakes and snow, the second is Islamic history and turquoise domes on the Silk Road. Almaty and Tashkent are only an hour and a half apart by air, so a family can combine the two destinations in a single ten-day trip, visa-free for Gulf citizens in both countries. We have run this program with dozens of families, and here we set it out day by day with the approximate cost.

Why combine Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan?

  • Visa-free for citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman in both countries.
  • Complete variety: mountains and lakes in Kazakhstan, historic cities and bazaars in Uzbekistan.
  • Halal food everywhere, and both peoples welcome Arab visitors.
  • Reasonable cost: noticeably less than a comparable European trip, with good hotels.

Best time for the combined trip

The best months are May and early June, or September and October: the mountains around Almaty are green and mild, and Samarkand and Bukhara are between 20 and 28 degrees. In July and August Almaty is excellent but Uzbekistan is hot (35 to 40 degrees), so its tours are arranged for the morning and evening. The itinerary day by day

Day 1: arrival in Almaty

Airport pick-up, hotel check-in and an evening stroll along Arbat street and in Panfilov Park.

Day 2: Medeu and Shymbulak

A three-stage cable car up to 3,200 metres, lunch at the top, and a visit to the Green Bazaar on the way back.

Day 3: Big Almaty Lake and Kok Tobe

The turquoise lake in the morning, then Kok Tobe hill and its view over the city at night.

Day 4: Charyn Canyon

A full-day trip to the "Grand Canyon of Kazakhstan" with lunch at the camp. See the top 10 attractions in Kazakhstan.

Day 5: flight to Tashkent

A morning flight (an hour and a half), then the Hazrati Imam complex, the Uthman Quran at the Center for Islamic Civilization, Chorsu Bazaar and the Tashkent Metro with its decorated stations.

Day 6: Afrosiyob train to Samarkand

Just over two hours on the high-speed train. Registan Square and its three madrasas, and the light show in the evening.

Day 7: Samarkand

The Gur-e-Amir mausoleum of Tamerlane, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque, the Shah-i-Zinda necropolis and the Ulugh Beg Observatory. Details in Samarkand, capital of the Silk Road.

Day 8: to Bukhara

By train (one and a half to three hours, depending on the service) or by car. The Kalyan Minaret, the domed bazaars and the Lyabi-Hauz complex in the evening.

Day 9: Bukhara and the Ark Fortress

The Ark Fortress, the Bolo Hauz Mosque, craft workshops and dinner in a traditional house.

Day 10: departure

Fly home from Tashkent (or from Bukhara when a suitable flight is available), or return to Almaty if your return flight leaves from there.

For a family of four, roughly $4,500 to $6,000 depending on the season and the hotel category. The breakdown is in the cost of tourism in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan facts: entry, flights and costs.

Tips for the combined trip

  • Book your arrival into Almaty and your return from Tashkent or Bukhara (an open-jaw ticket) to save a full day.
  • The Almaty–Tashkent flight operates daily with several airlines; we book it for you as part of the program.
  • The Afrosiyob train fills up in high season and is booked weeks before the trip.
  • The two currencies are different (the tenge and the som); exchange only what you need in each country.
  • With small children, replace the Charyn day with a relaxed day in Almaty and reduce Bukhara to one day.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Uzbekistan or Kazakhstan?

No. Citizens of the six Gulf states enter both countries visa-free for up to 30 days.

Are 10 days too many for children?

The program is balanced between nature and cities, and it can be shortened to 8 days by dropping Bukhara or Charyn.

Which comes first: Kazakhstan or Uzbekistan?

We usually start with Kazakhstan so the trip opens with the mountains and ends in the historic cities, finishing in Tashkent or Bukhara.

Is the food halal in both countries?

Yes. Both are Muslim countries and the food in restaurants and bazaars is halal.
