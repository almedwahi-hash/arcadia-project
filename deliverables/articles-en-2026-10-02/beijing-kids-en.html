Beijing is not only a city of museums and palaces; it is one of the most family-friendly capitals in Asia: spacious parks, the Great Wall of China two hours away, a huge zoo and aquarium, and halal food readily available in the Muslim quarter. In this guide we have gathered the best activities for children of different ages, with a suggested four-day program and an approximate cost, as part of your trip to China with Arcadia.

When to visit Beijing with children

The two best periods are April–May and September–October: mild weather and mostly clear skies, which matters when you climb the Wall. Summer is hot, humid and crowded with the Chinese school holidays, and winter is cold and dry but suits those who like ice festivals and lower prices. Avoid Golden Week (the start of October) and the Chinese New Year holiday (January/February) if you can.

1. The Great Wall of China: the Mutianyu section

The Mutianyu section is the best choice for families: it is less crowded than Badaling, it has a cable car for the way up, and a toboggan for the way down that children aged 6 and over adore. Set aside a full day with a private driver, and leave early to arrive before the tour buses.

2. The Forbidden City and Jingshan Park

Enter through the Meridian Gate on the south side (reached from Tiananmen Square, which needs its own advance reservation) and walk north through the palaces. For children, the fun lies in the golden roofs, the bronze lions and the vast courtyards. After leaving by the northern gate, climb Jingshan Hill to see the whole Forbidden City from above. Book tickets in advance with your passports, because daily numbers are limited.

3. Beijing Zoo and the Panda House

Seeing the giant pandas is usually the moment children remember most from the whole trip. The zoo is close to the city centre, and next to it is the aquarium, which has dolphin shows.

4. The Summer Palace and a boat ride

Gardens, a wide lake and a long corridor painted with thousands of pictures. Hire a boat on Kunming Lake or take the dragon boat from the pier to the temple island; a calm activity that suits small children and grandparents alike.

5. Niujie, the Muslim quarter, and halal food

The Niujie district is home to the oldest mosque in Beijing, the Niujie Mosque, and many halal restaurants (grilled lamb, soups, sweets). It is also useful for buying snacks for the following days. Read our guide to halal food in China before you travel.

6. The Temple of Heaven and its park

Early morning in the Temple of Heaven park is an experience in itself: older residents practise tai chi, play cards and dance. Children enjoy the green spaces and the "Echo Wall".

7. The hutongs by rickshaw

A rickshaw ride through the old alleys around Houhai Lake gives children a feel for everyday life in old Beijing. In winter the lake freezes over and turns into a rink for skating and ice bikes.

8. Universal Beijing Resort

It opened in 2021 and includes Harry Potter, Transformers and Kung Fu Panda areas. It needs a full day, and tickets are bought in advance through the official app. Very well suited to ages 5 to 15.

9. The China Science and Technology Museum

One of the largest interactive science museums in Asia, with a hall dedicated to children and a dome cinema. An excellent choice for a rainy or very hot day.

10. An acrobatics show and a kung fu show

In the evening, a Chinese acrobatics show or a kung fu show; the performances last a little over an hour and need no language.

A suggested 4-day program

  • Day 1: Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City, Jingshan Hill, lunch in Niujie, an acrobatics show in the evening.
  • Day 2: the Great Wall of China (Mutianyu) by cable car and toboggan, then back to the hotel to rest.
  • Day 3: the zoo and the pandas, the aquarium, the Summer Palace and a boat ride.
  • Day 4: the Temple of Heaven in the morning, the hutongs by rickshaw, Wangfujing for shopping, or a full day at Universal.

Approximate cost for a family of four

The figures are approximate for four days and vary with the season and the hotel:

  • Tickets to the sights: $150–250 (Universal is separate: about $350 for the family).

Practical tips for families

  • Check the China visa rules early: citizens of Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain enter visa-free for up to 30 days under a policy that currently runs until 31 December 2026, and citizens of the UAE and Qatar are visa-exempt for 30 days under standing mutual agreements. Confirm the latest exemption and transit rules for your nationality before you book.
  • Install the Alipay and WeChat apps and link an international Visa or Mastercard card; cash is rarely used, and most shops accept neither foreign currency nor foreign cards directly.
  • Arrange a roaming eSIM or a VPN before you arrive so that you can use WhatsApp and Google Maps, which are otherwise blocked in mainland China; the details are in our guide to the internet in China.
  • Carry the hotel's card in Chinese, and a light folding pushchair (most metro stations have lifts, but the stations are large).
  • Young children enter most sights free and school-age children at half price (the rule goes by age or height, depending on the sight).

Frequently asked questions

Is Beijing safe for families?
Yes, crime rates are very low and the streets and the metro are safe at night. The biggest challenge is the language, which an Arabic-speaking guide and translation apps solve.

How many days are enough for Beijing with children?
Four days cover the essentials at a comfortable pace; add a fifth day if you want Universal.

Can Beijing be combined with Shanghai in one trip?
Yes, the high-speed train links the two cities in about 4.5 hours on the fastest trains. See the 10-day China itinerary for families.

Is halal food available outside the Muslim quarter?
Yes, "Lanzhou" halal noodle restaurants are found in every district of Beijing, and are recognisable by a green sign bearing the Chinese characters for "halal" (qingzhen).
