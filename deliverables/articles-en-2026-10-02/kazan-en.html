Kazan, the capital of the Russian Republic of Tatarstan, is the city where the Arab traveller feels at home: about half its population is Muslim, the call to prayer rises from the Kul Sharif Mosque inside the Kremlin, and halal food is on every street. It is an hour and a half by air from Moscow, or an overnight train journey, and it is the smartest addition to a tourism in Russia itinerary for anyone who wants something different from Moscow and St Petersburg.

Why Kazan?

  • An Islamic city in the heart of Russia: the Kul Sharif Mosque, historic mosques in the Old Tatar Quarter, and a history going back a thousand years.
  • Halal food everywhere: Tatar cuisine (echpochmak, chak-chak, belish) is all halal.
  • Cheaper than Moscow: hotels and restaurants cost noticeably less.
  • Good for families: a large aquarium, a water park and a long embankment on the Volga.

The best time to visit Kazan

May to September is best (18–28°C), and June is the season of the Tatar festival of Sabantuy, with its folk contests. Winter is snowy and beautiful but cold (often -10°C and below), and suits those who want to give the children a taste of the snow.

The main sights of Kazan

The Kazan Kremlin and the Kul Sharif Mosque

A white fortress on the UNESCO World Heritage list, and inside it one of the largest mosques in Russia, with its blue dome and four tall minarets, and next to it the leaning Söyembikä Tower. Entry to the Kremlin is free.

Bauman Street and the Old Tatar Quarter

The main pedestrian street, with its restaurants and cafes, and the Old Tatar Quarter with its colourful wooden houses and historic mosques such as the Marjani Mosque.

The Temple of All Religions and the town of Sviyazhsk

An unusual building that brings together the symbols of the world's religions, and the historic island town of Sviyazhsk, an hour away by car, or by boat in summer.

The Kremlin Embankment and the Family Centre

A promenade on the Kazanka River with a view of the Kremlin, and the Family Centre building, shaped like a giant cauldron, with a viewing platform.

Ancient Bolgar

A day trip to the archaeological city of Bolgar on the Volga, where the ancestors of the Tatars embraced Islam in 922, and which holds the largest printed Quran in the world. It is the most important visit for the Muslim traveller.

A suggested 3-day itinerary

  • Day 1: the Kremlin and the Kul Sharif Mosque, Bauman Street, a Tatar dinner.
  • Day 2: the Old Tatar Quarter, the Temple of All Religions, the embankment and the Family Centre in the evening.
  • Day 3: a day trip to Bolgar (or Sviyazhsk), and the return to Moscow in the evening.

Practical tips

  • The flight from Moscow to Kazan takes an hour and a half, with many departures a day, or there is a comfortable overnight train with family compartments.
  • Entry rules are the same as for the rest of Russia, with no extra procedures: citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Oman enter visa-free, and citizens of Kuwait and Bahrain use the e-visa. See the Russia visa guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kazan suitable for conservative families?

Yes. It is the most suitable city in Russia for the Muslim traveller, with halal food and mosques in every neighbourhood.

How many days are enough?

Two days for the city, and three with the Bolgar trip.

Do people speak Arabic?

Some mosque imams and tourism workers know Arabic, and our Arabic-speaking guide accompanies you throughout the visit.

Can it be visited in winter?

Yes, but with heavy clothing; the Kremlin under snow is an unforgettable sight.
