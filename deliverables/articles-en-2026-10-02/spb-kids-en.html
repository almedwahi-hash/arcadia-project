Saint Petersburg is a city of palaces and canals, but it is also a city that families love: interactive museums, one of the largest aquariums in Russia, fountain gardens and amusement islands inside the city. If you are planning a trip to Russia with children, these are the ten best experiences in Saint Petersburg, arranged by age, with a suggested 4-day program.

The best time to visit with children

From mid-May to August: the Peterhof fountains are running, the days are long, and the white nights in June are an unforgettable experience. September is quiet and cheaper. Winter is cold, but the city is lit up and the museums are almost empty, and it is the choice for those who love snow (see winter tourism in Russia).

1. Peterhof Palace and its fountains

More than 150 fountains in gardens on the shore of the Gulf, among them the "trick fountains" that spray water without warning and make children laugh. Go by hydrofoil from the city centre; the ride itself is part of the fun. Set aside at least half a day.

2. The Hermitage, the children's way

The Hermitage is enormous, so do not try to see all of it. Choose a short route: the Grand Staircase, the Throne Room, the golden Peacock Clock (it runs at set times) and the Egyptian galleries. Two hours are enough with children, and book tickets in advance to avoid the queue.

3. The Saint Petersburg Oceanarium

The first public aquarium in Russia and still one of the largest, with an underwater tunnel and seal and shark shows. It is inside a shopping centre, which makes it ideal for a rainy day.

4. Hare Island and the Peter and Paul Fortress

The fortress walls, the cannons and the golden spire, and at exactly midday the traditional cannon shot is fired. In summer children play on the sandy beach inside the fortress, and from the wall you can see the Hermitage across the river.

5. A canal cruise by boat

One hour along the canals and under the bridges gives children a sense of the whole city without walking. During the white nights there are cruises to watch the bridges being raised after midnight (for older children).

6. The Space Museum and LabyrinthUm

The Space Museum inside the Peter and Paul Fortress displays real rocket engines and spacecraft exhibits. LabyrinthUm is an interactive science museum where touching everything is allowed; an excellent choice for ages 5–12.

7. Divo Ostrov and Krestovsky Island

An amusement park inside a large park on Krestovsky Island, close to the metro, with bikes and boats for hire and a lake.

8. The Catherine Palace and the Amber Room (Pushkin)

About 30 kilometres away, the famous blue palace and its vast park. Children love the park more than the halls, so plan a stroll there. Book timed tickets in advance.

9. The cathedrals from a different angle

The Church of the Saviour on Spilled Blood, with its colourful mosaics, dazzles children, and the climb to the dome of Saint Isaac's Cathedral (262 steps) gives the most beautiful view over the city.

10. Shopping and sweets on Nevsky Prospekt

The historic Yeliseyev store, with its sweets and the mechanical displays in its windows, then the cafes serving Russian pancakes (blini). Halal restaurants are available near the Saint Petersburg Mosque and in the city centre.

A suggested 4-day program

  • Day 1: the Hermitage (short route), Palace Square, a canal cruise, Nevsky Prospekt.
  • Day 2: Peterhof by hydrofoil, returning to the city centre in the evening.
  • Day 3: the Peter and Paul Fortress and the Space Museum, the midday cannon shot, the Oceanarium in the afternoon.
  • Day 4: the Catherine Palace in the morning, the Church of the Saviour on Spilled Blood, then Divo Ostrov or the climb to the dome of Saint Isaac's.

If your trip includes Moscow, combine this program with Moscow with kids; the high-speed Sapsan train links the two cities in about 4 hours.

Tips for families

  • Check the Russia visa rules before you book: citizens of Saudi Arabia (since 11 May 2026), the UAE, Qatar and Oman enter visa-free for up to 90 days, while citizens of Kuwait and Bahrain use the e-visa.
  • Many museums admit young children free and school-age children at a discount (the age limits differ from one museum to another); carry their passports.
  • Advance booking is essential for the Hermitage, the Catherine Palace and Peterhof in summer.
  • Foreign Visa and Mastercard cards do not work in Russia; carry cash (roubles) or a UnionPay/Mir card, or rely on Arcadia's arrangements.

Frequently asked questions

How many days are enough for Saint Petersburg with children?
Four days are comfortable; three days are possible if you drop the Catherine Palace. See also the comprehensive Saint Petersburg guide.

Is the city suitable for a pushchair?
The centre is flat and its pavements are good, but the metro is very deep and its escalators are long; use a private car to get around with little ones.

When do the Peterhof fountains run?
Usually from late April to mid-October, daily from morning until evening.

Is halal food available?
Yes, halal Uzbek, Caucasian and Turkish restaurants are widespread in the city centre.
