Astana, the capital of Kazakhstan, is a young city built from scratch on the banks of the Ishim River in just thirty years, which has given it a futuristic architecture unlike that of any other capital in Central Asia. For anyone visiting Kazakhstan for the first time, Astana is the ideal two- or three-day addition after Almaty and its mountains: a clean, orderly and safe city, with mosques that are among the largest in the region and shopping malls that shelter visitors from the winter cold and the summer heat. This guide explains when to visit, what to see, how much time and budget you need, and how to combine Astana with Almaty in a single trip.

Why visit Astana?

  • Unique architecture: Baiterek Tower, the Khan Shatyr tent, the pyramid-shaped Palace of Peace and Reconciliation, and the Expo 2017 site with its giant glass sphere.
  • A modern Muslim city: the Hazrat Sultan Mosque and the Astana Grand Mosque, the largest mosques in Central Asia, and halal food everywhere.
  • Family-friendly: spacious parks, an aquarium, interactive museums, and short distances between the sights.
  • Close to Almaty: an hour and a half by plane or one night on the fast train, so a single trip covers both nature and the modern city.

Best time to visit Astana

Astana is one of the coldest capitals in the world in winter, so the timing of a visit is different from Almaty:

  • May to September: the best period. Temperatures of 18–28°C, long days, and festivals and events along the riverbanks.
  • October and April: acceptable with a heavy jacket, and prices are lower.
  • November to March: temperatures drop as low as minus 25°C. A visit is possible for those who want to experience real snow, but activities move indoors.

Top sights in Astana

Baiterek Tower

The symbol of the city, 105 metres high, with an observation deck at 97 metres. From its glass top you can see the whole of the capital's main axis. The best time to go up is an hour before sunset.

Khan Shatyr

The largest transparent tent in the world, with a shopping mall, restaurants and an artificial beach inside, with sand from the Maldives and summer temperatures all year round. It is ideal for children in winter.

Hazrat Sultan Mosque and Astana Grand Mosque

Hazrat Sultan, with its white domes, stands next to the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation, while the Astana Grand Mosque, which opened in 2022, is the largest in Central Asia. Both welcome visitors outside prayer times.

Palace of Peace and Reconciliation

A glass pyramid designed by Norman Foster, housing an opera hall and a hall for interfaith meetings. A visit with a guide reveals the story of how the capital was built.

The Expo site and the Museum of the Future

The Nur Alem glass sphere has eight floors that present the future of energy in an interactive way, well suited to children and teenagers.

Ishim River embankment

A long promenade with illuminated bridges, pleasure boats in summer, and family cafés on both banks.

A suggested two-day itinerary in Astana

Day one: Baiterek Tower in the morning, the Hazrat Sultan Mosque, lunch at a traditional Kazakh restaurant, then the Palace of Peace and the Expo site in the afternoon, finishing on the river embankment.

Day two: the Astana Grand Mosque, the National Museum, Khan Shatyr for lunch, shopping and playtime for the children, and the evening at the Mega Silk Way mall or on a boat trip along the river.

With a third day you can add Burabay National Park, two and a half hours away by car: lakes, pine forests and strangely shaped rocks.

How much does a visit to Astana cost?

Hotels are slightly more expensive than in Almaty: 4-star hotels cost $80–120 a night, and 5-star hotels start from $150. A family meal in a good restaurant costs $30–50. A private car with a driver and an Arabic-speaking guide for a full day starts from $120. Two days for a family of four cost roughly $600–900, excluding flights. Practical tips

  • The international airport is 15 kilometres from the city centre; being met by a private car is more comfortable than taking a taxi.
  • Distances between the sights on the left bank are short, but the wind is strong, so carry a jacket even in summer.
  • Card payment is available almost everywhere, and exchange offices in the city give better rates than the airport.
  • For families with children: Khan Shatyr, the aquarium and the zoo.

Frequently asked questions

Is one day enough for Astana?

One day is enough for the main sights (Baiterek, Hazrat Sultan, Khan Shatyr), but two days are more comfortable for families.

Is Astana cold in summer?

No. Summer is mild, between 18 and 28°C, but the wind is strong, so a light jacket is enough.

How do I travel between Almaty and Astana?

By domestic flight, an hour and a half and several times a day, or by fast overnight train, which takes about 13 hours.
