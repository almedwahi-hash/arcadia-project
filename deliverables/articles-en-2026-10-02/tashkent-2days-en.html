Tashkent is the gateway to Uzbekistan and the arrival point for every trip from the Gulf, yet many travellers pass through it quickly on their way to Samarkand. The truth is that the capital deserves two full days: one of the oldest Qurans in the world, some of the most beautiful metro stations in Asia, a vast covered bazaar, and the Chimgan mountains about an hour and a half away. This guide gives you a ready-made two-day program with prices and tips.

When to visit Tashkent

April, May, September and October are the ideal months (18–28°C). Summer is hot (35–40°C), so tours are arranged for the morning and evening, and winter is mildly cold, with skiing in Chimgan from December to February.

Day 1: the old city and its history

The Hazrati Imam complex

The spiritual heart of the city: the Hazrati Imam Mosque with its minarets, the Barak-Khan Madrasah and the Muyi Muborak Library. The Quran of Caliph Uthman ibn Affan, one of the oldest Qurans in the world, written in Kufic script on parchment, was moved in November 2025 from the library to the new Center for Islamic Civilization next to the complex. Open to visitors daily.

Chorsu Bazaar

A giant turquoise dome, and beneath it one of the largest markets in Central Asia: dried fruit, nuts, spices, tandoor bread and sections for handicrafts. Try the plov in the popular restaurants next door.

Kukeldash Madrasah and the Khoja Ahrar Mosque

A few steps from the bazaar: a 16th-century madrasah that is still in use, and the historic Friday mosque.

Evening: Amir Temur Square

The statue of Tamerlane, the Hotel Uzbekistan with its Soviet facade, and the pedestrian Sayilgoh Street with its cafes.

Day 2: the modern city and nature

The Tashkent metro

Opened in 1977, and its Kosmonavtlar, Alisher Navoi and Mustaqillik Maydoni stations are considered among the most beautiful in the world. A single ticket is enough for an hour-long tour between the stations.

The Plov Centre

Besh Qozon: plov is cooked in giant cauldrons in front of the visitors and served from late morning until it runs out, usually by early afternoon, so arrive before 1 pm.

The Chimgan mountains and Lake Charvak

About an hour and a half by car: the Chimgan cable car, a view over the turquoise lake, the Charvak beaches in summer for swimming and boating, and skiing in winter at the Amirsoy resort. Ideal for families.

Evening: Magic City and Tashkent City Park

A modern entertainment complex with dancing fountains and a children's amusement park, or the vast Tashkent City Park.

Practical tips

  • Visa-free entry for citizens of the Gulf states for 30 days.
  • Tashkent International Airport is 15 minutes from the centre; a private-car welcome is included in our program.
  • A Ucell or Beeline SIM card from the airport, and card payment is available in hotels and the larger restaurants.
  • Local food is halal, there are mosques in every district, and the best known is the modern white Minor Mosque.
  • Modest dress is appropriate in the mosques and the historic madrasahs.

Frequently asked questions

Is one day in Tashkent enough?

One day covers Hazrati Imam, Chorsu and the metro, but the second day for Chimgan is worth it.

Is Tashkent suitable for children?

Yes: Magic City, the Chimgan cable car, and Lake Charvak in summer.

How do I get to Samarkand?

The Afrosiyob train (just over two hours), which is booked in advance, and we handle the booking.

What is the currency?

The Uzbek som; exchange money at the banks or the exchange offices in the city.
