Tourism in Poland for families suits a family that wants historic cities, mountain scenery and well-organised travel by train or car. The best plan does not try to cover every city; it picks a clear axis such as Warsaw, Krakow and Zakopane, with lighter days for the children and realistic time for transfers.

Is Poland suitable for families?

Yes, especially for those who prefer parks, squares, interactive museums and mountains. However, the old pavements in some historic centres can be hard work with a pushchair, and winter calls for warm clothing and an indoor plan. Check the hotel lift, the room size and the distance to transport before you book.

The best cities for families

  • Warsaw: a practical starting point for flights, combining the Old Town, parks and museums.
  • Krakow: a historic centre that is easy to walk, with Wawel, Kazimierz and the Vistula River.
  • Zakopane: nature, mountains, cable cars and seasonal activities, all heavily dependent on the weather.
  • Gdańsk: a waterfront and an old town, but it adds a long distance from southern Poland.

A suggested 8-night family itinerary

  1. Two nights in Warsaw, with a light arrival day.
  2. Three nights in Krakow for the city and a nearby family-friendly excursion.
  3. Three nights in Zakopane for nature and rest.

The order can be reversed depending on your flights. With young children, do not turn a transfer day into a full sightseeing day, and do not add Gdańsk to this route unless you extend the trip.

Warsaw with children

Combine the Old Town and the Royal Route in one day, and keep the parks day or the museum day separate. Some museums require advance booking, so check the suitable age, the language and the entry time on the official website. See the Warsaw travel guide.

Krakow for families

The Main Market Square, Planty Park, Wawel Hill and the bank of the Vistula make a comfortable route. Children may enjoy the Wawel Dragon trail, while tours of the mines or of historic sites outside the city need to be weighed against age and duration. Read the Krakow guide.

Zakopane and the mountains

Choose one main mountain activity per day, and keep an alternative for fog, rain or wind. Gubałówka is easier than long mountain trails, while the national park has rules and trail conditions that must be checked. See the Zakopane guide and the resorts of southern Poland.

Getting between cities

The train works well between Warsaw and Krakow, while a car or organised transfer is more flexible with luggage and a child when heading to Zakopane. Compare door-to-door times, and book the seat and passenger name according to the transport company's requirements. Within the city, use public transport or an app-based car when needed.

Family-friendly accommodation

  • Confirm the bed type, the floor area and the number of bathrooms in writing.
  • Do not assume that a "family room" means two connecting rooms.
  • Choose to be near transport or the centre according to your itinerary, not just the name of the district.
  • In Zakopane, check how difficult the road and the parking are in snow.
  • A serviced apartment is useful for longer stays, but check the reception, cleaning and lift.

Food and special requirements

There is a wide range of options in the big cities, but halal or allergen-free food must be confirmed with the establishment and the specific branch. Keep your requirements written down in Polish or English, and do not rely on an old menu or a single review.

Trip budget

The price varies with the season, the flights, the room size, private transport and the number of cities. Ask for a quote that sets out the nights, the hotel, breakfast, the car, the tickets and the cancellation policy. See the cost of a Poland trip for families and the best time for family travel.

Travel documents for the family

Each child needs a separate travel document, and the border authorities or the airline may ask for additional documents depending on nationality, custody and the route of the trip. Do not issue non-refundable tickets before checking officially.

Entry documents: Poland is part of the Schengen area, and the requirements differ according to the traveller's nationality and residence status. Check the official European Union portal and your airline before paying. Arcadia does not issue or process visas.

The best activities for children in each city

  • Warsaw: the Copernicus Science Centre (book in advance), Łazienki Park and its peacocks, the zoo, and the viewing terrace of the Palace of Culture.
  • Krakow: the fire-breathing Wawel Dragon, the Wieliczka mine with its children's route, Energylandia (40 minutes), and a boat trip on the river.
  • Zakopane: the Gubałówka funicular and its summer toboggan run, the horse-drawn carriage to Morskie Oko, the Termy Bukovina thermal baths, and ski school in winter.
  • Gdańsk (in summer): Sopot beach and the wooden pier, Malbork Castle, and the children's museum in the Old Town.

Frequently asked questions

How many days are enough for a family in Poland?

Seven to nine nights suit the Warsaw, Krakow and Zakopane axis without rushing.

Do I need a car for the whole trip?

Mostly not inside the cities; a car or transfer service is more useful in the south and the mountains.

Is winter suitable for children?

It suits a family that is prepared for the cold, with an indoor programme, longer travel times and a back-up plan for the weather.
