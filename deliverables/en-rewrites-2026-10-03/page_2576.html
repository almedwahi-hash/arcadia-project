The main tourist cities of Uzbekistan are the four Silk Road cities: Tashkent, the capital, then Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva, with Shakhrisabz, Termez and the Fergana Valley for travellers who have more time. The high-speed train keeps the distances short, and each city has its own character: a modern capital, a city of blue domes, an old town you can cover entirely on foot, and an open-air museum inside its walls. This guide introduces each city, what to see there, how many nights it needs and how to reach it, with links to the detailed guides.

1. Tashkent: the capital and the way in

The largest city in Central Asia, with around 3 million people, and the arrival point for flights from the Gulf, about four hours away. It is a modern city of wide avenues, parks and a decorated metro. The main sights are the Hazrati Imam complex, the Uthman Quran (now displayed in the Centre of Islamic Civilisation next to the complex), the domed Chorsu Bazaar, the TV Tower, the Magic City amusement park and the new Tashkent City district. Two nights at the start of the trip and one at the end are enough. See Tashkent in two days and the best hotels in Tashkent.

2. Samarkand: jewel of the Silk Road

The best-known city in Uzbekistan and a World Heritage Site. Registan Square, with its three madrasas, is the most famous image of Central Asia. Around it are the tiled mausoleums of Shah-i-Zinda, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque, the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum of Tamerlane, the Ulugh Beg Observatory and Siyob Bazaar. The Imam al-Bukhari complex, the most important religious visit of the trip, is 25 kilometres away. The Afrosiyob train from Tashkent takes two hours. Allow two to three nights. Details are in Samarkand, capital of the Silk Road and the Islamic heritage guide to Uzbekistan.

3. Bukhara: the holy city

More than 140 monuments stand in an old town that can be explored entirely on foot: the Kalon minaret and mosque, the Mir-i-Arab Madrasa, the Ark fortress, the Ismail Samani Mausoleum (the oldest standing Islamic building in Central Asia), the Lyabi-Hauz pool and the domed bazaars. Outside the city is the Bahauddin Naqshband complex. The high-speed train from Samarkand takes an hour and a half. Allow two nights. See the Bukhara travel guide and the best hotels in Samarkand and Bukhara.

4. Khiva: the open-air museum inside the walls

The walled city of Itchan Kala, in the west of the country, is the best-preserved old town in Central Asia: the turquoise Kalta Minor minaret, the Islam Khoja minaret, the Tash Hauli palace and the Kunya Ark citadel. You reach it by flying to Urgench (an hour and a half from Tashkent) or by train from Bukhara (about six hours across the desert). Allow one or two nights; it suits trips of eight days or more. Details are in Khiva, city of preserved history.

5. Shakhrisabz: birthplace of Tamerlane

Ninety kilometres from Samarkand over a scenic mountain pass, Shakhrisabz has the remains of the vast Ak-Saray palace and the Dorut Tilovat complex. It is visited as a day trip from Samarkand and is also on the UNESCO list.

6. Termez: city of Imam al-Tirmidhi

In the far south on the Amu Darya river, Termez has the mausoleum of Imam Abu Isa al-Tirmidhi, compiler of the Sunan, the mausoleum of al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi, and rare Buddhist and Islamic remains. It is an hour's flight from Tashkent and suits an extended religious itinerary.

7. The Fergana Valley: silk and ceramics

A green valley east of Tashkent with Margilan (traditional silk workshops and the mausoleum of Imam al-Marghinani, author of al-Hidaya), Rishtan (blue ceramics) and Kokand (the Khan's palace). It is visited by train from Tashkent over one or two nights.

How do you travel between the cities?

The Afrosiyob high-speed train (250 km/h) links Tashkent with Samarkand and Bukhara, with comfortable seats and a restaurant car; tickets should be booked in advance in season. A private car with a driver and an Arabic-speaking guide suits the day trips (Shakhrisabz, the Imam al-Bukhari complex, Chimgan), and domestic flights serve Khiva and Termez. Practical details are in Uzbekistan for Saudi travellers.

Best time to visit the cities of Uzbekistan

April to May and September to October are the two best seasons (20–28°C). Summer is very hot in Bukhara and Khiva (40°C), and winter is cold, quiet and cheaper. See the best time to visit Uzbekistan.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the most beautiful city in Uzbekistan?
Samarkand for photographs and architecture, Bukhara for its old-town atmosphere, and Khiva for a completely preserved city.

Are 5 days enough?
Yes, for Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara; adding Khiva needs 8 days.

Are the cities suitable for children?
Yes; see Uzbekistan with kids.

Is the food halal in every city?
Yes. Uzbekistan is a Muslim country and halal food is the norm everywhere.
