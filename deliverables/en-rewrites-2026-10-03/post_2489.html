This Poland travel guide helps you choose your cities, length of stay, season, accommodation and transport before you book. Poland is a large country (only slightly smaller than Germany in area) and its main cities are far apart, so the most important decision is: how many cities, and in what order? That is what we answer here. For the day-by-day schedule use the 7-day Poland itinerary, and for prices see the cost of travelling in Poland.

How many days does Poland need?

5 nights suit one main city with a day trip (Warsaw + the Wieliczka mine, for example, or Krakow + Zakopane). 7 to 8 nights is the most common choice for families: Warsaw, Krakow and Zakopane. 10 nights let you add Gdańsk on the coast in summer, or Wrocław. We do not recommend fewer than two nights in any city; changing hotel every night wears children out and wastes half a day on every transfer.

Which cities should you choose?

  • Warsaw: the capital, with an international airport, shopping, interactive museums and the Old Town. A good point of entry.
  • Krakow: the most beautiful historically and walkable, with the salt mine, Energylandia and Zakopane nearby. Not to be missed.
  • Zakopane: mountains, chalets and the cable car in summer, skiing in winter.
  • Gdańsk: the Baltic Sea and its beaches, for July and August.
  • Wrocław: the city of dwarfs and bridges, an excellent stop between Warsaw and Krakow on a longer trip.

The best time to visit

From mid-May to mid-September for mild weather (18–26°) and long days. December for the Christmas markets, and January and February for snow in Zakopane. Avoid November and March. The full month-by-month table is in the best time to visit Poland.

Getting there and airports

Warsaw Chopin Airport (WAW) is the main gateway, with direct flights from Doha (Qatar Airways), Dubai (Emirates and flydubai), Riyadh (LOT Polish Airlines) and Abu Dhabi; the flight takes 6 to 7 hours. Krakow (KRK) is reached via Doha, Dubai or Istanbul, and is ideal for those starting in the south. You can fly into Warsaw and out of Krakow (an open-jaw ticket) to save a full day of travelling back.

Travelling between cities

Fast PKP Intercity trains: Warsaw–Krakow 2.5 hours, Warsaw–Gdańsk 3 hours, Krakow–Wrocław 3 hours; book two weeks ahead through the official website or through us, with fares of 15–35 dollars per person in second class and family seating on some services. Krakow–Zakopane is easier by car (two hours). Within the cities: Warsaw's trams and metro are excellent, and the Uber and Bolt apps are cheap. For families with young children, a private car with an Arabic-speaking driver for the whole trip is the most comfortable option and costs 120–160 dollars a day.

Choosing a hotel

In Warsaw, choose the city centre (Śródmieście) or somewhere near the Old Town; in Krakow, inside the Old Town or the Kazimierz district; in Zakopane, a wooden chalet or a hotel with a thermal pool. Four-star hotels cost 90–150 dollars per room per night, and serviced apartments are an economical choice for large families. Details are in the best hotels and chalets in Poland.

Budget and payment

The currency is the zloty (about 4 zloty to the dollar). Cards are accepted almost everywhere, even at kiosks, so there is no need for much cash. A meal for a family of four in a good restaurant costs 40–60 dollars, attraction tickets 5–15 dollars per person, and children under 7 go free in most museums and on public transport. A week's budget for a family of four (excluding flights): 2,600–3,900 dollars in summer.

Food and halal options

Warsaw and Krakow have dozens of halal restaurants (Turkish, Arab, Indian and Middle Eastern), and chains such as Kebab King are widespread. In Zakopane and Gdańsk the options are fewer, but seafood and vegetarian restaurants are available, and hotels will cook halal food on advance request. A selected list is in Warsaw's halal restaurants.

A weather plan and day trips

Keep an indoor alternative for every day (a water park, a science museum, a shopping mall), because summer showers are short but frequent. Spread the long day trips (Wieliczka, Energylandia, Morskie Oko) across separate days, and do not combine two big activities in one day with children under 8.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a visa?
Most Gulf nationals do: a Schengen visa from the Polish embassy or a VFS centre (UAE citizens are exempt for short stays); start 6 to 8 weeks ahead in summer. Details are in the Poland visa guide.

Is English widely spoken?
In hotels, restaurants and at the sights, yes, especially among young people. An Arabic-speaking guide takes care of the rest.

Is Poland safe for families?
Yes, it is one of the safest countries in Europe, and the rate of crime against tourists is very low.

Which is the better place to start: Warsaw or Krakow?
For a first trip, fly into Warsaw and finish with Krakow and Zakopane; that way the most beautiful part comes at the end.
