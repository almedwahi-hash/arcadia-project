St Petersburg in 3 days calls for an itinerary that divides the city between the historic centre, one major museum and a palace excursion. This page is devoted to the short schedule, while the full guide covers the city in greater depth.

Are three days enough?

They are enough for the main experiences, provided you arrive before the programme starts and leave after it ends. Four or five nights are better during the White Nights season, or if you are adding Kronstadt and several museums.

Day one: Nevsky Prospekt and the historic heart

Start on Nevsky Prospekt and the streets around it, then see Kazan Cathedral, the Church of the Saviour on Spilled Blood and Palace Square from the outside. Leave time for a walk along the canals, and do not book a major museum on your arrival day.

Day two: the Hermitage and the canals

Set aside a clear block of time for the Hermitage and choose your sections in advance; the museum is far too large to see in full in a single visit. Afterwards, make a canal cruise or a walk your lighter activity, with a back-up plan in case of rain.

Day three: Peterhof

Peterhof is a vast complex of gardens and palaces, and the fountain experience varies with the season and operating schedule. It can be reached in several ways, including the fast boat in season; the official portal notes that the water route links the centre with the Lower Park. See the official route.

A back-up for rain or closures

Keep an indoor museum or a palace in the city as an alternative, and do not make all your tickets for the same day non-changeable. The city portal publishes family-friendly places and activities and seasonal updates: Visit Petersburg.

The best area to stay

Staying near Nevsky Prospekt or a central metro station cuts travelling time, but check the noise and whether there is a lift, as some buildings are historic. For a family, confirm the bed types, the room size and access with luggage.

Getting around the city

The metro and walking both work well, while the boat is a seasonal experience rather than a dependable alternative. When booking a car, mention your luggage, the child seat and the meeting point, especially near the large squares and the piers.

The White Nights

The period of long daylight is beautiful but busy, and prices and hotel availability shift. Book early, and do not count on a bridge-opening time or an event without checking on the day itself. Read the White Nights guide.

Moscow and St Petersburg in one trip

The two can be linked by train or by air. Allow time for the station and the luggage, and do not book an activity straight after arrival. For broader coverage, see the complete St Petersburg guide and the cost of a family trip to Russia.

Clothing and weather

The city is damp and changeable, and rain and cold wind are possible in summer. Take a rainproof layer and comfortable shoes, and check the weather month by month.

The Hermitage in two hours: a suggested route for families

The Hermitage is enormous (three million items) and cannot be seen in a day. The route that works with children: the grand Jordan Staircase → the throne rooms → the golden Peacock Clock (it runs on Wednesdays and Fridays at 7 pm) → the Egyptian Hall with its mummies → the Rembrandt paintings → out onto Palace Square. Book timed tickets online and go in through the main entrance; children under 7 enter free. In the afternoon, take a canal boat tour (one hour) to see the whole city without walking.

Peterhof in detail

Go by the fast "hydrofoil" boat from the Hermitage pier (35 minutes, about 15 dollars per person) to arrive directly at the Lower Park, with its 150 fountains, the Grand Cascade and its golden statues, and the "trick fountains" that spray the children. The fountains run from late April to mid-October, from 10 am. Set aside half a day and have lunch at the restaurants in the park; there is no need to go inside the Grand Palace with young children, as the park is the experience.

What should we add if we stay a fourth day?

The Catherine Palace and the Amber Room in Pushkin (30 km, timed booking), or the Peter and Paul Fortress with the space museum and the midday cannon shot, or the Oceanarium, the largest aquarium in Russia, for a rainy day. Frequently asked questions

Should I put Peterhof on the arrival day?

It is best not to, as it takes time and a transfer and depends on the season.

Is the Hermitage suitable for children?

Yes, with a short, focused visit rather than an attempt to cover every hall.

Do the boats run all year round?

No; they operate seasonally and are affected by the weather, so check the official source.
