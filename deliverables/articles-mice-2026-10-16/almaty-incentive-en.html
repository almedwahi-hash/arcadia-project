An incentive trip is the strongest reward a company can give its team: a group journey that combines recognition, leisure and team building in a few days employees remember for years. Almaty has become one of the most requested destinations for these trips from the Gulf and Europe: mountains and ski resorts 30 minutes from city hotels, direct flights of four to five hours from the Gulf, lower costs than Europe and halal food everywhere. This guide explains how to plan the trip step by step, what determines its cost and which mistakes spoil it.

Step 1: Define the purpose before anything else

A trip to reward the top 30 salespeople differs from one that merges two teams after a company merger, and from one that combines the annual management meeting with leisure days. The purpose decides everything after it: the ratio of working days to leisure days, the hotel level, the type of activities and whether family members are invited. Write the purpose in one sentence and share it with your organiser.

Step 2: Choose the season and the length

  • May to September: mountains and lakes, outdoor activities, the best photos. The most requested season.
  • December to March: skiing and snow activities on Shymbulak; a spectacular experience for teams from hot countries, with alternatives for non-skiers.
  • Ideal length: three to four nights. Less is not enough for the mountains, more raises the cost without much added effect.

Step 3: Build the programme on a "work, then reward" rhythm

The programme that always works: a calm arrival day with a welcome dinner, then a day of sessions or workshops in the hotel's conference room in the morning with a light activity in the afternoon, then a full day in the mountains (Shymbulak and Medeu cable car, lunch at 2,200 metres, team challenges), then a day at Big Almaty Lake or Charyn Canyon ending with a gala dinner and awards. Step 4: Choose the hotel by company culture, not stars alone

Almaty has good options for every budget: five-star hotels such as Swissôtel Almaty, Rixos Almaty and InterContinental Almaty for companies that want luxury and VIP services, and excellent four-star hotels such as Novotel Almaty City Center, Kazakhstan Hotel and Holiday Inn Almaty for companies that prefer to spend the difference on activities and the gala dinner. What matters is a hall that fits your numbers and a location close to the city centre. See the best hotels in Almaty 2026.

Step 5: Understand what drives the cost

There is no fixed per-person price for incentive trips, because five factors change it considerably:

  • Group size: the coach, guide and coordinator are fixed costs shared by participants; 60 people pay clearly less per head than 15.
  • Hotel category and room type: single or shared rooms, four or five stars.
  • Season: July, August and the year-end holidays are more expensive and less available.
  • Programme: a hall with simultaneous interpretation and a gala dinner with entertainment differ from a simple meeting and a restaurant dinner.
  • Extras: gifts, professional photography, branded signage, travel insurance.

Five mistakes that spoil an incentive trip

  1. An overloaded programme: three excursions a day exhaust the team; one main activity per day and free evenings.
  2. Ignoring those who do not ski or walk far: every activity needs a comfortable alternative in the same place.
  3. A distant hotel: a small saving on rooms is lost in hours on the coach.
  4. A closing dinner without awards: the public moment of recognition is what justifies the trip to management and staff.
  5. Dealing with ten suppliers: hotel, hall, transport and restaurants each on its own contract; one ground partner carries the whole responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

Do our employees need visas?

Gulf citizens enter Kazakhstan visa-free, as do most European nationalities for short stays. For other nationalities we provide the bookings and invitation letters needed; visa issuance is not part of our services.

Can family members join?

Yes. We arrange a parallel programme for families during the working sessions, then shared days in the mountains.

What is the minimum group size?

There is none; we have organised ten-person management meetings and groups of several hundred, and the programme and pricing change with the size.
