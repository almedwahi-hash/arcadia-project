Families often ask whether to hire just a private driver or book a complete tour package. Both are valid, and we sell both. The right answer depends on the destination, the ages of your children and how much planning you want to do yourself. Here is an honest comparison from a company that arranges both every week.

Private driver only

You book your own flights and hotels, and we provide a car with a driver for the days you choose. It is cheaper on paper (roughly 90–140 USD per day for a car and driver in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan or Poland, more in Russia) and very flexible: you decide each morning where to go.

The trade-offs: you research the sights, buy tickets, find halal restaurants and plan the route yourself. Drivers are not guides; most speak the local language and some English, not Arabic. Popular attractions (the Hermitage, Wieliczka salt mine, the Kolsai lakes in high season) need advance tickets or permits that are easy to miss. With young children, a wasted day because of a closed road or a sold-out ticket is expensive.

Full tour package

Hotels, transport, an Arabic- or English-speaking guide, pre-booked tickets and a day-by-day itinerary, all organised in advance with one fixed price and a local office on WhatsApp during the trip. It costs more per day than a driver alone (a family of four typically pays 2,500–3,800 USD for a week in Kazakhstan, see how much a family trip costs), but the price includes things you would otherwise pay separately: tickets, guide, airport transfers and the planning time.

The trade-off is less spontaneity: the days are planned. In practice our programs keep free afternoons and can be changed on the spot; the plan is a safety net, not a cage.

Which suits families?

  • First visit to the destination, children under 10, or a trip of 7 days or more: the full package. The guide handles language, tickets, prayer times and halal meals, and the parents actually rest.
  • Repeat visitors who know the city, teenagers, or a short city break of 3–4 days: a driver only, or a driver plus a guide on two key days.
  • Russia and China: the package, because of the language barrier, the ticket system and (in Russia) the payment restrictions on foreign cards.
  • Poland: either works; English is widely spoken and trains are easy. Many families take a driver plus a hotel package from us.

A middle option that works well: we book the hotels and a driver for the whole stay, and add a guide only for the big days (the Great Wall, Samarkand, the Hermitage). You get most of the comfort at a lower price.

Questions to ask before choosing a driver-only option

  • Does the driver speak English or Arabic, and does the price include fuel, parking and the driver's meals?
  • Is the car a 4x4 for mountain roads (Kazakhstan) and does it have child seats?
  • Who buys the attraction tickets, and what happens if a site is sold out?
  • Is there a local contact if the driver is late or the plan changes?

Frequently asked questions

Is a package always more expensive?
Per day, yes; in total, often not, because tickets, transfers and guide days are included and bought at local prices.

Can we have a driver only in one city and a package in another?
Yes. Mixed programs are common, for example a package in Samarkand and Bukhara and a driver only in Tashkent.

Do your drivers speak Arabic?
Some do in Almaty and Tashkent; guides always do. Tell us if an Arabic-speaking driver is essential.

Can we change plans during a package tour?
Yes. Swapping a day or adding free time is usually possible on the spot.
