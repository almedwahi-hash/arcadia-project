A few common mistakes can spoil a family trip, and most of them happen before departure, at the booking stage. After organising hundreds of family tours to Kazakhstan, Russia, Poland, Uzbekistan and China, these are the mistakes we see most often, and how to avoid each one.

1. Overpacking the schedule

Three cities in seven days, a museum every morning and a day trip every afternoon exhausts children by day three, and the rest of the trip is spent managing tantrums. The rule that works: one main activity per day, a rest after lunch, and no more than one hotel change every three nights. Fewer places seen well beats many places seen tired. See how to choose the right family tour program.

2. The wrong season

Bukhara in July is 42°C; Astana in January is -20°C; Charyn Canyon and the Kolsai lakes can be closed by snow until late May; Peterhof's fountains do not run in winter. Check the month before you fall in love with a destination, and choose the city or the dates accordingly. Our month-by-month guide: the best time for family travel.

3. Ignoring hidden costs

A low package price often excludes attraction tickets (25 USD per person for the Shymbulak cable car, 30 USD for the Hermitage), airport transfers, the guide's fee on day trips, or breakfast. Ask for a written list of inclusions and exclusions and compare totals, not headline prices. Typical totals are in how much does a family trip cost.

4. No clear itinerary

"Full-day Almaty tour" tells you nothing. A proper program names the sights, the order, the time at each, the restaurant plan and the hotel. Without it you cannot judge whether the day is realistic or whether the hotel is in the right area. Insist on a day-by-day document before paying anything.

5. Booking the wrong hotel location

A beautiful hotel 40 minutes from the centre means 80 minutes a day in traffic with children. In Almaty stay in the centre or near the mountains; in Krakow inside the Old Town; in Samarkand and Bukhara in the historic quarter; in Moscow near a central metro station. Ask for the hotel name and check it on the map.

6. Forgetting visas and documents

GCC citizens enter Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan visa-free, need an e-visa for Russia (about four days) and a Schengen visa for Poland (six to eight weeks in summer). China's rules change often. Start early and carry passports everywhere; Russian and Chinese ticket desks check them.

7. Ignoring children's ages in the plan

Energylandia is wonderful at 8 and pointless at 2; the Terracotta Army fascinates a 12-year-old and bores a 5-year-old; a 6-hour desert drive to Khiva is fine for teenagers, not toddlers. Send every child's age when you request a program so each day is planned for them.

8. Relying on cards and apps that will not work

International Visa and Mastercard do not work in Russia; in China, cash and foreign cards are rarely accepted and Alipay must be set up before arrival; in Uzbekistan bazaars are cash only. Know this before departure, or let the tour company handle local payments.

9. Choosing on price alone

The cheapest quote usually means shared buses, no guide, hotels far from the centre, or no local office to call when something goes wrong. Compare the inclusions, the hotel names, and whether the company has a team in the destination. Read private driver vs full tour package to decide what level of service you actually need.

Frequently asked questions

How many cities should a 7-day family trip include?
Two, with at most one day trip from each. Three cities need ten days.

How early should we book?
Six to eight weeks before summer and Eid holidays, three to four weeks in other seasons.

What if a child gets sick during the trip?
Our local office arranges a doctor or clinic the same day; travel insurance is included in our packages on request.

Can we add free days?
Yes, and we recommend it; free days with the car and driver available cost little and improve the whole trip.
