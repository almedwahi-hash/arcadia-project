History in five minutes

Samarkand was already ancient when Alexander the Great took it in 329 BC; the Silk Road made Bukhara and Samarkand the richest cities between China and the Mediterranean; Genghis Khan destroyed them in 1220; and Timur (Tamerlane) rebuilt Samarkand as his capital in the 1370s, importing artisans from across his empire, which is why the turquoise domes and tilework you see today date from the 14th and 15th centuries. Bukhara later became a centre of Islamic scholarship, home of Imam al-Bukhari's teachers and the Naqshbandi order. Khiva's walls and madrasas are mostly from the 18th and 19th centuries, preserved intact.

Culture and etiquette

Uzbeks are famously hospitable; expect to be invited for tea. Dress modestly at religious sites, remove shoes in mausoleums, and ask before photographing people. Plov is the national dish and Friday is plov day; bread is never thrown away or placed upside down. Tipping is appreciated but not expected.
