Kazakhstan, the world's largest landlocked country, is Central Asia's rising star: modern cities, soaring mountains, turquoise lakes and warm hospitality, four to five hours from the Gulf and visa-free for GCC citizens. Here are the best things to do in Kazakhstan for your 2026 trip, with the practical detail families need: how far, how long, and what suits which ages.

1. Explore Almaty, the cultural capital

The gateway for most visitors: tree-lined avenues, the Green Bazaar with its dried fruit and horse-meat sausage, Panfilov Park and the wooden Zenkov Cathedral, the Kok-Tobe hill by cable car with its small funfair, and the Central State Museum. Almaty also has the best halal restaurants, malls (Mega, Dostyk Plaza, Esentai) and family hotels, which is why most programs use it as the base. See the Almaty guide and the best hotels in Almaty 2026.

2. Big Almaty Lake

A turquoise reservoir at 2,500 metres, 45 minutes from the city, framed by 4,000-metre peaks. It is a drinking-water source, so no swimming, but the viewpoint, the picnic spots and the falcon centre on the way make an easy half-day for any age. Combine with the Kok-Zhailau meadows or the Tian Shan Astronomical Observatory above the lake.

3. Shymbulak ski resort and Medeu

Medeu is the world's highest Olympic skating rink (1,700 m), open in winter for skating; above it the Shymbulak cable car rises in three stages to 3,200 metres, where there is snow even in July and a glacier view. In winter it is Central Asia's best ski resort, with a children's ski school and sledging slopes. Cable-car tickets are about 25 USD per person; go early to avoid queues.

4. Astana, the futuristic capital

Baiterek Tower, the Khan Shatyr tent-shaped mall with its indoor beach, the Hazrat Sultan Mosque (the largest in Central Asia), the Nur Alem sphere from Expo 2017 and the National Museum. A 90-minute flight from Almaty; one or two nights is enough. Summer is pleasant; winter drops below -20°C, so keep winter trips in Almaty. See the National Museum.

5. Charyn Canyon

"Kazakhstan's Grand Canyon", three hours east of Almaty: the Valley of Castles trail (3 km, flat, fine for children from 5) winds between red rock towers to the Charyn River, where lunch is served on the bank. A long but unforgettable day; start by 7 am and use a 4x4 with an experienced driver.

6. Kolsai and Kaindy lakes

Alpine lakes at 1,800–2,000 metres with boat hire and a boardwalk at Kolsai, and the eerie drowned forest of Kaindy, reached by 4x4 or horse. Best combined with Charyn as a two-day trip with a night in a wooden guesthouse in Saty village; see the 10 most important tourist attractions in Kazakhstan.

7. Turkistan and the mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi

A UNESCO-listed 14th-century mausoleum built by Timur, the most important Islamic monument in Kazakhstan, surrounded by the new Karavansaray complex with canals and a flying theatre. Reached by high-speed train from Almaty or a short flight; a good two-night extension for Muslim travellers.

8. Nomad culture: horses, eagles and yurts

Horse riding on the Assy plateau's summer pastures, a golden-eagle hunting demonstration, and dinner in a yurt with dombra music and beshbarmak. These are the experiences children talk about for years; see unique activities in Kazakhstan and traditional dishes of Kazakhstan.

Best time to visit Kazakhstan

Mid-May to mid-September for nature (25–32°C in Almaty, cooler in the mountains, all roads open); December to February for snow and skiing in Almaty. Avoid November and March. See the best time for family travel and Almaty in winter.

Where to stay

Central Almaty (Dostyk Avenue, the Golden Square) for malls and restaurants, or the foothill hotels for views and quiet; 4-star family rooms cost 80–130 USD, 5-star 150–260 USD. In Astana stay on the left bank near Baiterek. Family costs for a week are 2,500–3,800 USD in summer; see the cost of tourism in Kazakhstan.
