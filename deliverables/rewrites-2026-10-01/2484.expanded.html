Nowruz in Uzbekistan: how to experience it

Nowruz, the spring new year on 21 March, is the biggest celebration in the country and a public holiday that stretches over a week. The main squares of Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara fill with folk theatre, traditional costume, wrestling and horse games, while families cook sumalak, a sweet wheat paste stirred all night in giant cauldrons; visitors are invited to take a turn at the pot. Plan the day itself around Registan Square in Samarkand or Navoi Park in Tashkent, book hotels two months ahead, and expect some museums to close on 21 and 22 March.

Other spring festivals

  • Boysun Bahori (April or May): mountain village music and dance in Surkhandarya, a UNESCO-listed tradition; add two days from Termez.
  • Silk and Spices Festival (late May, Bukhara): craft markets, fashion shows and plov cooked in the old-city squares; the most family-friendly festival on the calendar.
  • Tulip season (April): the steppes near Tashkent and the Fergana Valley bloom; combine with the Rishtan ceramics workshops.
