1- Krakow

Krakow is one of Poland's charming tourist cities, located in the south. You are in front of the most beautiful cities in Poland. Krakow is an ancient city whose history extends back to the Middle Ages. It is simply an artistic painting rich in the enchanting colors of nature. The city derived part of its importance from the fact that it was the capital of Poland for several centuries. The city's architecture takes on a Gothic and Baroque character. Places you should not miss in Krakow:

  • Market Square: The square is not just a square to walk around but a journey into the history and heritage of the city. The square's buildings have wonderful historical European architecture that helps you enter the atmosphere of the past with a trip through time without effort. You can enjoy many activities taking place in the square and shop from traditional shops and markets that display handmade products that are ideal for souvenirs.

  • Kazimierz District: Wander through the narrow alleys between buildings with distinctive architecture, or have drinks or a meal in the cafes and restaurants spread around. The Kazimierz neighborhood is an opportunity to learn about the history of the Jews in the region, as the neighborhood is one of the Jewish neighborhoods in the region.

  • Polonia Park: Krakow is rich in natural places and gardens, and Polonia Park is considered one of the distinctive places for those who love to wander in nature and enjoy the green spaces and wonderful flowers. The garden includes an attractive lake and fountains that enrich the charm of the view. The garden also includes children's play areas.

  • Wawel Castle: The castle is located on a high hill and reflects the prestige of the city, whether with its panoramic view  or its design. The castle was a former residence of the Kings of Poland. Visiting the castle and touring its wings is an enjoyable historical journey through the different eras. The castle is surrounded by green scenery and overlooks the Vistula River and Bridge. Do not miss taking souvenir photos when you visit the castle.

2- Warsaw

Warsaw is the current capital of Poland so; It is the most important city in Poland and the architecture of its buildings is a mixture of classical, baroque and modern architecture at the same time. Tourism in Warsaw is not only historical and recreational but also nature. Learn more information about the city of Warsaw, one of Poland's tourist cities, from This link.

There are many landmarks worth visiting in the city of Warsaw, including:

  • The Old City: It is enough to know that it is on the UNESCO list of World Heritage Sites to know its prestigious value. The city was restored and its buildings were painted in a distinctive color to repair what the city suffered as a result of World War II.

  • The Royal Palace: One of the most prominent landmarks of the city, the palace includes amazing royal rooms and halls decorated with paintings and precious antiques. You can visit the palace and learn about the history of Poland by wandering between the different halls. Don't miss enjoying the palace gardens and fountains that transport you to the world of legendary stories. 

  • Warsaw Fortress: The fort is one of the most prominent historical monuments in Warsaw. The fort documents important historical events and visiting it is a great opportunity to learn about the military architecture of the region. The massive walls, powerful towers and the design of the entire fort building amazingly document the architecture of this era. Wander through the palace's courtyards and enjoy its wonderful view of the surrounding green nature.

  • Palace of Culture and Science: The palace dates back to the 1950s. The palace is a gift from the Soviet Union to Poland. The palace is distinguished by its towering height of 231 metres, consisting of 30 floors, and is part of the old city. So; The palace is the tallest building in Warsaw and one of the tallest buildings on the European continent as well. You can imagine the panoramic view from the roof of the palace when you think of this soaring height. Some of the palace's spaces are allocated for cultural performances, art exhibitions, conferences, libraries and theaters, so; You can consider the palace a platform for culture and arts. You can attend one of the palace's cultural events or one of the theatrical performances and document your visit by taking photos.

  • Lazinki Royal Park: The park includes not only wonderful scenes of nature, but also wonderful architecture and artistic sculptures. You can walk around the park on the shaded paths and enjoy the flowers, small lakes, and fountains in an atmosphere of the beauty and majesty of nature. You can get around by walking or cycling, as well as visiting nearby museums.

  • Museums: The city includes several museums worth visiting, including the Warsaw Uprising Museum and the National Museum.

3- Wroclaw

Wroclaw is one of Poland's tourist cities, and some call it the city of dwarves, where you can see more than 600 bronze statues of dwarves in the city squares. The statues commemorate the peaceful protests against the communist regime that were held in the past, and the protesters wore hats that resemble the hats of dwarves in fairy tales. In fact, the matter was not limited to that, but some legendary stories appeared about dwarves who lived within the walls of the ancient city, which was destroyed in the war. Legend has it that people built the modern city and forgot the dwarves inside the old city walls, and then the dwarves began placing bronze statues in the streets at night to remind people of the city's true heritage and the need to revive it.

World War II destroyed about 70% of the city and it is said to have been Hitler's favorite city. When you see pictures of the destroyed city during the war, you will be amazed at how the city was amazingly rebuilt and restored until it returned to its splendor and beauty. You can walk around the city on foot without needing a car, as most of the landmarks are close to each other. You can also visit the Monopol Hotel, where Hitler gave his famous speech from its balcony overlooking the square.

Some of the landmarks you can visit in Wroclaw:

  • Old Main Market and Penitent Bridge: A large shopping center where locals and tourists buy many of their needs. If you visit the city in December, you can enjoy visiting the largest Christmas shopping center. You can also see the Bridge of the Penitents, which is called the Bridge of the Witches. It dates back to the fourteenth century. In the past, those accused of being witches were required to walk on it without barriers. Those who survive are considered witches and are executed immediately, while those who slip or fall are acquitted. The bridge connects the two towers of the Church of Mary Magdalene, and the bridge is linked to many myths and stories.

  • Zoo: The first zoo of its kind in Poland and includes a huge number of animals. The park also includes an exhibition of marine creatures that live on the flats of Africa.

  • The National Palace: It includes a distinguished collection of Silesian and Polish art. A visit to the palace is a journey through time to learn about the arts of the Gothic, Baroque, and Renaissance eras, with the pleasure of viewing modern art exhibits.

  1. Zakopane

In the heart of the Tatra Mountains lies the charming, green city of Zakopane, a land of nature and adventure and one of the most important tourist cities in Poland. Like many mountainous destinations, Zakopane allows you to walk and enjoy nature during the summer and go snowboarding during the winter. 

There are many destinations in Zakopane that are worth visiting, including:

  • Tatra Park: The park combines the peaks of the Tatra Mountains with green valleys in a view that looks like an artistic painting. You can walk around and walk the trails. Enjoy the charming view of the picturesque Moreski Lake, which is embraced by the Tatra Mountains on all sides.

  • Kropoki Street: The vibrant heart of the city with its shops displaying local handicrafts and souvenirs. 

  • Kasprowy Wierch Mountain: You can reach the top of the mountain by riding the cable car and enjoy a legendary view resembling fairy tales and legends. 

  • The Great Waterfall: A global and legendary view where water cascades over mountain rocks and penetrates cracks. Walk around and listen to the sound of the roaring water and feel the spray of waterfall water on your face. These are moments that transport you to another world.

  1. Gdańsk

Gdańsk is a coastal city with a very special taste and is considered the amber capital of the world. Gdańsk is one of the tourist cities in Poland, which at night looks like the atmosphere of a scary movie, as fog prevails in the streets and the sound of music flows through its alleys from a street performer in a scene that is incredibly similar to the atmosphere of the movies. 

There are many places worth visiting in the city of Gdańsk, including:

  • Gdańsk Wooden Crane: It is the largest wooden crane in Europe and dates back to the Middle Ages. The crane was used to load ship cargo as well as repair the masts of large ships.

  • Mariska Street: The street has the largest concentration of amber, which is used in the manufacture of perfumes, incense, and jewelry.  Amber is extracted from the intestines of sperm whales. Most stores offer jewelry pieces that were made from amber, and you can obtain a certificate proving that the piece you purchased is from original amber and listing the specifications of the piece.

  • Mariska Church: Mariska or Mary Church has very great value from an artistic and historical standpoint, as it is the largest Gothic church built of brick or tiles in the world. The church was completed in 1500 and can accommodate 25,000 worshipers. The highest of the church towers can be reached by climbing 400 steps and its summit has a majestic view of the city. The church is distinguished by its colorful window paintings, wooden altar, royal tomb, and astronomical clock. The astronomical clock is very complex and considered the oldest of its kind in Europe. The interior design of the church dates back entirely to the Middle Ages.

  • St Bridget's Church: The 14th-century church features the world's largest amber altar, which is a natural for the Amber Capital of the World. The church is also famous for the Tomb of Skulls, which includes the skulls of war victims and the skulls are lined up on a wall in a majestic scene that you can visit.

  • Deja Vu Museum “Optical Illusions Museum”: A unique modern experience and a wonderful opportunity to take photos involving optical illusions.

  • World War II Museum: You cannot miss visiting this museum. The museum tells the story of the war in an organized and chronological manner and includes original pieces dating back to the war. Since the war began when the Nazis stormed Gdańsk, the museum makes you feel like a biography of the war, which not only tells the events, but through which you can see the impact of the war on Poland and on the whole world. The museum includes more than 2,000 artifacts. 

  • Amber Museum: An ancient 3-storey building dating back to the 16th century. The museum includes a rare collection of amber. The color of amber is warm golden and sometimes light green. The museum includes wonderful and rare pieces of amber, and you can buy some tools and accessories made from amber from the bazaars that sell products in the museum.
