  1. Red Square

The main square in Russia, near the walls of the Kremlin in Moscow. Red Square annually hosts the country's main military parade and is the site of activities and festivals throughout the season. On Red Square there are St. Basil's Cathedral, the Monument to Minin and Pozharsky, the Lenin Mausoleum, the Historical Museum, as well as many cafes and shops. The amazing thing is that this wonderful square was just a random slum until the 15th century. In the 15th century, Ivan III, or Ivan the Great, came and got rid of the slums and transformed the square into this dazzling Red Square.

While walking around the square, you can imagine experiencing one of the many historical events that the square witnessed in the past. This square witnessed many events such as military marches, visits by Russian presidents, and other recorded historical events. If you are wondering why the square is called Red Square or Red Square, some stories tell that Tsar Ivan IV of Russia carried out a major massacre in the square in which a very large number of people were killed to avenge the killing of his wife. 2. Kremlin

Kremlin is a Russian word that means castle or fortress. The Kremlin is a symbol of Russian authority. It was built and opened in the 19th century. The State Museum-Reserve “Moscow Kremlin” is located on the territory of the Moscow Kremlin, and includes a number of luxurious palaces that have been converted into museums. You can visit seven museums: the Armory Room, the Ascension Cathedral, the Archangel Cathedral, the Annunciation Cathedral, the Patriarchal Chambers, and the Church of the Deposition of Saint John. Robes and bell tower of Ivan the Great.

 The Kremlin is one of the most important tourist attractions in Moscow and one of the most visited museums in the world. It is the headquarters for the most important events such as the inauguration of Russian presidents, events for granting the highest government honors, and receiving world presidents. The Kremlin contains 20 towers and 5 squares for wandering, and you can also see the largest bell and cannon in the world.

3. So we got drunk

Fadnakha is the largest entertainment complex, museum, exhibition and park in terms of area in the world. It is also one of the landmarks of Soviet culture, architecture and history. Vdenkha records the successes of the Soviet agricultural industry and is now the site of contemporary Russian and international exhibition events. The Gallery of Achievements of the National Economy was first opened in 1939. VDNKh offers views of all of Moscow's famous sights, such as the Ostankino TV Tower and Stalin's high-rise buildings. In VDNKh there are the famous sculptures “Working Woman and Collective Farm” and “Fountain of Friendship between Peoples”, and 49 objects housed in VDNKh are recognized as monuments of cultural heritage. Vadenkha is visited by 33 million tourists annually, so; Visiting it during tourism in Moscow is indispensable.

The green paradise of Fadnakha attracts visitors of all ages with its natural charm and distinctive architecture. The origins of Fadankha go back to the eighteenth century, and the beginning of its story is that it was a royal garden, and you can understand this when you visit it for the first time. The opening of the exhibition did not affect the royal beauty, but rather added to the splendor of an atmosphere that accompanies you on a journey into the heritage, culture and history of the region. We invite you to wander through the gardens and record your special moments by taking pictures next to the fountains. If your visit is in the summer, we invite you to participate in some events such as concerts, distinctive theatrical performances, or attend one of the artistic and heritage festivals. 4. Tretyakov Gallery

One of the largest state collections of paintings, sculptures and objects made of precious metals in Russia. The Tretyakov Collection includes more than 180 thousand objects that are displayed in two exhibition centers: in the old building of the Tretyakov Gallery in Lavrushinsky Lane, and in the new building of the Tretyakov Gallery in Krymsky Val. The exhibition includes one of the world's most famous paintings of the first Tsar of All Russia in the sixteenth century, Ivan IV, nicknamed Ivan the Terrible.

5. Moscow Zoo

The renovated Moscow Zoo is a corner of exotic nature in the heart of the capital. At the Moscow Zoo, you can see more than 1,200 species of world animals such as lions, tigers, polar bears, wolves, elephants, ducks, swans, peacocks and ostriches. On the territory of the zoo there is a greenhouse in which you can see more than 100 species of exotic plants. There is also a children's garden, a museum, lecture halls and exhibitions. After visiting the park, you can also attend one of the planetarium shows, which is located a short distance from the Moscow Zoo.

The park map can help you reach the animals you wish to visit, as the park is huge and you may not have time to visit it completely. You will feel that you are in the world of stories and legends when you visit the park, starting from the main entrance, which resembles medieval castles, as well as the clock tower, waterfall, and other park landmarks.

Start your journey inside the park clockwise (to the left) to be able to cross all the animal areas. If you are a fan of marine life, do not miss visiting the swimming pool, where there are seals, penguin ponds, and many wonderful marine creatures. Also visit and learn about different Russian animals.

6. Moscow - City

The first complex of high-rise buildings (skyscrapers) in Russia, Moscow City is the largest commercial center in Moscow. Moscow City is a business district with its own culture, character and environment. A unique location on the banks of the Moscow River was chosen for the construction of Moscow City. On the territory of the complex there is a metro station and a shopping center with a food court. The museum complex is a skyscraper complex with panoramic platforms and is available for visiting. In addition, there are many observation platforms that are more than 200 meters high.

 In total, 23 high-rise buildings are planned to be built on the territory of Moscow City. Don't miss the panoramic view from the 89th floor of the Federazia Tower. The city will seem to you like an endless city. Despite the high altitude with a 360-degree angle, you will find that you feel completely safe. Visiting this summit is not limited to the view only, there are also entertainment places that you can visit and spend time in, such as visiting the highest chocolate and ice cream factory in the world and other entertainment places.

7. Moscow cable car

The Moscow cable car is one of the smallest cable cars in Russia and was built to serve the FIFA World Cup, which was held at the Luzhniki Stadium. The cable car connects the right and left banks of the Moscow River, giving you access to Luzhniki Stadium in eight-seater gondola cabins. During your ride inside the cable car you will be able to enjoy enchanting views such as the magnificent views of the Moscow River, skyscrapers, Moscow University, Luzhniki Sports Complex and many other Moscow city buildings. 

The cable car, which was designed to serve the World Cup event, has become a wonderful excursion and a unique experience to enjoy the beauty of tourism in Moscow from a height equivalent to approximately 9 floors, or 220 meters above sea level. The cable car also serves sporting events in winter and summer, such as skiing and beach ball matches.

8. Ostankino TV Tower

The Ostankino TV Tower is a symbol of television and radio broadcasting in Russia. The tower was the tallest building in the world, with a height of 541 metres. Imagine a height equivalent to about 180 floors and imagine what you can see from this great height. From the glass observation decks of the Ostankino TV Tower, you can see a stunning panoramic view of the capital. 

Do not miss visiting the revolving Seventh Sky Restaurant, which consists of 3 floors. You will notice that the tower's floor numbers are not regular numbers like most towers, but rather they are numbered with signs along with the height of the floor. The tower is designed to withstand lightning strikes and winds, and it is an architectural masterpiece that is well worth a visit and you can learn more about it Ostankino Tower in Moscow.

9. Bolshoi State Academic Theater of Russia

The Bolshoi Theater is one of the most important opera and ballet theaters in the world, as it is considered one of the oldest opera houses in the world. During the theater's existence, more than 800 works were shown. The theater's repertoire preserves many classic operas and ballets, but at the same time the theater keeps pace with the times. For example, the theater posts recordings of performances on its YouTube channel.

10. Zaryadi Park

The smallest and most popular park for tourists in Moscow is Zaryadye. The park is located on the bank of the Moscow River, a few minutes from Red Square. It is a modern entertainment space that includes several museums, a concert hall and the famous “floating” bridge. The park was included in the list of the 100 best places in the world by The Times magazine for the year 2018, making it one of the favorite tourist destinations among Moscow’s destinations.
