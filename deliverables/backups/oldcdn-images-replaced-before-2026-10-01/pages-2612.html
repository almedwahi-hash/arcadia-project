Ukrainian cities

Kyiv city

Kyiv

The city of Kyiv is the capital of Ukraine and its largest city. It is located in the north-central part of the country on the Dnipro River. Its population is about 2.8 million people (according to 2012 statistics).

The city of Kiev is considered one of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe, and the first texts I mentioned refer to the sixth century AD, while some historians attribute its establishment to before that, specifically in the year 482. It is said that the city took its name from “Ki”, the person who founded the city with the help of his brothers Khorev, Shchik and Lebed.

Kiev has been a commercial, political and religious center since ancient times, and it remains so to this day. It has agriculture, trade, industrial production and folk crafts. In the eleventh century, it had eight markets, and today it has 4 airports and 3 railway stations.

Contemporary Kiev is divided into 10 districts, and transportation is well developed, the most prominent of which are buses and subway lines.

Coexistence and religious tolerance are prominent in Kiev. In addition to churches, it has mosques and temples, and citizens and residents from several countries around the world live in the city.

Odessa city

Odessa

The city of Odessa is located on the Black Sea coast in southern Ukraine, and it has the country's largest sea ports. The beginning of its history dates back to the year 1415, when it was a palace and port in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, where it was called “Katsyupiev” at that time. The population of Odessa is more than one million people (according to 2012 statistics), and it is one of the largest cities in Ukraine.

Because of its strategic location, Addessa has been famous since ancient times as a center for industry, trade and transit. And it remained that way until this time.

Today, the “Sidmoy” market (seventh kilometer) is located in Odessa, which is considered the largest in Ukraine, and it also has the largest stores in all of Europe.

In addition to trade, the city's economy depends on oil refining and the food industry. As it is considered the largest port in Ukraine and is considered a popular tourist destination for Ukrainians themselves, as well as for tourists from abroad.

The main means of transportation in the city are trams, trains and buses.

Odessa is connected to the rest of the cities and countries of the world by huge railway lines and an international airport, which is considered one of the most prominent and active airports in Ukraine.

Odessa is a city known for its culture and its prominent representatives. The famous Ukrainian poet Oleksandr Pushkin, the Russian painter Vrubel, the Polish writer Adam Mickiewicz, and other men of literature and culture lived and were influenced by it.

There are many museums and theaters in Odessa, the most famous of which are: the Odessa Numismatic Museum, the Museum of Western and Eastern Art, and the Oleksandr Pushkin Museum. Odessa is also famous for its Opera House, which is considered an architectural masterpiece of historical value.

Kharkov city

Kharkiv

The city of Kharkov is considered the beating heart of culture, science, trade and industry in Ukraine, as it was for the former Soviet Union, as it had - and still has - 60 scientific research institutes, about 30 universities, 8 museums, and 7 theaters, and its population exceeded 4 million people at that time. In the current era, Kharkiv remains a major economic center for industry and trade. Among the most prominent factories there are the engine factory, the tractor factory, the machinery factory, and the tank factory.  

Kharkiv is also known for its markets, as it has more than twenty different markets, the most famous of which is the “Barabashovwa” market, which is considered the largest in Ukraine, after the “Seventh Kilometer” market in the city of Odessa.

In the Soviet era, Kharkiv was a developed city in terms of the spread of transportation, and the level of transportation services remains high there to this day, as it has an international airport, huge railway and subway networks, in addition to the spread of trams, buses, electric buses and other means of transportation.

Architecture in Kharkiv is distinguished from other cities by features that combine elegance and grandeur, and it was called the city of skyscrapers, because tall buildings appeared there before others in the early twentieth century (even before the capital, Kiev).

In Kharkiv, Freedom Square is considered the largest square in Europe and the second largest square in the world after Red Square in Beijing, China.

Kharkiv is gaining great fame as a center of culture and education due to the abundance and diversity of its universities and institutes. It was - and still is - a major destination for students from countries around the world.

Every year, Kharkiv hosts several cultural and artistic festivals, and several famous museums, such as the History Museum, the Arts Museum, and others.

Dnipro city

Dnipro

The city of Dnepropetrovsk is located in central Ukraine, and its population is about one million people, according to 2012 statistics.

Dnepropetrovsk was first established in 1776 by order of the Empress of Russia, nicknamed “Catherine the Great,” who wanted to establish the city to strengthen the empire’s political and economic position in the region. In those days, Dnipropetrovsk was called “Ekaterinoslav”, which means “Catherine’s City” in Ukrainian and Russian. 

Then the construction of the city failed due to its geographical location close to the Dnieper River, which caused floods that submerged it every spring. Eight years later, it was rebuilt in another place on the same river that divides it, which is its current location.

Dnepropetrovsk is one of the largest cities in Ukraine and its most prominent industrial centres. Vikha has many factories and laboratories that support its economy and the Ukrainian economy, and its fields vary between metal mining, processing, mechanical, food industries, etc., and it is considered the business capital of Ukraine.

Dnepropetrovsk has an international airport and two train stations, and the most prominent means of transportation are buses, electric buses, and subway lines.

Vinnytsia city

Vinnytsia

The city of Vinnytsia is located in central Ukraine, and its population is about 370 thousand people, according to 2011 statistics.

The lands of Vinnitsa have been inhabited since ancient times, and the first mention of the city of Vinnitsa came in historical texts in the year 1396, where it is indicated that Vitovt, who was the king of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, established a castle in the city to defend it. The castle was burned by the Mongol-Tatar armies in the year 1431, and then Vitovt died. In the following centuries, Vinnytsia fell into the middle of devastating military operations, and suffered greatly from them. Then it witnessed the defeat of the Tatars in 1541, the Cossack revolt in 1651, the civil war between 1648 and 1654, a series of peasant revolts in 1704-1702, and other events. At the beginning of World War II, the Nazi German forces established a huge military center in the city, which was considered the headquarters of leader Adolf Hitler. Today, Vinnytsia is a beautiful, quiet city, in which many historical monuments and statues remain, indicating the city’s heroic past, including the ruins of Hitler’s headquarters. Vinnytsia also has various museums, such as the Local History Museum, the museum of its famous doctor Mykola Pirohov, and others.

Mykolaiv

The city of Mykolaiv (Nikolaiv) is located in the southern half of Ukraine. It is bordered to the north by Kirovgrad and to the south by the Black Sea. It is also bordered by Dnipropetrovsk to the east and to the northeast by Kherson.

The estimated area of ​​Mykolaiv  is 24,598 km², about 4.07% of the total area of ​​Ukraine.

Mykolaev is a maritime and ship town on a picturesque peninsula. Mykolaev has become a ship town and is the cradle of the Black Sea Fleet. It is considered one of the centers of shipbuilding and the maritime defense industry. The shipbuilding industry in Mykolaiv was represented by three large shipbuilding and delivery facilities in Ukraine, which have the capacity to manufacture any type of ship. This characteristic was imposed on the fate, nature and appearance of the city of Mykolaev, but now, due to the history of shipbuilding, citizens have recalled the memory through the Museum of Shipbuilding and Naval Fleets, which was previously the headquarters of the Black Sea War Fleet in 1793.

In Mykolaiv is located one of the most important Ukrainian monuments - the Greek city-state of Olivia, dating back to the 6th century BC, and now a treasure trove of archaeology. It also contains an inexhaustible treasury of scientific discoveries. Its territory covers an area of ​​​​the city of Nikolaev, estimated at 330 hectares, and its population reached 20-25 thousand people during its period of prosperity. The city still reminds us of the works of Herodotus. This city was part of the union of Athens' military fleets. High-quality works of art were also found preserved in Ukrainian museums and the museums of Berlin, London, Paris and Petersburg. This deposit contains museums, precious stones, money stores, and scientific libraries. Another wonderful attraction in the city is the zoo, which was founded in 1901 and contains 370 unique species from 2,700 individuals, including 80 species listed in the World Red Book; Therefore, it is the largest collection in Ukraine. More than 50 species of animals in Ukrainian zoos are found only here in Mykolaiv. In 1993, the park was accredited as part of the European Association of Zoos and Aquariums (EAZA). It has participated with 18 unique species in European programs for breeding rare species. There is also a museum inside the zoo, where the visitor can see the skulls and surrounding animals that lived in the zoo. The museum also contains a collection of eggs, maps, and guide books for other zoos. This type of museum is the only one inside a zoo in Ukraine. An exceptional building in the city consists of a water tower with a hyperboloid, which was built on the background of a project by Vladimir Shukhov (1907). It is considered one of the first buildings with extra roofs in the world. It represents the shape of an interlocking perforated iron tower. There are 11 towers like it in the world, and they are among the 200 towers built by Shukhov.

Lugansk city

Lugansk

The city of Lugansk is located in the Donbass region in eastern Ukraine, and its population is about 470 thousand people.

The foundation of Luhansk dates back to 1790, when large reserves of iron ore and coal were discovered in its place.

In 1795 - after the decree of the Russian Empress Catherine II - an iron factory was established on the territory of Luhansk, which laid the foundation stone for the construction of the new city.

The economic importance of the city of Luhansk increased after its establishment, and in the early twentieth century it became a major industrial center. It had about 16 different factories and factories, manufacturing products that were used throughout the Russian Empire, as well as during the Soviet Union.

Luhansk changed its name twice to “Voroshilovgrad”, but this name was not popular among the people, so the name returned to its previous historical era.

Luhansk is now considered a large industrial city of enormous economic importance for Ukraine. Its main economic fields include mechanical engineering, metallurgy, and food industries, in addition to natural gas extraction.

Thanks to its geographical location and economic role, Luhansk is today one of the most attractive Ukrainian cities for investors.

The main transportation in Luhansk is trams, buses, and electric buses, and it has a railway station and an international airport. (There is currently a conflict between the separatists and the Ukrainian regime)

Lviv city

Lviv

The city of Lviv (Lvov) is located in the western side of Ukraine. It is bordered to the north by Volyn and to the south by Zakarpattia. To the east, it is bordered by Ternopil and Ivano-Frankivsk. It is also bordered to the west by Poland.

The area of ​​Lvov is estimated at 21,833 km², about 3.61% of the total area of ​​Ukraine. Lvov is considered one of the cultural centers in Ukraine. The city was founded at the beginning of the year 1200 during the reign of Danilo Halytsky, king of the state of Halych-Volhynia, in honor of his son Lev. After Danilo’s death, Lev made Lvov the capital of Halych-Volhynia. Since the beginning of its existence, this city has become the cultural capital of the western Ukrainian lands. The history of Lvov is very rich and is linked to the history of many cities and empires. Today, Lvov is one of the largest cities in Ukraine. It is the place where history meets modern architecture. In order to gain many ideas, you will not need a tourist guide. The only thing you need is to start with a tour in the old part of Lvov, and you can ask people while you are wandering in the streets - many of them speak foreign languages.

Lvov universities are an important educational center in Ukraine. It is home to three important universities and there are eight institutions of the National Scientific Academy of Ukraine and more than forty research centers, three academies and eleven universities owned by the district. One institution that was and still is famous in the region is the Lvov Polytechnic Foundation

Ivan Franko National University in Lviv is one of the oldest universities in Central Europe, founded as a Jesuit school in 1608. Danylo Halytskyi National Medical Academy, Ukrainian Catholic University and Ukrainian Academy of Printing are all well-known in Ukraine and around the world.
