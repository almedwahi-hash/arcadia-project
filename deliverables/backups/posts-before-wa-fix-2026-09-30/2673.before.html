Tourism in Saint Petersburg, a comprehensive guide

Russia, this vast expanse of land that extends across two continents, stands as a unique cultural and historical center that combines the grandeur of ancient empires with modern developments, and offers its visitors an unparalleled experience. It is a country that is proud of a rich heritage that is evident in its stunning architecture, world-class art museums, and cities that tell stories from ancient times. From its soaring mountain peaks to its vast plains and deep lakes, Russia offers a mesmerizing geographical diversity that attracts explorers and nature lovers alike.

Tourism in Saint Petersburg, the jewel of northern Russia, is a tourist experience in itself, as it is a city whose charm is not only limited to being Russia's window to the world, but also its beating cultural and artistic heart. Founded by Tsar Peter the Great, it was called the “Venice of the North” thanks to its charming network of canals and elegant bridges, which create an unforgettable romantic scene. St. Petersburg is distinguished by its luxurious palaces, its churches with sparkling golden domes, its wide streets decorated with Baroque and neoclassical architecture, and its museums that contain priceless treasures.

Visiting this picturesque city is like a journey through time, where one can follow in the footsteps of tsars and princes, and enjoy the beauty of Russian arts that have enriched the world. The splendor of Russia is not limited to St. Petersburg alone, but extends beyond it to include its major capital and many other cities that are full of ancient history and pristine natural beauty. It is a destination that promises its visitors a unique blend of adventure, culture, and experiences that remain engraved in the memory. In this comprehensive guide, we will review the highlights of this charming destination, focusing on the most important tourist places in St. Petersburg, to help you plan the trip of a lifetime.

The most prominent tourist places in Russia

Russia is full of many tourist attractions, ranging from luxurious palaces, world-class museums, towering cathedrals, and stunning landscapes. Because tourism in St. Petersburg represents a pivotal part of the Russian experience, we will devote a large part of this section to it, in addition to highlighting other places that are no less important.

Saint Petersburg: the jewel of the north

Winter Palace and Hermitage Museum

The Winter Palace, which was the residence of the Russian Tsars, and the Hermitage Museum, one of the largest and oldest art museums in the world, are an indispensable destination when visiting St. Petersburg. The museum includes a huge collection of more than three million pieces of art and antiquities, extending from ancient times to modern art. Visitors wander through the palace's stunning halls, viewing masterpieces by Leonardo da Vinci, Rembrandt, and other art giants, in addition to the palace rooms decorated with gold and marble that reflect the greatness of the Russian Empire. It is not just a museum, it is a rich history and art that speaks for itself.

Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood

This stunning church is known as the “Blood-bound Church of the Resurrection” and is considered an architectural icon in the heart of Saint Petersburg. Built on the spot where Emperor Alexander II was assassinated, it is distinguished by its traditional Russian facade and brightly colored domes, inspired by the capital's St. Vasily Cathedral. Inside, the walls and ceilings are covered with stunning mosaics depicting scenes from the Bible, giving the place an aura of spirituality and breathtaking artistic beauty. It is a masterpiece that cannot be missed.

Palace of Peter the Great (Peterhof) and its gardens

Located outside Saint Petersburg, the Palace of Peter the Great is known as the “Russian Water Gardens” for its splendor and grandeur that rival the world’s greatest palaces. The main palace is distinguished by its magnificent Baroque design, but what makes it unique are its lower and upper gardens, which contain more than 150 fountains, the most famous of which is the “Samsun Fountain,” which depicts a lion stalking its prey. The fountains are moved by a unique natural hydraulic system, and the “Dance of the Fountains” is performed daily during the summer, providing an enchanting visual and sound spectacle that will delight visitors and make tourism in St. Petersburg a complete experience.

Nevsky Prospekt

Nevsky Prospekt is the main artery of St. Petersburg and its beating heart, extending for more than four kilometers. This lively street includes a wide range of luxury stores, elegant restaurants, trendy cafes, and famous theatres. Along its length, visitors can admire the beauty of Russian architecture, and visit some of the most notable landmarks such as the Kazan Cathedral and the House of Books. It is the perfect place to walk, shop, observe daily city life, and observe how St. Petersburg's tourism culture is evident around every corner.

Saint Isaac's Cathedral

St. Isaac's Cathedral is one of the most magnificent cathedrals in Russia, famous for its massive golden dome that dominates the St. Petersburg skyline. It took more than 40 years to build and has a luxurious neoclassical design, featuring red granite columns and bronze statues. Inside, visitors are dazzled by mosaics and frescoes that cover vast areas. Visitors can climb the “Colonnade” (circular walkway) around the cathedral’s dome to enjoy an unparalleled panoramic view of the city and its charming canals.

Mariinsky Theater

For art connoisseurs and fans of opera and ballet, the Mariinsky Theater in Saint Petersburg is a global cultural mecca. The theater has a long history and international fame thanks to the prestigious performances it has presented over the centuries. Enjoy a unique cultural experience and watch performances of classical Russian ballet or world-class opera in a luxurious setting that reflects the artistic history of the city. A visit to the Mariinsky Theater is an unforgettable experience and adds a rich cultural dimension to your Saint Petersburg tourism trip.

Islands and water channels

Saint Petersburg is known as the "Venice of the North" thanks to its extensive network of canals connecting the Neva River and several smaller rivers and canals. Taking a boat ride through these canals is a great way to explore the city from a different perspective, and see its ancient palaces and bridges. During the White Summer Nights, a unique natural phenomenon where the sun never sets completely, the lifting performances of the drawbridges that pass over the Neva River take place at midnight, presenting an enchanting spectacle that attracts thousands of visitors and showcases the magic of tourism in St. Petersburg at its best.

Tsar's Village (Tsarskoye Selo)

The Tsar's Village is located a short distance from St. Petersburg and is home to the famous Catherine Palace, which served as the summer residence of the Imperial Family. This destination is known for its legendary “Amber Room,” a lavish room decorated entirely with amber panels that illuminate with a golden glow. Although the original room was lost during the war, the reconstructed version remains a masterpiece worth seeing. The palace is surrounded by beautiful and carefully landscaped gardens, making it an ideal place for picnics and enjoying the historical and natural beauty.

The major capital and its environs

Red Square and the Kremlin

Red Square in the capital is one of the most famous squares in the world and a symbol of Russia. Surrounded by the Kremlin, a fortified complex of palaces, churches and cathedrals, it is the official residence of the head of state. Inside the Kremlin, visitors can explore the Transfiguration Cathedral, the Archangel Cathedral, and the Armory Museum, which houses precious imperial treasures. Red Square itself is a living historical place, having witnessed many important events throughout the ages.

Saint Vasily's Cathedral

St. Vasily's Cathedral stands tall at the southern end of Red Square, and is famous for its colorful and unique domes that resemble rising flames. This cathedral is a Russian architectural icon, built by order of Ivan the Terrible to celebrate his military victories. Its intricate design and bright colors make it one of the most distinctive churches in the world, attracting photographers and visitors from far and wide.

Subway

The capital's subway is not only a means of transportation, but an underground art gallery. Many of its stations feature luxurious architectural designs, exquisite artistic details, huge mosaics, and stunning sculptures. Some stations are nicknamed “underground palaces” because of their beauty, which rivals the most beautiful palaces. Taking a subway tour is a unique cultural experience that should not be missed.

Golden cities

The Golden Cities are a group of historic cities northeast of the capital, which preserve the beauty of ancient Russian architecture and traditional Orthodox churches. Sergiev Posad, Suzdal, and Vladimir are the most prominent of these cities, offering visitors a glimpse into Russian history and culture away from the hustle and bustle of the big cities. You can explore ancient monasteries and cathedrals decorated with frescoes, and enjoy the tranquility and charming nature.

Lake Baikal

Lake Baikal is the world's largest freshwater lake in terms of volume and depth, and is known as the "Pearl of Russia." It is located in the eastern part of the country, and is famous for its clear turquoise waters, unique biodiversity, and the stunning natural scenery that surrounds it. Visitors can take boat trips in the summer, or ice skating and tobogganing on its frozen surface in the winter. It is an ideal destination for nature and adventure lovers.

Other places

Sochi

The city of Sochi, located on the country's southern coast, is a popular resort that combines the beauty of warm beaches and the majesty of snowy mountains. The city hosted the Winter Olympics, making it a world-class sports and tourism destination. Visitors can enjoy swimming and relaxing on the beaches in the summer, or snowboarding and downhill skiing in the winter. It offers a unique mix of entertainment activities all year round.

Best time to visit Russia

The best time to visit Russia depends on the type of experience you are looking for, as each season offers its own flavor and unique beauty, whether you are planning tourism in St. Petersburg or exploring other cities.

Summer (June - August)

Summer is the most popular season to visit, especially if you want to enjoy nice weather and outdoor activities. In St. Petersburg, this period witnesses the magical phenomenon of “white nights”, when the sun does not set completely, giving you more time to explore and enjoy the charming beauty of the city and watch the bridge raising displays. The weather is moderate and suitable for strolling through parks and palaces, and strolling along the banks of rivers and canals. However, it is also peak season, which means higher prices and crowded tourist sites.

Spring (April - May)

Spring is characterized by the blooming of flowers and the melting of snow, giving the landscape vitality and freshness. The weather is relatively cold at the beginning of spring and gradually improves. This is an appropriate period to visit if you prefer to avoid the large crowds that occur in summer, and enjoy the beauty of nature at the beginning of its annual cycle. Museums and parks are beginning to return to their summer hours, providing a fun and relaxing experience.

Autumn (September - October)

Autumn presents a stunning sight as tree leaves turn in bright gold and red colours, creating perfect backdrops for photography. The weather is fresh and dry in September, and mild in October before the cold sets in. This season is ideal for cultural visits, as museums are less crowded and theaters and art shows can be enjoyed. This period is also considered a very suitable period for tourism in St. Petersburg to enjoy its architectural beauty under a clear sky and a calm atmosphere.

Winter (November - March)

For an authentic Russian experience, nothing beats the beauty of winter. Cities transform into magical snowy worlds, and buildings and landscapes become pure white. Despite the extreme cold, the festive atmosphere of Christmas and New Year adds a special charm. You can enjoy ice skating, visit the winter markets, or simply enjoy the beauty of the cities under the cover of snow. Indoor locations such as museums and theaters provide a warm and inviting escape. If you are prepared to brave the cold, winter offers an unforgettable and unique experience.

Budget and costs for tourism in Russia

Tourism costs in Russia vary greatly depending on your travel style and the cities you plan to visit. However, a general estimate can be provided to help plan your budget, whether you are targeting tourism in St. Petersburg or other destinations.

Accommodation

  • Luxury hotels: In major cities like St. Petersburg and the capital, the cost of a night in a luxury hotel can range from $150 to $400 or more, especially in central areas during peak seasons.
  • Mid-Range Hotels: Good hotels range in price from $70 to $150 per night and offer excellent comfort and service.
  • Hostels and guest houses: For budget travelers, many hostels and guesthouses are available with prices starting from $20 to $50 per night, offering comfortable and affordable accommodation options.

Food and drink

  • Luxury restaurants: A dinner at a fine dining restaurant can cost anywhere from $50 to $100 per person or more, not including drinks.
  • Mid-Range Restaurants: The cost of one meal at an average restaurant ranges from $20 to $40 per person.
  • Budget meals and local cafes: Delicious and affordable meals can be found in small cafes and local fast food restaurants, where the cost of a meal ranges from $5 to $15.
  • Grocery stores: Buying food from grocery stores and preparing snacks can significantly reduce food expenses.

Transportation

  • Metro: In major cities, the metro is an efficient and economical means of transportation. A single ticket costs a few dollars, and multi-use cards are available that provide discounts.
  • Buses and trams: These methods are also available at reasonable prices.
  • Taxis: Taxi booking apps can be used to ensure fair prices and avoid haggling. Costs vary depending on distance and time of day.
  • Intercity trains: For long trips between cities, such as the trip from St. Petersburg to the capital, high-speed trains provide a comfortable and fast experience, but at a higher cost than regular trains.

Activities and entertainment

  • Museums and attractions: Entrance fees for the most popular museums and attractions, such as the Hermitage and the Kremlin, range from $10 to $25 per entry. The cost can increase for some special tours or temporary exhibitions.
  • Ballet and opera performances: Tickets for ballet and opera performances at theaters like the Mariinsky and the Bolshoi can be expensive, starting at $50 and going up to hundreds of dollars for premium seats.
  • Organized tours: Organized day tours range from $50 to $150 or more, depending on the length and type of tour.

Money saving tips

  • Travel outside peak season: Traveling in the spring or fall can result in significant savings on accommodation and flights.
  • Using public transportation: Relying on metro and buses instead of taxis.
  • Local meals: Try small local cafes and restaurants that serve good food at reasonable prices.
  • City cards: Some cities offer tourist cards that allow free or discounted entry to many tourist attractions and public transportation.

Tips for the Arab traveler when tourism in Russia

To ensure an enjoyable and smooth travel experience when visiting Russia, we provide some important advice for the Arab traveler, taking into account the cultural and practical aspects.

Language and communication

  • Russian: The official language is Russian, and English may not be widely spoken outside of major hotels and major tourist sites in St. Petersburg and the capital.
  • Translation apps: It is recommended to download simultaneous translation applications that work offline, as they may be very useful in daily communication.
  • Basic phrases: Learning some basic Russian phrases like “hello”, “thank you”, “please”, “how much” can make a big difference in daily interactions and endear yourself to the locals.

Currency and cash

  • Russian ruble: The local currency is the Russian Ruble (RUB).
  • Credit cards: Major credit cards are accepted at major hotels, restaurants and large shops, but it is a good idea to carry some cash for use in small shops and public transport.
  • Exchange offices: There are exchange offices in airports, banks and commercial centers. Make sure to compare exchange rates and look for reliable places.

Communications and the Internet

  • Local SIM cards: It is recommended to purchase a local sim card upon arrival, as they offer affordable data packages and good coverage.
  • Wi-Fi networks: Free Wi-Fi is available in most hotels, cafes and restaurants, and sometimes on public transport and tourist sites.

Safety and security

  • General caution: Russia is generally considered safe for tourists, but it is recommended to take basic precautions such as being aware of personal belongings in crowded places, especially at tourist attractions and public transportation.
  • Emergencies: Keep emergency numbers (police, ambulance) and contact information for your embassy or consulate.

Respect cultural and traditions

  • Visiting religious places: When visiting churches or monasteries, it is preferable to wear modest clothing that covers the shoulders and knees. Women may be asked to cover their heads in some churches.
  • Calm: Maintain calm in public places and during cultural performances.
  • Show respect: Russian people value respect and appreciation, so be polite in your dealings.

Food and drink

  • Halal food: In larger cities such as St. Petersburg and the capital, there are some restaurants that serve halal food, but the options may be limited. It is advisable to research in advance or choose restaurants that offer vegetarian or fish options.
  • Water: It is preferable to drink bottled water instead of tap water.

Weather and clothing

  • Adapting to the weather: Temperatures vary greatly between seasons. In winter, prepare for the extreme cold by wearing several layers of warm clothing, hats, scarves, and gloves. In summer, light clothing is appropriate but it is a good idea to carry a light jacket for cooler evenings.

Prayer and mosques

  • Mosques: There are mosques in major cities such as St. Petersburg and the capital. The nearest mosque can be searched using smartphone applications.
  • Determine the Qibla: Applications are available to determine the direction of the Qibla to help you perform prayers in different places.
