Why Uzbekistan is ideal for families from the Gulf

Uzbekistan is a Muslim-majority country where halal food is the default, mosques and madrasas are the main sights, and families are welcomed everywhere. Flights from the Gulf take four to five hours, prices are among the lowest in Central Asia, and the high-speed Afrosiyob train links Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara in comfort. Read why to choose Uzbekistan and the best things to do in Uzbekistan 2026.

The four cities every itinerary should include

  • Tashkent: the modern capital, Chorsu Bazaar, the Hazrati Imam complex with one of the world's oldest Qurans, and a metro famous for its decorated stations.
  • Samarkand: Registan Square, Shah-i-Zinda, Bibi-Khanym Mosque and the Ulugh Beg Observatory. See our Samarkand guide.
  • Bukhara: a living old city of 2,000 years, Poi Kalon minaret, the Ark fortress and covered trading domes.
  • Khiva: the walled museum-city of Itchan Kala, best explored on foot at sunset. See Khiva, city of preserved history.

Sample itineraries

  • 5 days: Tashkent (1) → Samarkand (2) → Bukhara (2). The classic first visit.
  • 7 days: adds Khiva by domestic flight or the Bukhara–Khiva road through the Kyzylkum desert. Full day-by-day plan in Uzbekistan 7-day suggested program.
  • 10 days: combines Uzbekistan with Almaty in Kazakhstan, ideal for summer when the mountains stay cool.

Best time to visit

Spring (April to early June) and autumn (September to early November) are ideal, with 20–30°C days and clear skies. July and early August exceed 40°C in Bukhara and Khiva, so summer trips should start sightseeing early, rest at midday and resume in the evening when the monuments are lit. Winter is cold but cheap and uncrowded. Festivals worth planning around: Nowruz on 21 March, the Silk and Spices Festival in Bukhara in late May and Sharq Taronalari in Samarkand in late August. What a family trip costs

For a family of four on a 7-day private program with 4-star hotels, a car with driver and an Arabic- or English-speaking guide, expect roughly 2,200–3,400 USD in spring and autumn, and 1,700–2,900 USD in summer and winter, excluding international flights. Afrosiyob train tickets cost about 15–25 USD per person per leg, and a full meal in a good restaurant 8–15 USD per person.

Visa and practical notes

  • Citizens of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman enter visa-free for tourism (30 days). Other nationalities can usually apply for the e-visa online within a few days.
  • The currency is the Uzbek som; cards work in hotels and larger restaurants, but carry cash for bazaars. ATMs are common in all four cities.
  • Dress is relaxed; modest clothing is appreciated at religious sites. Halal food is universal.
  • Book Afrosiyob trains and Bukhara boutique hotels early in April, May, September and October.

Frequently asked questions

Is Uzbekistan safe for families?
Yes. It is one of the safest countries in the region, with low crime and tourist police in the main cities.

Do guides speak Arabic?
Yes. Arcadia provides Arabic-speaking guides in Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva, as well as English-speaking guides.

Can Uzbekistan be combined with another country?
Yes. The most popular combination is Uzbekistan with Kazakhstan (Almaty) over 10 days; Kyrgyzstan is another option in summer.

How far in advance should we book?
Six to eight weeks for spring and autumn, three to four weeks in other seasons.
