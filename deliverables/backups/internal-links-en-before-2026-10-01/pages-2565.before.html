Location and how it affects tourism in Poland

Poland enjoys a strategic location in the heart of Europe, as Poland is considered a bridge between East and West in Europe. Germany is bordered by Poland to the west, the Czech Republic and Slovakia to the south, Ukraine and Belarus to the east, Lithuania and the Russian Kaliningrad region to the northeast, and the Baltic Sea to the north.

Which made it throughout history an area of ​​conflict and influence between the major European powers. Its terrain is diverse, including the northern plains that extend towards the Baltic Sea, and the highlands and mountains in the south, such as the Sudeten Mountains and the Carpathian Mountains.

Poland's culture is one of the attractions for tourism in Poland

Poland has a rich and diverse cultural heritage, reflecting its long and complex history. Poland has been the cradle of many prominent figures in the fields of literature, music and science, such as composer Frederic Chopin and Nobel Prize-winning scientist Marie Curie.

Poland is famous for its classical music festivals and cultural festivals, which include many cultural events worth attending. We mention the Krakow Film Festival and the Krakow Music Festival, both of which attract a large number of visitors annually.  Also, Woodstock Poland Festival, which you can attend for free and enjoy various musical performances. As for lovers of modern music, the Warsaw Festival is the perfect choice for them, as is the Orange Warsaw Festival. We advise you to review the cultural and artistic events available on the date of your trip to the different cities of Poland so that you do not miss a wonderful opportunity to attend one of them. Holidays and traditions that you can observe during tourism in Poland

Poland is famous for its rich traditions, especially on religious holidays such as Christmas (Wiegerke) and Easter (Wilkanok). It also has many cultural and artistic festivals that reflect its cultural diversity, as we mentioned.

One of the Polish customs is that you pay exactly for your purchases, otherwise you will find it difficult. Always keep change with you when shopping and buying food. When eating with others, it is necessary to say “thank you” even if the food preparers are not present.  Repeating the word “thank you” after eating is their way of expressing gratitude, and they consider it good manners. On the contrary, it is considered rude to eat and then leave in silence. Expect strangers to ask you to join your table if there are no available tables.

 The Polish citizen is very punctual, whether on official occasions or meetings. If you visit a Polish house, you must offer sweets, flowers, or a souvenir from your country. Shake hands with the owners of the house after entering. Some people ask that you take off your shoes and provide you with slippers, but others do not care about taking off their shoes as it has become a rather old habit. The host will often prepare a large meal to host you. Remind the host that you cannot eat pork or lard or drink alcohol because of your religious beliefs. Poles respect other beliefs and will never force you to do any of this. 

If you are a smoker, avoid smoking at home. Polish smokers smoke outside the house or near the window. Try to avoid leading conversations about politics or controversial topics. If you are Muslim and the host family is Christian, expect some questions about Islam. The host will feel curious about Islam, which is normal. Conversations usually start after finishing eating, so; Never leave after eating. You will notice that the farewell will be more warm than the reception, as the host has gotten to know you better now.

History of Poland The main attraction of tourism in Poland

Poland's history is full of major events, from the period of medieval prosperity when Poland was one of Europe's largest countries, to the partitions that led to its disappearance from the European map in the 18th century, and its re-emergence as an independent country after World War I. During World War II, Poland was one of the worst affected countries, being invaded by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union, and losing millions of its population.

Poland's history is rich in events and heroic stories of trying to regain its land, get rid of occupation, and achieve independence again, which is one of Poland's characteristics. Poland went through the horrors of World War I and World War II and the cruel hand of communism, but each time it regained its strength again and rebuilt itself. 

You can visit many tourist places in Poland and get to know the history of Poland better, including:

  • Krakow Castle: Wander among the buildings that commemorate the Middle Ages. Don't miss strolling around the Market Square in Krakow as well.

  • Warsaw's Old Town: Stroll the historic Market Square. The Market Square is an open history book, wander through its pages.

  • Salt Mine: The mine is located in Wieliczka, near Krakow, and dates back to the thirteenth century.

  • Gdańsk Old Town: It's an open-air museum of ancient architecture. Walk around the Market Square and get to know the city.

  • Mallork Castle: The castle also dates back to the thirteenth century, and is the largest castle in the world.

  • Museums: Poland has wonderful museums worth visiting. Top of the list are the World War II Museum, Auschwitz-Birkenau Museum, Warsaw Uprising Museum and Krakow National Museum.

The most frequently asked questions for those coming to tourism in Poland

  • What are the most popular foods in Poland and is there halal food in Poland?

Polish cuisine is very diverse, which is one of the characteristics of Poland. Among the most famous Polish dishes are pierogi (dumplings filled with either cheese, potatoes, meat, or fruit), golumbki (cabbage stuffed with minced meat and rice and cooked in tomato sauce), kibasa (garlic-flavored sausages), and bigos (so-called fishermen's skewers).

As for halal food, it is easy to obtain in Krakow and Warsaw, where there are many Indian, Turkish, and Arab restaurants that serve halal food. Some Polish restaurants also serve halal food. Just check first before ordering the food if there is a halal certificate. Many Polish dishes are also vegetarian and are prepared with potatoes, dough, vegetables, mushrooms and cheese.

  • Is Poland a safe country?

Yes, Poland is a relatively safe country, which makes Poland the ideal choice for family tourism. If you are looking for more details in this regard, visit This link.

  • Is Poland part of the European Union and can the euro be used there?

Yes, Poland is a member of the European Union, but it still uses the local currency, which is the zloty.

  • What is the weather like in Poland throughout the year?

The weather varies in Poland and it has 4 weather seasons as follows:

  1. The winter season is from December until February, and it enjoys cold weather and snowfall, where temperatures are sometimes below zero. So; Winter is the best time for lovers of winter sports such as skiing.

  2. Spring is from March to May and enjoys somewhat better weather compared to winter. During which temperatures range between 5 degrees and 15 degrees, rain falls on some days and sunshine on other days.

  3. Summer is between the months of June and August. Summer is considered a warm season compared to the winter and spring seasons, where temperatures reach twenty-five degrees.

  4. The fall semester is between September and November. The weather is divided into two phases: it begins with warm weather and then turns cold, and even snow falls in some areas during the month of November. Autumn is characterized by dazzling colors and the leaves begin to fall. 

  • What is the best time to visit Poland?

It depends on your personal preferences. Many visitors prefer the spring and fall seasons to enjoy moderate weather, especially since those months enjoy better prices and fewer tourists, thus eliminating crowding during tourist visits. Others prefer winter to enjoy winter sports and market decorations while preparing for the Christmas season and holiday activities.
