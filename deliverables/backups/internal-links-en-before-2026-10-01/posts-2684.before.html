Khiva, City of Preserved History: A Journey Through Time in the Heart of Uzbekistan

Uzbekistan, the enchanting land located in the heart of Central Asia, which has always been and continues to be a meeting point of civilizations and a cradle of ancient cultures, invites travelers to explore its hidden treasures. It is a republic rich in history that extends back thousands of years, a witness to the glory of the legendary Silk Road, and home to cities where every stone tells stories of bygone times. From its green oases to its vast deserts, from its towering mountains to its spacious plains, Uzbekistan appears as a unique artistic painting in which the threads of the past and the present are intertwined to weave an unforgettable experience. This country is an endless mine of charming Islamic architecture, bustling markets bustling with life, and friendly people who cling to their authentic traditions and overflowing generosity.

In the heart of this rich history, Khiva stands out as one of the most fascinating and inspiring destinations. It is not just a city, but rather a living museum, an architectural masterpiece preserved with the utmost care, giving its visitors a rare opportunity to travel through a time machine and live in a medieval atmosphere. Khiva is a city of history preserved in all its details, from its towering walls to its winding alleys, from its towering minarets to its ancient schools, offering an immersive experience unparalleled anywhere else on earth. Uzbekistan, with its ancient cities such as Samarkand And Bukhara andTashkentIn addition to Khiva, it is an ideal destination for lovers of history, art and culture, where an unforgettable adventure awaits them in a world of magic and beauty.

The most prominent tourist places in Uzbekistan

Uzbekistan is full of many ancient cities and tourist attractions that are living witnesses to the greatness of the civilizations that flourished on its land. Each city is a story in itself, and each landmark carries within it a cultural and architectural heritage worth exploring and appreciating.

Khiva: a world heritage jewel and a city of preserved history

Khiva sits on the throne of historical cities in Uzbekistan as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Khiva is truly a city of preserved history, as it represents a unique model of an oasis city in Central Asia that has preserved its original architectural form. As soon as you set foot in this city, you will feel as if you have gone back in time to centuries ago, where the ancient atmosphere still dominates every nook and corner.

  • Ichan Kala (Inner Walled City): It is the beating heart of Khiva and its most precious treasure. Surrounded by massive mud walls, some more than 10 meters high, and extending over 26 hectares, this inland city has more than 50 historical monuments and 250 ancient houses. Walking within the walls of Ichan Kala is like walking into an open-air museum, where narrow cobblestone alleys lead you through a maze of stunning ancient buildings. Each of its four gates (Ota Dervoza, Palwan Dervoza, Tash Dervoza, Bkhacha Dervoza) is a masterpiece in itself and a history to tell.
  • Konya Ark Castle: This castle was the official residence of the rulers of Khiva, and includes several courtyards, mosques, baths, storerooms and prisons. From its balconies, visitors can enjoy a charming panoramic view of the entire city, especially at sunrise or sunset, as the clay walls turn golden.
  • Minaret and Madrasah of Islam Khoja: The Islam-Khoja Minaret is the tallest minaret in Khiva at nearly 45 metres, and is decorated with mesmerizing blue and green ceramic tiles that rise towards the sky. Next to it is the school of the same name, another architectural masterpiece showcasing traditional Uzbek court art.
  • Tash Palace Hawalli: Known as the “Palace of Stones,” this luxurious palace served as the winter palace for the rulers of Khiva. Comprising more than 160 rooms and six courtyards, it is lavishly decorated with wood carvings and stunning blue and white ceramic tiles, reflecting the height of 19th-century architecture.
  • Friday Mosque (Juma Mosque): This mosque is distinguished by its unique design, as it includes more than 200 finely carved wooden columns, each column telling a different story and design. The mosque is bathed in cool shadows during the day, making it a peaceful haven for contemplation.
  • Mausoleum of Pahlawan Mahmoud: This shrine is a tribute to the legendary poet, philosopher and wrestler Pahlavan Mahmud, who is considered the patron saint of Khiva. The shrine is distinguished by its bright blue dome and lavish interior decorated with beautiful majolica tiles. It is a highly respected site that reflects the spiritual side of the city.
  • Muhammad Amin Khan School: Located next to the Uta Dervuza Gate, this grand madrasa was one of the largest madrasas in Central Asia. Its facade decorated with turquoise and dark blue tiles makes it one of the most beautiful buildings in Khiva.
  • Minaret like a minaret: Although incomplete, the turquoise and green tiled Kalmenor Minaret is an iconic symbol of Khiva, and stands out for its stunning beauty.

Khiva is a city of preserved history that deserves a visitor to spend at least two days to explore all its corners and secrets, wander through its ancient markets, talk to its friendly residents, and enjoy its eternal beauty.

Samarkand: Pearl of the Orient

Samarkand, often called the “Pearl of the Orient” or the “City of One Thousand and One Nights,” is one of the oldest cities in Central Asia and a major center on the Silk Road. It was the capital of the Timurid Empire, famous for its opulent architecture, stunning mosques, and historic madrassas.

  • Registan Square: It is undoubtedly the heart of Samarkand and its most prominent symbol. It consists of three beautiful schools surrounding a spacious square: Ulugh Beg School, Sher Dur School, and Tilia Kuri School. Each school features its own unique architectural design, elaborate geometric and floral decorations, and colorful tiles that sparkle in the sun, creating an unforgettable visual spectacle.
  • Bibi Khanum Mosque: This huge mosque, commissioned by Timur after his return from his Indian campaign, is one of the largest mosques in the Islamic world. Although time has taken a lot from it, its remains still bear witness to its greatness and architectural beauty.
  • Shah-i-Zindah Tomb: This cemetery is a stunning funerary complex featuring a series of sumptuously decorated mausoleums, some dating back to the 11th century. The tomb is distinguished by its shimmering blue tiles covering its walls and domes, giving it the nickname “The Blue Cemetery.” It is a place of great spiritual importance, and some believe it houses the tomb of the Prophet Muhammad's cousin.
  • Gur Amir Mausoleum: It is the tomb of Emperor Timur and some of his descendants. This building is distinguished by its ribbed turquoise dome and lavish interior decoration of inlaid marble, ornate onyx and gold. It is a masterpiece that reflects the splendor of Timurid architecture.
  • Ulugh Beg Observatory: This observatory was built in the 15th century by Ulugh Beg, Timur's grandson and a prominent astronomer. Although only a portion of it survives, it represents a tremendous scientific achievement of its time, and bears witness to the great contributions made by Muslim scholars to astronomy.

Bukhara: the holy city

Known as the "Holy City" of Central Asia, Bukhara is home to more than a thousand historical architectural monuments. It is a city that has preserved its medieval essence, characterized by its covered markets, traditional baths, and winding streets that invite exploration.

  • Boy Kalyan Collection: It is the architectural heart of Bukhara, and consists of three main elements:
    • Kalyan Minaret: It is also known as the "Tower of Death" because it was used to execute criminals. Standing over 45 meters high, this towering minaret stands as a symbol of the city of Bukhara, distinguished by its finely carved bricks that form complex geometric patterns.
    • Kalyan Mosque: It is a huge mosque that can accommodate thousands of worshipers, and is distinguished by its spacious courtyard and elaborate columns.
    • Mir Arab School: Still operating today, this religious school is a fine example of 16th-century Islamic architecture, famous for its facade decorated with mosaic tiles.
  • Basin core group: It is a quiet central square that includes a large water pool (basin), surrounded by three historical buildings: the Nader Divan-Bigi Madrasa, the Nader Diwan-Bigi Khanqah, and the Kokeldash Madrasa. This area served as a social and commercial center for local residents.
  • Samanid Mausoleum: An architectural masterpiece from the 10th century, this shrine is one of the oldest and most beautiful buildings in Central Asia. It is famous for its elaborate design made of carved bricks that change color with the movement of the sun.
  • Jar Minor: Jar Minor means “four minarets,” and is a small, unconventional madrasa that features four uniquely decorated minarets, each topped with a turquoise dome. It is a distinctive architectural landmark that showcases the diversity of design in Bukhara.

Tashkent: the modern capital with its ancient history

Tashkent is the capital of Uzbekistan and one of the largest cities in Central Asia. Although it is a modern city largely rebuilt after the 1966 earthquake, it still retains its historical roots and offers a unique blend of contemporary and traditional.

  • Hazrat Imam Complex: It is the spiritual heart of Tashkent, and includes many important landmarks such as the Kval Shashi Mausoleum, the Barak Khan Madrasa, and the Tilia Sheikh Mosque, where the Uthman Qur’an is displayed, one of the oldest copies of the Holy Quran in the world.
  • Chorsu Bazaar: It is a huge traditional market located under a giant blue dome, full of life, colors and scents. Here visitors can find everything from spices and fresh fruit to artisanal textiles and traditional antiques.
  • Amir Temur Square: It is a central square that includes a statue of Amir Timur on horseback, surrounded by many modern buildings and beautiful gardens.
  • Tashkent Metro: Similar to the Moscow Metro, Tashkent Metro stations are famous for their architectural beauty and artistic designs, with each station resembling a small museum.

These are some of the most prominent destinations in Uzbekistan, each offering a different facet of this country's beauty and ancient history. Whether you are looking for Khiva, a city of preserved history, the grandeur of Samarkand, the spirituality of Bukhara, or the vibrancy of Tashkent, Uzbekistan promises an unforgettable journey of discovery.

Best time to visit

Choosing the right time to visit Uzbekistan is crucial to ensuring a comfortable and enjoyable travel experience, given its diverse continental climate. In general, the country is characterized by hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters.

  • Spring and fall semesters (April-May, September-October): They are considered the best times to visit Uzbekistan. In these seasons, the weather is pleasant and mild, with ideal temperatures for exploring ancient cities and monuments on foot without being exposed to the intense summer heat or the bitter cold of winter. Nature blooms in the spring, and the trees take on bright colors in the fall, adding a special charm to the natural and architectural scenes. Many cultural festivals and traditional celebrations are also held at these times, giving visitors an opportunity to learn about the local heritage.
  • Summer (June-August): Summers can be very hot in Uzbekistan, with temperatures often exceeding 40°C, especially in desert areas and southern cities. This may be a good time for travelers who prefer hot climates or are planning indoor visits or in the early morning and evening. However, walking around outdoors for long hours can be exhausting.
  • Winter season (November-March): Winters are cold, and snow falls in some areas. Although some attractions may be closed or have shorter operating hours, visiting Uzbekistan in winter can be a unique experience, as the historic cities are covered in a layer of snow, giving them a charming beauty and a distinct calm away from the tourist crowds. Accommodation and flight costs may be lower this season.

In general, if you are looking for the best weather conditions to explore Khiva, a city of preserved history and other architectural treasures, spring and fall are the perfect choices.

Budget and costs

Uzbekistan is a relatively affordable destination compared to many other tourist countries, making it an attractive option for travelers with different budgets. However, costs can vary based on travel style and traveler preferences.

  • Flights: Airline tickets to Uzbekistan are often the largest portion of a travel budget, and their prices vary greatly depending on the starting point and time of year. It is recommended to book in advance to get the best rates.
  • Accommodation:
    • Economic budget: Guest houses and hostels are available at very reasonable prices, especially in larger cities such as Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva. The cost per night can range from US$10-30.
    • Mid-Range Hotels: Many boutique hotels in the Old Towns offer a luxurious accommodation experience at moderate prices, ranging from US$40-80 per night.
    • Luxury hotels: Luxury five-star hotels are available in Tashkent and some other cities, with prices ranging from US$100 and above.
  • Food: Food in Uzbekistan is very delicious and affordable.
    • Local restaurants (Chaikhana): A delicious lunch or dinner can be had at local restaurants for US$5-10 per person. They include traditional dishes such as plov (rice with meat and vegetables), shashlik (grilled meat), and samosas.
    • Fine dining restaurants: A meal in more upscale restaurants can cost US$20-40.
    • Drinks: Bottled water is very cheap, and juices and tea are plentiful.
  • Transportation:
    • Between cities:
      • Express trains (Afrosiyab): It connects major cities such as Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara, and is a convenient and efficient way. Ticket prices are reasonable (about 15-30 USD).
      • Regular trains and shared taxis: Options are less expensive but may take longer.
      • Domestic flights: Available between Tashkent and distant cities such as Urgench (to reach Khiva).
    • Within cities:
      • Taxis: Very cheap, especially if the price is negotiated before the start of the trip.
      • Public transportation: Buses and metro (in Tashkent) are very affordable options.
  • Activities and entry fees: Entrance fees to museums and historical sites are very reasonable, usually ranging between $1-5 USD per site.
  • shopping: Travelers can enjoy shopping in the bazaars for souvenirs, handicrafts and textiles, and it is recommended to bargain to get the best prices.

In general, an average budget traveler can expect to spend around US$50-100 per day (excluding international flight costs), while a budget traveler can live on less than that, and luxury travelers can spend more.

Tips for the Arab traveler

For Arab travelers wishing to explore the magic of Uzbekistan, here is a set of practical tips that will help you enjoy a comfortable and fruitful trip in Khiva, the city of preserved history, and the rest of Uzbek cities:

  • the language: Uzbek is the official language, and Russian is widely spoken, especially among the older generation. In the larger tourist areas, you will find some English speakers, but learning some basic phrases in Russian or Uzbek (such as "Hello", "Thank you", "How much is this?") will be very useful and appreciated by the locals. Since Uzbekistan is a Muslim-majority country, the “Peace be upon you” greeting would be understood and welcomed.
  • Culture and Literature: The Uzbek people are known for their generosity and hospitality.
    • Respect: Show respect for elders and local traditions. When visiting religious sites, it is best to wear modest clothing that covers the shoulders and knees. Women may be asked to cover their heads when entering mosques or shrines.
    • Greeting: When shaking hands with men, both hands are usually used. For women, it is preferable to wait until the Uzbek woman initiates the handshake.
    • Hospitality: If you are invited to an Uzbek home, it is a great honor. It is polite to bring a small gift (such as candy or fruit).
  • Safety: Uzbekistan is considered a very safe country for travelers. However, it is always advisable to take basic precautions such as maintaining personal belongings and avoiding unoccupied areas late at night.
  • Currency and money: The local currency is the Uzbek som (UZS).
    • Automated teller machines: They are available in major cities, but may not always be available in remote areas.
    • currency exchange: US dollars or euros can be easily converted at authorized banks and exchange offices. It is advisable to carry some small change in som for shopping in the bazaars or paying transportation costs.
    • Credit cards: Acceptable in larger hotels and trendy restaurants in Tashkent and Samarkand, but may not be acceptable in smaller places.
  • Communication and Internet: A local SIM card can be easily purchased from official carrier stores (such as Ucell or Beeline) in airports or major cities, offering affordable data packages. Wi-Fi is available in most hotels and cafes.
  • Food and water:
    • Water: It is recommended to drink only bottled water and avoid tap water.
    • Halal food: Since Uzbekistan is a Muslim-majority country, most food is halal and readily available. Enjoy delicious local delicacies such as plov, shurpa, lagman and manti.
  • Health and insurance: Make sure your vaccinations are up to date. It is advisable to carry necessary personal medications and a small first aid kit. It is highly recommended to obtain travel insurance that covers medical emergencies.
  • Photography: Although Uzbeks are generally friendly, it is always polite to ask permission before photographing people, especially the elderly or children.

By following these tips, your trip to Uzbekistan will be an experience rich in culture, history and beauty, and you will have the opportunity to explore Khiva, the city of preserved history, with comfort and confidence.
