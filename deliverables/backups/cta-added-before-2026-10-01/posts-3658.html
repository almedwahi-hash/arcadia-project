Getting to Shanghai: Entry and Arrival

Visa Requirements for Arab Nationals

Most Arab nationals require a Chinese Tourist Visa (L Visa) or Business Visa (M or F Visa) to enter China. Requirements:

  • Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)
  • Completed visa application form
  • Recent passport photo (white background, 2x2 inches)
  • Proof of accommodation booking
  • Return flight booking (or itinerary)
  • Bank statement showing sufficient funds

Important Update 2024-2026: China has significantly expanded its visa-free transit policy. Travelers from many countries (including several Arab states) can now transit through Shanghai airports visa-free for up to 144 hours (6 days). This is an excellent way to explore Shanghai on a tight schedule. Verify current policy on the official Chinese embassy website for your country.

Top Attractions in Shanghai

1. The Bund (外滩) – Shanghai's Iconic Waterfront

The Bund is Shanghai's most famous image: a 1.5-kilometer promenade along the Huangpu River, flanked on the west by 52 magnificent colonial-era buildings from the early 20th century (British, French, American, and Russian architectural styles), and facing the sci-fi skyline of Pudong across the river. Day and night are equally spectacular, but sunset and the first hour of darkness, when both banks light up simultaneously, produce one of the world's most dramatic urban panoramas.

Entry is free. Best viewing: take the Bund observation deck or visit the Waldorf Astoria bar for a cocktail with the view.

2. Yu Garden and Old City (豫园)

Built in 1559 during the Ming Dynasty, Yu Garden is a classical Chinese garden featuring ornamental rockeries, dragon-topped walls, tranquil ponds, and ancient pavilions on just 5 acres. The surrounding Old Town area is crowded with traditional architecture, street food stalls (soup dumplings!), tea houses, and craft markets.

Admission: $7 | Hours: 8:30 AM – 5:00 PM

3. The Bund Architecture Walk

The colonial buildings along Zhongshan Road are individually extraordinary. Notable ones include the Peace Hotel (1929 jazz bar still operating), the former HSBC building with its stunning interior murals, and the Custom House with its Big Ben-inspired clock tower. Pick up a free architectural guide map at any tourist information point.

4. Shanghai Tower and Pudong Skyline Experience

Shanghai Tower (632 meters, world's second tallest) houses the world's highest observation deck and is an architectural marvel with its twisted double-skin design. Equally impressive are its neighbors: the Jin Mao Tower (421m, Art Deco-influenced) and the Shanghai World Financial Center (492m, with a distinctive rectangular sky aperture). The three skyscrapers together form one of the world's most photographed urban clusters.

Observation deck admission: $20-25 | Best time: morning before haze builds

5. The French Concession (法租界)

The most atmospheric neighborhood in Shanghai. Between 1849 and 1943, the French Concession operated as an autonomous foreign territory, and its legacy is a warren of shady plane-tree-lined streets dotted with 1920s and 1930s villas, independent boutiques, internationally acclaimed restaurants, creative studios, and café terraces. This is where Shanghai residents come to feel the city at its most human scale.

Key streets: Wukang Road (most photographed), Xintiandi (restored Shikumen alley houses with restaurants), Tianzifang (artisan market district).

6. Tianzifang (田子坊) – Artist Village

A labyrinthine network of Shikumen (stone gate) alleyways in the French Concession, transformed from a residential neighborhood into an arts and crafts district. Narrow lanes connect galleries, independent boutiques, cafés, craft beer bars, and traditional design studios. An afternoon wandering Tianzifang with no particular agenda is one of Shanghai's great pleasures.

7. Shanghai Museum – World-Class Collections

One of China's finest museums, housing exceptional collections of ancient Chinese bronzes (Shang and Zhou Dynasties), ceramics (Tang through Qing Dynasties), jade, calligraphy, painting, and furniture. The ceramics collection alone is arguably the best in the world. Free entry | Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM, closed Mondays

8. Xintiandi – Architecture and Lifestyle

A beautifully restored block of Shikumen alley houses transformed into upscale restaurants, boutiques, and cafés. The architecture is authentic; the atmosphere is theatrical and pleasant. Also home to the original site of the First National Congress of the Communist Party of China – a fascinating historical monument in an entirely commercial context.

Halal Dining in Shanghai

Shanghai has a long-established Muslim community (primarily Hui ethnic Chinese) and a significant foreign Muslim population from Southeast Asia, South Asia, and the Middle East. Halal food is more accessible here than in most Chinese cities.

Best Areas for Halal Food

  • Xiaoyushan Road (小玉山路): Known as "Little Muslim Street," several halal Uyghur, Hui Chinese, and international Muslim restaurants
  • Jing'an District: Several halal Muslim restaurants near the city center
  • Near Shanghai South Railway Station: Concentration of Hui Muslim and South Asian halal restaurants

Shopping in Shanghai

Luxury Shopping

  • IAPM Mall (Jing'an): Flagship international luxury brands, Apple Store, premium dining
  • Plaza 66: Chanel, Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Hermès – the most prestigious luxury mall
  • Nanjing West Road: Shanghai's most upscale shopping corridor

Mid-Range and Fashion Shopping

  • Nanjing East Road Pedestrian Street: 1.7 km of shops, from international brands to local chains
  • Xintiandi Style Mall: International mid-range fashion in a beautiful setting

Markets and Bargain Shopping

  • The Science and Technology Museum Market (STMM): Electronics, tech accessories, bags, clothing at wholesale prices. Bargaining is expected and necessary.
  • Dongtai Road Antiques Market: Antiques, Mao-era memorabilia, traditional crafts
  • Tianzifang: Unique handmade crafts, original artwork, Chinese design souvenirs

Shanghai for Business Travelers

Key Business Districts

  • Lujiazui (Pudong): Financial and commercial hub; home to major banks, trading companies, and multinational headquarters
  • Jing'an District: Tech companies, creative agencies, and professional services
  • Zhangjiang High-Tech Park: Biotech, pharma, semiconductor companies
  • Waigaoqiao Free Trade Zone: Import/export logistics, bonded warehousing

Shanghai Free Trade Zone Benefits

The China (Shanghai) Pilot Free Trade Zone (FTZ), established in 2013, offers significant advantages for international businesses:

  • Simplified business registration and licensing
  • Lower tariffs on certain imported goods
  • More flexible currency exchange
  • Access to otherwise blocked internet services (within the FTZ)
  • Streamlined customs procedures

Getting Around Shanghai

Metro System

Shanghai's metro is one of the world's most extensive (20 lines, 500+ stations) and most efficient. It covers virtually every tourist attraction and business district. Single trip fares: $0.40-1.50 depending on distance. Buy a Shanghai Public Transport Card for seamless tapping in and out.

Payment: The Essential Info

  • WeChat Pay and Alipay: Nearly everything in Shanghai is cashless. Foreign visitors can now link an international Visa/Mastercard directly to WeChat Pay and Alipay (implemented 2023). Set this up immediately upon arrival.
  • Cash (RMB): Useful as backup; exchange at airport, hotels, or bank branches
  • ATMs: Available everywhere but China UnionPay logo is local only – look for Visa/Mastercard/Maestro logos for international cards

3-Day Shanghai Itinerary for Arab Tourists

Day 1: Historic Shanghai and The Bund

  • Morning: Yu Garden and Old Town (9:00-11:30 AM)
  • Lunch: Xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) at Din Tai Fung or Nanxiang Mantou Dian
  • Afternoon: Walk the Bund southward, visit the Bund Architecture Museum (free)
  • Sunset: Riverside promenade for the golden hour view
  • Evening: Dinner at a halal restaurant, then night view from a rooftop bar

Day 2: Modern Shanghai and French Concession

  • Morning: Pudong – Shanghai Tower observation deck (arrive early to beat crowds)
  • Lunch: Restaurant in Lujiazui area
  • Afternoon: French Concession – walk Wukang Road, explore Tianzifang
  • Evening: Xintiandi for dinner and after-dinner stroll

Day 3: Culture, Shopping, and Departure

  • Morning: Shanghai Museum (free, 2 hours minimum)
  • Midday: Nanjing Road shopping
  • Afternoon: Science and Technology Museum Market for deals and electronics
  • Evening: Huangpu River cruise for final views before departure
