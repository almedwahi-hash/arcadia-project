 It makes sense to start our tour from Poland's capital and largest city, Warsaw. Although at first glance it appears to be a modern-style city, it combines modern architecture with a scent of history. Its ancient roads tell you about the great destruction it suffered in World War II, but at the same time they tell you about the glory of its people who quickly reconstructed it. In the Old Town in particular, you will notice colorful historical buildings around you, gravel roads under your feet, and local cafes within sight, so you will feel like you have traveled on a time trip to the Middle Ages. Also located in the heart of the old town is the Royal Palace, which is one of its most prominent landmarks Warsaw Which combines amazing architecture with historical symbolism, as this palace was the residence of the Polish kings. There is also the Palace of Culture and Science, which is the tallest building in the capital, Warsaw, with a height of 237 meters, and you can go to it to have a look at the city from above.  If you want to enjoy nature, you should go to Lazienki Park, also called the Royal Garden, which is decorated with lakes and fountains, and in the middle of the water is the small Lazienki Palace.  Despite the difference in tastes, the tourist places in Poland satisfy everyone, and in Gdansk specifically, located on the coast of the Baltic Sea, you can enjoy the coastal nature of the city. Tourists know Wrocław as a charming city in Poland, built over 12 islands connected by more than 130 bridges. Wrocław is known as the City of Dwarves, as it is home to approximately 180 small bronze statues of dwarves who wear small shoes and carry large hats on their heads, and each of them performs a task, which makes them look funny, especially for children.  Therefore, many visitors who want to visit Poland for families visit this area, as maps are sold to search for these dwarves in the old city, which makes it an exciting adventure.
