Complete Guide to Moving and Staying in Kazakhstan 2026

Kazakhstan, the world's ninth-largest country and the largest landlocked nation on Earth, is rapidly establishing itself as a premier destination for both tourists and expats in Central Asia. Whether you're planning a short vacation or a longer stay, understanding the essentials of transportation, accommodation, and daily life in Kazakhstan can make the difference between a frustrating experience and an unforgettable adventure. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know to navigate and settle in Kazakhstan with ease.

Entry Requirements and Visa Information

Visa-Free Access

Kazakhstan has significantly liberalized its visa policy in recent years. Citizens of over 75 countries can enter visa-free for periods ranging from 14 to 30 days. Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) nationals – UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, and Oman – enjoy 30 days of visa-free access. Always verify current requirements with the official Kazakhstan embassy before travel, as policies can change.

Electronic Visa (eVisa)

For those who need a visa, Kazakhstan's e-Visa system at evisa.e.gov.kz makes the process straightforward:

  • Processing time: 3-5 business days
  • Cost: approximately $70-80 USD
  • Valid for: single entry, 30-day stay
  • Documents required: passport, photo, accommodation confirmation, travel itinerary

Internal Transportation Network

Domestic Flights – The Fastest Option

Given Kazakhstan's vast size (nearly 2.7 million square kilometers), domestic flights are often the most practical way to travel between cities. The two main carriers are:

  • FlyArystan – low-cost subsidiary, great for budget travelers

Rail Network – Scenic and Affordable

Kazakhstan Temir Zholy (KTZ) operates an extensive railway network connecting all major cities. Trains are comfortable and offer a chance to see the dramatic Kazakh steppe landscape.

Train classes available:

  • Luxury (SV/Coupe): 2-berth private compartments, bedding included
  • Standard (Platzkart): Open-plan sleeping berths, economical choice
  • Seated: Day trains for shorter routes
Route Duration Coupe Price Platzkart Price
Astana → Almaty 12-14 hours $30-50 $15-25
Astana → Shymkent 18-20 hours $35-60 $18-30
Almaty → Karaganda 14-16 hours $28-45 $14-22

Book tickets in advance at railways.kz – popular routes sell out quickly, especially around public holidays.

Intercity Buses

An affordable option for medium distances. Buses connect most cities and towns with tickets typically costing $5-20 depending on distance. Quality varies – shared minibuses (marshrutkas) are cheapest but often lack comfort on long journeys.

Car Rental

Available at major airports and city centers. Prices range from $30-80/day depending on vehicle type.

  • International driving license is accepted
  • Roads in major cities are generally good quality
  • Rural and steppe roads can be rough – 4WD recommended for nature exploration
  • Speed limits: 60 km/h city, 90-100 km/h highways
  • Zero-tolerance for drunk driving (0% blood alcohol limit)

SIM Cards and Internet Connectivity

Local SIM Cards

Purchase a local SIM at the airport or any telecom store with your passport. Major operators:

  • Kcell: Best overall coverage nationwide
  • Beeline Kazakhstan: Competitive pricing, good 4G in cities
  • Tele2 Kazakhstan: Budget-friendly data packages
  • Activ (KCell): Popular prepaid choice

A 30-day data package (5-10 GB) costs approximately $5-10 USD. Coverage in rural and steppe areas can be patchy.

Internet in Accommodations

Free Wi-Fi is standard in all mid-range and luxury hotels. Speeds are generally good in major cities (50-200 Mbps). Budget guesthouses may have slower or less reliable connections.

Safety and Health

General Safety

Kazakhstan is considered one of the safer countries in Central Asia for tourists. Violent crime targeting foreigners is rare. However:

  • Be vigilant against pickpockets in crowded markets and public transport
  • Avoid walking alone late at night in poorly lit areas
  • Keep copies of important documents (passport, visa) separate from originals
  • Register with your country's embassy if staying long-term

Health Considerations

  • Water: Drink bottled water in rural areas; city tap water in Almaty and Astana is generally treated
  • Medical facilities: Good in major cities; limited in rural areas
  • Travel insurance: Strongly recommended, especially for mountain activities
  • Vaccinations: No mandatory vaccines; Hepatitis A/B and Typhoid recommended
  • Temperature extremes: Summer can exceed 40°C on the steppe; winters drop to -40°C – pack accordingly

Cultural Etiquette for Visitors

  • Hospitality is sacred: Kazakhs take enormous pride in hosting guests – accepting food and tea offered is a sign of respect
  • Shoes off: Always remove shoes when entering a yurt or traditional home
  • Religious sensitivity: Kazakhstan is predominantly Muslim; dress modestly when visiting mosques
  • Greetings: Handshakes are common; Kazakh men greet with a handshake and slight bow to elders
  • Photography: Ask permission before photographing people, especially in rural areas
  • Gifts: Bringing a small gift when visiting someone's home is appreciated
