Poland is one of Europe's most rewarding and affordable destinations, blending medieval old towns, dramatic mountains and moving historical sites, with mild summers and guaranteed winter snow. Here are the top tourist attractions in Poland to put on your 2026 itinerary, with the time each needs, what suits children, and how to combine them in a week.

1. Kraków Old Town and Wawel Castle

Kraków's UNESCO-listed Old Town is the heart of Poland's history, centred on Europe's largest medieval market square (Rynek Główny) with the Cloth Hall, St. Mary's Basilica and its hourly trumpet call, and horse carriages. Above the river, Wawel Castle holds the royal chambers, the cathedral and the dragon's den, where a bronze dragon breathes fire every few minutes to the delight of children. Allow a full day; book the castle's state rooms online. See tourism in Kraków.

2. Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial

The former Nazi concentration camp, 70 km from Kraków, is the most visited and most sobering site in Poland. Entry is free with a timed reservation; guided tours in English take 3.5 hours. The memorial recommends it for visitors aged 14 and over, so families with younger children usually plan an alternative activity for that half-day.

3. Wieliczka Salt Mine

The oldest working salt mine in the world, 20 minutes from Kraków: 800 steps (or a lift) down to carved chambers, underground lakes and the Chapel of St. Kinga sculpted entirely from salt, chandeliers included. The tour lasts 2.5 hours at a constant 14°C, and a shorter children's route with puzzles is available. Book ahead in summer.

4. Warsaw Old Town and the Royal Castle

Destroyed in 1944 and rebuilt brick by brick, Warsaw's Old Town is a UNESCO site precisely for its reconstruction. The Royal Castle, the market square with the mermaid statue and the Barbican walls take two to three hours; add the Copernicus Science Centre for children and Łazienki Park with its palace on the water and free Chopin concerts on summer Sundays. See the Warsaw guide and halal restaurants in Warsaw.

5. Tatra Mountains and Zakopane

Poland's mountain capital: the Gubałówka funicular with a summer toboggan at the top, the lake of Morskie Oko reached by horse carriage or a two-hour walk, Krupówki street with smoked cheese stalls, and in winter ski schools for children from the age of four and sledging runs. The thermal baths at Bukowina are the best bad-weather plan. See tourism in Zakopane.

6. Malbork Castle

The largest brick castle in the world, the headquarters of the Teutonic Knights, an hour from Gdańsk. Triple walls, drawbridges, armour and a summer sound-and-light show make it a hit with children from the age of seven. Allow three hours with the audio guide.

7. Białowieża Forest

Europe's last primeval lowland forest, on the Belarusian border, home to the European bison. The bison reserve and the horse-cart rides into the forest suit all ages; it is a long day trip from Warsaw (3.5 hours each way), so most families stay one night.

8. Gdańsk and the Baltic coast

The colourful Hanseatic old town, the medieval port crane, St. Mary's Church (the world's largest brick church) and the Museum of the Second World War; beside it the sandy beach and Europe's longest wooden pier at Sopot. Best in July and August. See the tourist city of Gdańsk.

9. Wrocław

The city of 600 bronze dwarfs hidden in the streets (a treasure hunt that keeps children walking for hours), the colourful market square, Cathedral Island with its gas lamplighter, Poland's largest zoo with the Afrykarium, and the multimedia fountain at the Centennial Hall. Two to three hours by train from Warsaw or Kraków.

10. Toruń

Copernicus's birthplace, a Gothic old town that survived the war intact, with a leaning tower and the Gingerbread Museum where children bake their own cookies. A half-day stop on the Warsaw–Gdańsk route.

How to combine them

  • 7 days (classic): Warsaw (3) → Kraków (3) with Wieliczka and a Zakopane day trip; see Poland tour programs.
  • 10 days (summer): add Gdańsk, Malbork and Toruń in the north.
  • 10 days (history): add Wrocław between Warsaw and Kraków.

Best time to visit Poland

June to August for families (22–26°C, long days, everything open), May and September for lower prices, December for Christmas markets, January and February for snow in Zakopane. Avoid November and March. See the best time for family travel.

Practical notes

  • GCC citizens need a Schengen visa; apply six to eight weeks before summer travel. See the Poland visa guide.
  • Trains link Warsaw, Kraków, Gdańsk and Wrocław in 2.5–3.5 hours; a car with driver suits families with small children.
  • Most shopping malls close on Sundays; museums often close on Mondays.
  • A family of four spends roughly 2,600–3,900 USD for a week on a private program in summer, excluding flights; see the Poland family trip cost guide.
