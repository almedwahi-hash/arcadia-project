China is a country of staggering contrasts: ancient wonders, futuristic skylines and landscapes from misty karst mountains to neon megacities, with halal food in every city and the world's best high-speed rail to connect them. Here are the best things to do in China for your 2026 trip, with the practical detail families and traders need.

1. Walk the Great Wall of China

The most iconic sight in China, winding over mountains for thousands of kilometres. The Mutianyu section, two hours from Beijing, is the family favourite: a cable car up, a toboggan slide down, fewer crowds than Badaling and restored watchtowers to climb. Leave Beijing by 7 am, allow a full day, and bring layers; it is cool on the ridge even in summer.

2. The Forbidden City and Tiananmen Square, Beijing

Enter from the south through Tiananmen Square and walk north through 900 buildings of the Ming and Qing emperors' palace; the golden roofs, bronze lions and vast courtyards impress children more than the exhibits. Book tickets with passport numbers days ahead (daily numbers are capped), then climb Jingshan Hill behind the north gate for the view over the whole palace. See our top tourist cities in China and top tourist attractions in China.

3. The Bund and the skyline, Shanghai

Colonial waterfront on one bank, the Pudong skyscrapers on the other: go up Shanghai Tower (the world's second-tallest building) by day, then take a night river cruise for the lights. Add the Yu Garden and bazaar, Nanjing Road for shopping, the water town of Zhujiajiao and Shanghai Disneyland for a full family day. See the Shanghai guide for Arab tourists and traders.

4. The Terracotta Army, Xi'an

Thousands of life-size clay soldiers guarding the first emperor's tomb, an hour from Xi'an. Children remember it for years. In the city, cycle the 14-km Ming city wall (tandem bikes and family carts available) and eat your way through the Muslim Quarter, the best halal street food in China, beside the Great Mosque.

5. Li River and Guilin

The karst peaks of the Li River between Guilin and Yangshuo are the landscape on the 20-yuan note: a four-hour cruise or a bamboo raft, bicycle rides through rice paddies, and the Reed Flute Cave. Best from April to October; a two-night addition for families who want nature.

6. Chengdu's giant pandas

The Chengdu Research Base is the place to see dozens of pandas, including cubs, early in the morning when they are fed. Chengdu also offers Sichuan opera with face-changing, spicy halal-friendly hotpot options and a relaxed pace; two nights, reached by high-speed train from Xi'an.

7. Guangzhou, Yiwu and the trade cities

For traders, the Canton Fair in Guangzhou (April and October) and the Yiwu International Trade Market with 75,000 shops are the main draws, and both cities have large Arab communities with halal restaurants on every corner. Many families combine four tourist days with three business days. See the Canton Fair guide, the Yiwu guide for Arab traders and tourism in Guangzhou.

8. Theme parks for families

Shanghai Disneyland (the largest Disney castle in the world), Universal Beijing Resort (Harry Potter, Transformers, Kung Fu Panda) and Chimelong in Guangzhou with its safari park and ocean kingdom. Each needs a full day and advance tickets through the official apps; arrive before opening.

Suggested itineraries

  • 7 days: Beijing (4) → Shanghai (3) by high-speed train (4.5 hours).
  • 10 days: Beijing (4) → Xi'an (2) → Shanghai (4), the classic family route.
  • Trade trip, 6–8 days: Guangzhou → Yiwu → Shanghai or Shenzhen for factory visits; see how to visit factories in China.

Best time to visit China

April to May and September to October: mild in all the main cities with clear skies for the Great Wall. Avoid Chinese New Year (late January to mid-February), the Golden Week of 1–7 October and 1–5 May; summer is hot, humid and crowded. See the best time for family travel.

Practical tips for visiting China

  • Check the latest visa-free and transit rules for your nationality or apply four weeks ahead; see the Chinese visa guide 2026.
  • Set up Alipay and WeChat Pay with an international card before arrival; cash and foreign cards are rarely accepted. See essential China travel apps.
