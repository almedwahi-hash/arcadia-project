This Moscow travel guide is for travellers who have chosen the city and now need to turn flights, hotel location, transport and sightseeing into a realistic plan. Moscow is large: the best itinerary is not the one with the most stops, but the one that groups nearby places and leaves time for security checks, meals and changing weather.

Planning summary: three full days cover the headline sights; four days give a more comfortable first trip. Stay near a useful metro station, reserve major timed attractions through official channels and keep the arrival day light.

How many days do you need in Moscow?

Three full days are enough for Red Square, one Kremlin route, a major gallery, the metro and either VDNH or Moscow City. Four or five days are better if you travel with children, prefer slower mornings, want several museums or plan a performance.

Count usable sightseeing days, not hotel nights. A late arrival and an early departure should not be treated as two full touring days.

Where to stay in Moscow

Choose an area according to your itinerary rather than selecting a hotel from an old “best hotels” list. Properties, service levels and prices change; compare recent reviews, room details and cancellation terms before paying.

Tverskoy and the historic centre

A strong choice for a first visit and short stays. Many central sights are easier to reach, but rooms may cost more and busy streets can feel less quiet.

Arbat and Khamovniki

Useful for travellers who want central walks, cafés and access toward the river. Check the exact distance to the nearest station because a neighbourhood name can cover a broad area.

Zamoskvorechye

A practical base south of the river for the Tretyakov area and a calmer residential feel while remaining close to the centre.

Moscow City

Fits business travellers and visitors who prefer modern towers and skyline views. It is less convenient if most of your itinerary is around Red Square and the older centre.

Getting around Moscow

The metro is usually the most predictable way to cross the city. Station names may be shown in Cyrillic and Latin characters, but a saved offline map and the destination's Russian name are still useful. Current ticket products and payment methods can change, so check official Moscow Transport information near your travel date.

  • Travel outside weekday rush hours when possible, especially with luggage or children.
  • Keep bags and coats clear of closing doors and stand away from platform edges.
  • Use an official taxi or transfer channel and confirm the pickup point before leaving the terminal.
  • Allow extra road time for airports, railway stations and evening performances.

Arriving at the airport or railway station

Moscow is served by multiple airports and major railway terminals. Write the exact airport code or station name into your itinerary; “Moscow airport” is not precise enough for a transfer. Before arrival, confirm the terminal, baggage allowance, meeting point and the contact method that will work when you land.

Families, larger groups and travellers arriving late may prefer a pre-arranged vehicle. Independent travellers should compare the current official rail, public-transport and authorised taxi options for their specific terminal.

Best time to visit Moscow

  • Late spring: a good balance for walking, parks and longer days, with changeable weather.
  • Summer: long daylight and active outdoor spaces; popular dates can be busier.
  • Early autumn: often comfortable for city sightseeing, with cooler evenings.
  • Winter: seasonal scenery and indoor culture, but cold conditions, shorter daylight and a need for warm breaks.

Avoid promises about exact snow, festival dates or temperatures months ahead. Check a reliable forecast and the official event page shortly before each activity.

A balanced 4-day Moscow itinerary

  1. Day 1 — Arrival and orientation: hotel check-in, a short neighbourhood walk and an early night.
  2. Day 2 — Historic Moscow: Red Square, the booked Kremlin route and Zaryadye Park.
  3. Day 3 — Art and riverside: Tretyakov Gallery, Muzeon or Gorky Park, then selected metro stations.
  4. Day 4 — Choose your interest: VDNH and space history, Moscow City, a family attraction or a performance.

Food and dining considerations

Moscow has Russian, Central Asian, Caucasian, Middle Eastern and international dining options. If halal food is essential, ask the restaurant directly about current certification, ingredients, shared kitchens and alcohol service; do not rely only on an old directory entry or a review label.

Tell your tour planner about allergies and dietary rules in writing. Save the requirements in Russian as well as English when a misunderstanding could create a health risk.

Planning Moscow with children

  • Limit long museum blocks and schedule a seated break every few hours.
  • Choose a hotel that reduces transfers, not simply the cheapest room.
  • Carry weather layers, water and a compact snack that meets the child's needs.
  • Keep one indoor alternative ready for rain, heat or severe cold.
  • Share children's ages when requesting transport, rooms and activities.

How to build a realistic trip budget

Old fixed dollar estimates are unreliable. Build the budget from live components: flights, room type, number of travellers, private or shared transport, guide language, attraction tickets, meals and intercity travel. Dates around major events can change availability and price sharply.

When comparing quotations, check what is included rather than comparing only the total. Airport transfers, breakfast, entry tickets, child arrangements and cancellation rules can make two similar-looking offers very different.

Booking checklist

  1. Confirm names, nationality, adults, children and children's ages.
  2. Record exact arrival and departure airports, terminals and times.
  3. Select the cities, nights and preferred hotel level.
  4. Prioritise must-see attractions before booking timed tickets.
  5. Check official opening days and current payment instructions.
  6. Keep confirmations, addresses and emergency contacts available offline.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moscow walkable?

Individual districts are walkable, but the city is too large to cover only on foot. Combine local walking with the metro or planned transfers.

Should I stay near Red Square?

It is convenient for a short first visit, but not essential. A well-reviewed hotel near a useful metro station can offer better value while keeping travel times reasonable.

Can I combine Moscow and St Petersburg?

Yes. Many first trips allocate three or four nights to each city plus the transfer. Add more time when travelling with young children or planning several museums.

Do I need a guide in Moscow?

Independent travel is possible, while a guide can add context and simplify a short visit, a complex museum day or a family itinerary. Choose according to language needs and the amount of time available.
