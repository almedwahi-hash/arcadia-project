Russia travel essentials

Plan the route before you book the details

Russia rewards careful planning. Cities are large, distances can be substantial and the experience changes noticeably with the season. These ten essentials will help you build a trip that is comfortable, realistic and suited to your group.

1. Decide which cities deserve your time

Moscow and Saint Petersburg are the most common first choices, but they offer different experiences. Moscow is energetic and expansive, with major landmarks, museums and modern districts. Saint Petersburg is known for imperial architecture, art collections, palaces and canals. Compare them in our guide to cities in Russia.

2. Choose a season that suits the group

Late spring and summer provide longer daylight and more time outdoors. Autumn can offer colour and a quieter atmosphere. Winter brings snow and a distinctive city experience, but shorter days and cold conditions require appropriate clothing and a less crowded programme. There is no single best month for every traveller.

3. Treat travel time as part of the itinerary

Map distances before assigning attractions to a day. A route that looks short on a list can become tiring when it includes long walks, transfers and entry procedures. Keep one or two major priorities per day and place nearby experiences around them.

4. Plan important museums and landmarks early

If a museum, palace or performance is central to the trip, check its official opening information and reservation process before finalising the daily order. Leave room for security checks and avoid placing several time-sensitive visits too close together.

5. Use the right transport for each day

Public transport can be efficient for many city routes, while private transport may be more practical for families, luggage, airport movements or places that are difficult to combine. The best plan often mixes walking and transport rather than relying entirely on one method.

6. Prepare for language and connectivity

Keep accommodation names and addresses available in a clear written form. Save essential confirmations and contact details for offline access. A local guide can add context at important sites and simplify coordination for groups that prefer direct support.

7. Pack for comfort, not only photographs

Comfortable walking shoes, adaptable layers and protection from rain or cold make a large difference. Check the forecast shortly before departure and account for indoor heating in colder months. Families should keep a small set of essentials available during transfers.

8. Make family days deliberately lighter

Children and older travellers benefit from shorter blocks, regular meal breaks and indoor alternatives. A central hotel can reduce repeated travel across the city. Tell your planner about ages, mobility requirements and dietary needs before the itinerary is assembled.

9. Keep arrival and departure days simple

Flight timing, baggage collection and airport transfers make arrival days unpredictable. Avoid placing a major timed attraction immediately after landing. The final day should also allow a sensible margin before the airport journey.

10. Review the complete trip as one journey

Check that hotels, transfers, intercity travel and scheduled visits connect logically. A strong plan should make clear where the group sleeps, how each major move happens and which reservations are fixed.
