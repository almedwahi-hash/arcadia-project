Two days in Astana are enough for Kazakhstan's futuristic capital: Baiterek Tower, the glass Khan Shatyr tent with its indoor beach, the Hazrat Sultan Mosque, the Nur Alem sphere and the National Museum, all within a small area on the left bank. It is the natural add-on to an Almaty trip (a 90-minute domestic flight) or to a conference in the capital. A day-by-day programme for families.

In short: day one for the modern left bank (Baiterek, the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation, Khan Shatyr), day two for the National Museum, the Hazrat Sultan Mosque and the Expo area. Two nights in a left-bank hotel near Baiterek.

Day 1: The modern left bank

  • Morning: Baiterek Tower (up to the golden sphere), then the Nurzhol Boulevard and the Presidential Palace from outside.
  • Lunch: a halal restaurant in the Khan Shatyr mall or on the boulevard.
  • Afternoon: Khan Shatyr, a 150-metre glass tent with an indoor beach and a children's funfair; then the pyramid-shaped Palace of Peace and Reconciliation.
  • Evening: a walk along the Ishim River embankment and dinner at a Kazakh restaurant.

Day 2: Culture and Expo

  • Morning: the National Museum (the Gold Hall and the Golden Man, and the history of the steppe); see the National Museum of Kazakhstan.
  • Midday: the Hazrat Sultan Mosque, the largest in Central Asia.
  • Afternoon: the Expo 2017 area and the Nur Alem sphere (the museum of future energy with a view from the top floor).
  • Evening: a farewell dinner, or the transfer to the airport for the evening flight to Almaty.

Where to stay and when to go

The left bank near Baiterek: five-star hotels such as Rixos President, Hilton and The St. Regis, four-star such as Wyndham Garden and Park Inn; see the best hotels in Astana. Summer (June to August) is the right season; winter drops below -20°C, so keep winter trips in Almaty.

Frequently asked questions

Is Astana worth visiting?

Yes for a day or two if you are already in Kazakhstan; it is a modern capital with unique architecture, but nature and mountains are in Almaty.

Is the city walkable?

The left bank is designed for cars and distances are large; a private car or taxi saves a lot of time, especially in the cold or the heat.

Is halal food available?

Most Kazakh restaurants are halal, and Turkish and Arabic restaurants are common in the malls.
