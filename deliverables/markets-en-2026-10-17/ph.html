Filipino travellers rarely get a visa-free destination with snow, mountains and a modern city in one trip. Kazakhstan is one: holders of a Philippine passport enter visa-free for 30 days, no appointment, no fee and no show money beyond a return ticket and hotel booking. Almaty sits at the foot of the Tian Shan range, with a ski resort 30 minutes from the centre, alpine lakes and canyons for summer, and English-speaking guides from Arcadia, a destination management company based in the city. Flights from Manila connect once, via Seoul, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Dubai or Doha.

In short: no visa for Kazakhstan. Uzbekistan, China, Poland and Russia require a visa for Filipinos, so we build Philippine programmes entirely in Kazakhstan: Almaty, Astana and the national parks. Best months: December to March for snow, May to October for lakes and mountains.

Flights from the Philippines

  • Manila to Almaty: no nonstop service; one stop via Seoul (nonstop Korea to Almaty flights), Bangkok (Air Astana nonstop to Almaty), Hong Kong, Dubai or Doha; around 13 to 16 hours in total.
  • Manila to Astana: via Seoul, Istanbul or Almaty.
  • Within Kazakhstan: Almaty to Astana is a two-hour flight; the mountain parks are two to four hours by road from Almaty.

Suggested itineraries

  • Snow week, 5 nights: first skiing and sledging on Shymbulak with instructors and rental gear, the Medeu ice rink and hot springs; see Shymbulak and Medeu.
  • Almaty mountains and lakes, 4 to 5 nights (summer): Big Almaty Lake, Charyn Canyon, Kolsai and Kaindy lakes, the Green Bazaar and Kok-Tobe; based on Almaty in 3 days.
  • Almaty and Astana, 6 to 7 nights: add the capital and the pine forests of Burabay; see Astana in 2 days.
  • Company outing or incentive, 4 nights: meetings in the hotel, team building in the mountains and a gala dinner; see team-building activities in Kazakhstan.

Practical notes for Filipino travellers

  • Immigration on departure from Manila: carry your return ticket, hotel confirmation and our programme letter; we send all three with the booking.
  • Time zone: Almaty is three hours behind Manila.
  • Money: cards work in cities; carry US dollars to exchange.
  • Food: grilled meat, rice dishes, noodles, bread and dumplings; Filipino groups usually enjoy plov and shashlik. Catholic churches exist in Almaty and Astana.
  • Language: Russian and Kazakh; English with our guides and in hotels.
  • Winter clothing: January temperatures reach -10°C; we advise on gear and arrange rentals.

Frequently asked questions

Do Filipinos need a visa for Kazakhstan?

No. Philippine passport holders enter visa-free for up to 30 days per visit, within 90 days in any 180-day period.

Is Kazakhstan safe for Filipino travellers?

Yes. Almaty and Astana are calm, modern cities with low street crime. All our programmes use private vehicles and licensed guides.

Can we see snow in Almaty?

Yes, reliably from December to March on Shymbulak and often in the city itself.

Can we add another country?

Uzbekistan, China and Russia need a visa for Filipinos, which we do not process. Kazakhstan alone fills a week comfortably.
