An Indonesian passport opens the two Muslim-majority countries at the heart of the Silk Road without a visa: 30 days in Kazakhstan and 30 days in Uzbekistan. For Indonesian families and pilgrimage-minded travellers, Uzbekistan is a special destination: the mausoleum of Imam al-Bukhari near Samarkand, the Uthman Quran in Tashkent and the madrasas of Bukhara, all with halal food as standard. Kazakhstan adds snow-capped mountains and alpine lakes 30 minutes from Almaty. With AirAsia X flying nonstop from Kuala Lumpur to Almaty and Tashkent, the journey from Jakarta is a single connection. Arcadia, a destination management company based in Almaty, plans and runs the trip with halal meals, prayer stops and English-speaking guides.

In short: no visa for Kazakhstan or Uzbekistan. China allows Indonesians a 240-hour visa-free transit only; Poland and Russia require a visa, so we build Indonesian programmes around Central Asia. Best months: April to June and September to October; December to March for snow.

Flights from Indonesia

  • Jakarta to Almaty or Tashkent: one stop via Kuala Lumpur, where AirAsia X flies nonstop to both cities (Tashkent three times a week), or via Istanbul, Dubai or Doha; about eleven to thirteen hours in total.
  • Almaty to Tashkent: under two hours by air or overnight by sleeper train.
  • Tashkent to Samarkand and Bukhara: high-speed Afrosiyob train, two and four hours.

Halal food, prayer and Islamic heritage

Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are Muslim-majority countries: restaurants serve halal food by default, mosques are in every town and Friday prayer is part of the programme. Our Uzbekistan itineraries include the Islamic heritage sites: Imam al-Bukhari's mausoleum, the Hazrati Imam complex with the Uthman Quran, the Bahauddin Naqshband shrine near Bukhara and the Registan madrasas. In Almaty, the Central Mosque and halal restaurants serving beshbarmak, plov and shashlik are a short walk from our partner hotels.

Suggested itineraries

  • Uzbekistan heritage, 7 nights: Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva with the Islamic heritage sites, bazaars and craft workshops; start with Tashkent in two days and Bukhara.
  • Almaty mountains and lakes, 4 to 5 nights: Medeu and Shymbulak, Big Almaty Lake, Charyn Canyon and the Kolsai Lakes; based on Almaty in 3 days.
  • Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, 10 nights: our combined family itinerary flying into Almaty and out of Tashkent.
  • Winter snow break, 4 nights: sledging and skiing on Shymbulak with instructors, the Medeu ice rink and hot springs, December to March; see Shymbulak and Medeu.

Practical notes for Indonesian travellers

  • Time zone: Almaty and Tashkent are one to two hours behind Jakarta depending on the season.
  • Money: cards work in cities; carry US dollars to exchange. Prices are lower than in Japan or Europe; see what an Uzbekistan trip costs.
  • Language: Russian, Kazakh and Uzbek; English with our guides and in hotels. Many Uzbek words are familiar from Arabic and Persian.
  • Weather: dry continental climate; summers hot, winters cold. Pack layers, not tropical clothing.
  • Group size: no minimum. Families, mosque and community groups and corporate teams are all welcome.

Frequently asked questions

Do Indonesians need a visa for Kazakhstan?

No. Indonesian passport holders enter visa-free for up to 30 days per visit, within 90 days in any 180-day period.

Is Uzbekistan visa-free for Indonesians?

Yes, for stays of up to 30 days.

Can Indonesians visit China without a visa?

Only in transit for up to 240 hours when travelling on to a third country. A full China holiday requires a visa, which we do not process.

Is all food halal?

In Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan mainstream restaurants serve halal food by default, and we confirm halal certification for every restaurant on the programme.

Can you organise a group from a mosque or community?

Yes. We organise groups of any size with prayer stops, Islamic heritage visits and a dedicated coordinator.
