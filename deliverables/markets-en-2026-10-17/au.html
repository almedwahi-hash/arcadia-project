Travellers from Australia and New Zealand can explore Central Asia, China and Poland without a visa: 30 days in Kazakhstan, 30 days in Uzbekistan, 30 days in China under the unilateral exemption extended to the end of 2026, and 90 days in Poland and the Schengen area. Only Russia still requires a consular visa. For a long-haul traveller the region rewards time: two to three weeks that combine the Tian Shan mountains above Almaty, the Silk Road cities of Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva, and a few days in Shanghai or Beijing on the way home. Arcadia, a destination management company based in Almaty, plans and runs the whole journey with private transport and licensed English-speaking guides.

In short: no visa for Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, China or Poland. Fly via Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Dubai, Doha or Seoul; around 16 to 20 hours door to door. Best months: April to June and September to October. Winter brings skiing in Almaty.

Getting there

  • To Almaty: one stop via Kuala Lumpur (AirAsia X nonstop to Almaty), Singapore, Dubai, Doha, Seoul or Istanbul; typically 16 to 20 hours including the connection.
  • To Tashkent: one stop via Kuala Lumpur (AirAsia X three times a week), Dubai, Doha, Istanbul or Seoul.
  • To China: nonstop from Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Auckland to Shanghai, Guangzhou and Beijing; Guangzhou is a convenient gateway on to Almaty.
  • Open-jaw works best: fly into Almaty and out of Tashkent (or vice versa) to avoid backtracking; the two cities are under two hours apart by air.

Suggested itineraries

  • Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan Silk Road, 14 nights: Almaty and its mountains, Charyn Canyon and Kolsai Lakes, then Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva by train; built on our 10-day combined itinerary with extra nights in the mountains.
  • Mountains and steppe, 8 nights: Almaty, Shymbulak and Medeu, Big Almaty Lake, Charyn, Kolsai and Kaindy lakes, Altyn-Emel singing dunes; see best things to do in Kazakhstan.
  • Central Asia plus China, 18 to 21 nights: the Silk Road loop above, then Urumqi or a flight to Shanghai and Beijing before flying home.
  • Uzbekistan in depth, 9 nights: Tashkent, the Fergana Valley crafts towns, Samarkand, Shahrisabz, Bukhara and Khiva.
  • Poland and the Tatras, 7 nights: Warsaw, Kraków, Auschwitz memorial, Wieliczka and Zakopane.

Practical notes

  • Seasons: Central Asian summers are hot (35°C in Samarkand) and winters cold; April to June and September to October are ideal. Autumn in Almaty brings golden poplars and apple harvests.
  • Health: no special vaccinations required; travel insurance with medical cover is strongly recommended and required for Schengen.
  • Money: cards work in cities; carry US dollars for exchange. Tipping is appreciated but not expected.
  • Language: Russian, Kazakh and Uzbek; English with our guides and in hotels.
  • Food: grilled meats, plov, dumplings, fresh bread and fruit; vegetarian and gluten-free requests are handled with notice.

Frequently asked questions

Do Australians need a visa for Kazakhstan?

No. Australian citizens enter visa-free for up to 30 days per visit, within 90 days in any 180-day period. The same applies to New Zealand citizens.

Is Uzbekistan visa-free for Australians and New Zealanders?

Yes, both for stays of up to 30 days.

Can Australians and New Zealanders visit China without a visa in 2026?

Yes. China's unilateral visa-free policy allows stays of up to 30 days and is currently valid until 31 December 2026.

What about Russia?

Russia requires a consular visa for Australian and New Zealand passports. Arcadia does not process visas but can supply supporting documents for your own application.

How many days do I need for the Silk Road?

A comfortable loop of Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva takes seven to nine nights; add five to seven for Kazakhstan's mountains.
