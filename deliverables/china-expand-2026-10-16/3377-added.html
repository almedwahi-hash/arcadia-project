Checklist two weeks before departure

  • Visa, or confirmation of visa-free entry for your nationality (Chinese visa guide 2026).
  • Install Alipay and WeChat Pay, link an international card and make a small test payment before travelling.
  • An international eSIM or a paid, reliable VPN (VPN in China 2026).
  • Download Baidu Maps or Amap and a camera translation app that works offline.
  • A copy of your passport and the hotel address in Chinese, on your phone and on paper.
  • A power adapter (China uses 220 V with type A, C and I sockets) and a power bank.
  • Personal medication with a translated prescription; some medicines common in the Gulf need a prescription in China.

Etiquette and habits that help

  • Calm manners and a smile go a long way; raising your voice in a dispute closes doors.
  • Tipping is not customary in restaurants and taxis; it is welcome for private guides and drivers.
  • Queues and passport checks at train stations and most sights are normal; arrive 45 minutes before a train.
  • Photography is welcome almost everywhere but banned in some museums and official sites; watch for signs.
  • Every large city has mosques (Niujie in Beijing, Huaisheng in Guangzhou, the Great Mosque of Xi'an), and prayer times fit easily into the programme.

Frequently asked questions

Will my Gulf SIM work in China?

Roaming works but is expensive; an international eSIM before departure or a local SIM at the airport is cheaper, and the international eSIM has the advantage that blocked sites work without a VPN.

Is China safe for families and women?

One of the safest countries; streets are monitored and crime is rare. The only cautions are crowds in peak seasons and keeping belongings close in large markets.

What is the biggest first-timer mistake?

Arriving without an activated payment app and without a VPN, then discovering that neither the card nor WhatsApp works. Set up both before take-off.
